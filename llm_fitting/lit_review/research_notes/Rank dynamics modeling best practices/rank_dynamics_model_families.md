## 1. Rank-based / first-order / Atlas-type models (stochastic portfolio theory): stationary gaps, capital-distribution curve, estimation by rank, measurement error, hybrid models

### Takeaway
SPT gives closed-form stationary gap laws for rank-based (Eulerian) dynamics and a mature set of rank-indexed observables (gap variance, collision local time / "leakage", capital-distribution curve), but its estimation practice is ad hoc moment matching with cross-rank smoothing, has no measurement-error treatment, and only heuristically recognises the rank-conditioning bias the program calls Eulerian selection bias. In SPT vocabulary the program's model is a purely name-based model (parameters attach to the entity via permanent rank and an own home level); the "second-order/hybrid" literature concedes that name dependence is needed but reports that it could not estimate it well.

### Cited Findings
- First-order model: d log X_i = g_{r_t(i)} dt + G_n 1{r_t(i)=n} dt + sigma_{r_t(i)} dW_i, stable iff partial sums g_1+...+g_k < 0; Atlas model is the special case with one drift -g and a compensating push ng on the bottom rank. — [Fernholz & Fernholz, "Zipf's law for Atlas models", J. Appl. Probab. 57 (2020) 1276-1297, arXiv 1707.04285](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04285)
- Stationary gaps xi_k = log X_(k) - log X_(k+1) in Atlas / quasi-Atlas models are independent exponentials with rate a_k = 2 lambda_{k,k+1} / sigma^2_{k,k+1}, lambda_{k,k+1} = -2(g_1+...+g_k), sigma^2_{k,k+1} = sigma_k^2 + sigma_{k+1}^2; so E[xi_k] = (sigma_k^2 + sigma_{k+1}^2) / (-4(g_1+...+g_k)). Atlas log-log slope parameter s = sigma^2/(2g); Zipf iff sigma^2 = 2g. — [Fernholz & Fernholz 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04285)
- Finite systems of Brownian particles with rank-dependent drifts: the gap vector converges to a unique stationary law that is a product of exponentials with different means; infinite Atlas model with drift delta has i.i.d. Exp(2 delta) gaps as an equilibrium (abstract-level). — [Pal & Pitman, Ann. Appl. Probab. 18 (2008), arXiv 0704.0957](https://arxiv.org/abs/0704.0957)
- Large-n limit of market weights in rank-based models has three phases: all weights vanish, the top weight goes to one, or the ranked weights converge to a non-trivial Poisson-Dirichlet law (abstract-level). — [Chatterjee & Pal, arXiv 0706.3558](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.3558)
- Empirical first-order profile for US equities (CRSP 1990-1999, top 5000 monthly): estimated -g_k roughly constant, sigma_k^2 rising almost linearly with rank; curve is "quasi-Zipfian" (concave, slope -1 only around ranks 100-500); truncating to the top 100 breaks the "completeness" condition. Bank assets: gap variances are lower at higher k and the rank-based assumptions partly fail; New York State cities violate the rank-based assumption. — [Fernholz & Fernholz 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04285)
- First-order models can be built to have any stationary distribution; "quasi-Atlas" = constant drift with variance linear in rank, sigma_k^2 = sigma_1^2 + (k-1)(sigma_2^2 - sigma_1^2) (two variance parameters + one drift). — [Fernholz & Fernholz 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04285)
- State-of-the-art calibration (Itkin & Larsson, rank volatility-stabilized model, dS_i = a_{r_i} S-bar dt + sigma_{r_i} sqrt(S_i S-bar) dW_i): CRSP top d = 3500, in-sample 1990-2005, out-of-sample 2006-2022; rank volatility from summed squared log-increments, smoothed with a 15-rank uniform window; "collision" (reflection/leakage) parameters from a discretised top-k portfolio leakage estimator that avoids an occupation-density bandwidth; growth a_k backed out from an identity linking a_k, average weight, volatility and collision term; one free scalar (market return, set to 0.11) trades off capital-curve fit against collision fit. Validation = L2 error of the capital-distribution curve and collision parameters over the top 1000 ranks (50 simulated paths), in and out of sample, plus visual trajectory comparison of X_(k) for k = 1, 10, 50, 100, 500, 1000. Out-of-sample collision errors are larger; authors attribute this partly to overfitting/noise and leave regularisation to future work. No parametric form across rank; no proper scores. — [Itkin & Larsson, "Calibrated rank volatility stabilized models for large equity markets", arXiv 2403.04674 (preprint, v1 March 2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04674)
- Recognised estimator bias (the nearest thing to the program's Eulerian bias): with A = log-return over [t_i, t_{i+1}] of the name holding rank k at t_i, B = change in the k-th order statistic, and C = the end-of-interval gap between that name and the new rank-k holder, A = B + C and A^2 is approximately B^2 + C^2; the rank-based (order-statistic) estimator drops C^2 and is biased downward at fixed sampling interval; both are consistent only as the interval goes to zero; the argument is heuristic and a rigorous treatment is posed as an open question. No magnitude is given. — [Itkin & Larsson 2024, Appendix B.1](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.04674)
- A 2024 stylised-facts paper states that calibration of rank-based and volatility-stabilised models has typically been ad hoc moment matching with smoothing, without assessment of estimator accuracy or diagnostic checks (search-summary level; authors not verified here). — [“Macroscopic properties of equity markets: stylized facts and portfolio performance”, arXiv 2409.10859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.10859)
- Second-order model: d log X_i = (gamma_i + g_{r_t(i)}) dt + sigma_{r_t(i)} dW_i. Motivation: first-order models are ergodic so every stock spends equal long-run time at every rank, which real markets do not do. Estimation: long-run occupation rates theta_{k,i} are described as essentially impossible to estimate accurately; g_k comes from asymptotic local times via g-bar_k = (lambda_{k-1,k} - lambda_{k,k+1})/2 with g-bar_k = g_k + sum_i theta_{k,i} gamma_i; gamma_i from averaging forward and backward time-averages. Data: US equities 1990-1999, mostly the top 250. No confidence intervals; authors call the methods rudimentary. — [Fernholz, Ichiba & Karatzas, "A second-order stock market model", Annals of Finance 9 (2013) 439-454, arXiv 1302.3870](https://arxiv.org/abs/1302.3870)
- Hybrid Atlas models: drift and variance depend on both rank and name; within a rank region each coordinate is Brownian with rank-and-name-specific drift and variance; capital-distribution curve derived from the gap laws (abstract-level). — [Ichiba, Papathanakos, Banner, Karatzas & Fernholz, Ann. Appl. Probab. 21 (2011) 609-644, arXiv 0909.0065](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.0065)
- Identification from sampling-interval dependence: parameters of a simple Atlas model can be recovered from the variance of the top-ranked process at different sampling intervals; a simulation study judged it of theoretical more than practical interest (search-summary level). — [Fernholz, "Identification of Atlas models", arXiv 1502.04909](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04909)
- Open markets: a fixed number of top-capitalisation stocks whose membership changes with rank (the SPT analogue of a closed top-K universe); the published work concerns viability, numéraire and functionally generated portfolios, not estimation (abstract-level). — [Karatzas & Kim, "Open markets", Mathematical Finance 31(4) (2021) 1111-1161](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/mathfi/v31y2021i4p1111-1161.html); [Itkin & Larsson, "Open markets and hybrid Jacobi processes", Ann. Appl. Probab. 34(3) (2024) 2940-2985, arXiv 2110.14046](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.14046)
- Jumps: "Jump Atlas" models add jumps to rank-based Brownian models, prove a long-run stability result and use simulation to see which continuous-model properties survive (abstract-level); explicit exponential convergence rates exist for two competing Lévy particles. — [Barnes & Sarantsev, arXiv 1610.04323](https://arxiv.org/abs/1610.04323); [Sarantsev, ALEA 2016, arXiv 1509.01783](https://arxiv.org/abs/1509.01783)
- Entry/exit: geometric Brownian particles entering and exiting at rank-dependent intensities; many-firm limit is a reaction-diffusion equation; on CRSP the calibrated reaction term is bistable and the long-run capital distribution is a travelling wave; the authors report that turnover rather than drift stabilises the calibrated market, estimate volatility rank by rank, fit intensities from event counts, and contrast with a variant using the naive rank-dependent drift measured from surviving firms (abstract/search-summary only; unrefereed, posted 27 Aug 2026). — [Baker & Smyth, "Traveling waves in equity markets with rank-based entry and exit", arXiv 2608.27156](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.27156)
- Noise-induced size effects were recognised long ago in finance: measurement noise in quoted closing prices biases computed returns upward for small stocks; buy-and-hold returns halve the estimated size effect. — [Blume & Stambaugh 1983, J. Financial Economics 12, 387-404](https://ideas.repec.org/p/fth/pennfi/11-83.html)
- In firm dynamics the same issue is the "regression fallacy": firms classified small in a base year disproportionately have had transitory declines and tend to grow back, creating a spurious size-growth relation. — [Davis, Haltiwanger & Schuh, NBER WP 4492 / Small Business Economics 1996](https://www.nber.org/papers/w4492)

### Inferences
- Mapping. The program's model has no current-rank dependence at all: drift is reversion to the entity's own level, and variances are indexed by permanent rank, which is a fixed label of the name. In SPT terms it is the pure name-based corner of the hybrid family, with the ladder exogenous (homes), whereas Atlas/first-order models make the ladder endogenous through rank-based drifts and are ergodic across ranks. The second-order paper's stated motivation (names do not visit all ranks equally) is the same empirical fact that entity-home conditioning encodes; SPT did not find a workable estimator for it, the program's permanent-rank estimation is one.
- Is the current-rank selection bias recognised in SPT? Only partially: (i) Itkin-Larsson's B.1 heuristic (order-statistic increments omit the switch term); (ii) the structural fact that rank-based "reversion rates" alpha_k are by construction negative at the top, which in a noisy panel is partly regression to the mean of whoever is transiently on top; (iii) Baker-Smyth's contrast with drift measured from survivors. None of these papers models observation noise or proposes time-averaged-rank conditioning. The closest explicit precedents are outside SPT (Blume-Stambaugh; the regression-fallacy literature). The program's 3x inflation result therefore appears to be a publishable methodological point for the SPT/rank-econometrics audience rather than a known result. (Inference from absence in the texts read; not a proof of absence.)
- Where SPT transfers: its rank-indexed observables are well defined on any ranked panel and are the natural "Eulerian" validation targets: mean gap by rank, gap quadratic variation, collision/leakage rate, the capital-distribution curve, and X_(k) trajectories. Where it does not: (a) continuous-semimartingale theory with no observation noise, while the program's weekly data are dominated at short horizons by transitory and measurement components; (b) exponential-gap/product-form results require rank-only coefficients; (c) local-time estimators have no established sampling theory at coarse sampling.
- Head law (R2). Chatterjee-Pal's phase structure implies the top weight is a condensation-prone functional in rank-based systems: small parameter changes move it a lot. A 2.7x error in simulated top-1 share is therefore weak evidence about the bulk dynamics; top-k share curves and head-gap distributions are more stable targets.
- "Completeness" and the universe. Fernholz-Fernholz's observation that truncation to a short list breaks completeness is a formal counterpart of the program's top-coverage K rule (K chosen from concentration alone): their conditions (conservation, completeness) could be reported per platform as part of universe justification.

### Gaps
- Banner-Fernholz-Karatzas (2005) empirical estimation details were not re-read; the linear-in-rank variance finding is cited from Fernholz & Fernholz (2020) instead.
- No SPT paper was found that treats measurement error in sizes, zeros/ties, or censored instruments. Search was not exhaustive.
- Shkolnikov's competing Lévy particle systems (Ann. Appl. Probab. 21(5) 2011, cited in Fernholz's reference list) was not read.
- The Davis-Haltiwanger-Schuh remedy of classifying by average rather than base-period size (the direct analogue of permanent-rank conditioning) is recalled from memory but was not verified in this session.

## 2. Fernholz's rank-based econometrics for dynamic power laws; can the gap identity be a stationarity constraint?

### Takeaway
Fernholz's identity says the stationary mean log-gap at rank k equals gap variance over twice the collision local-time rate; it is exact only for a constant-coefficient "stable version" of the gap process and approximate otherwise, and every ingredient is an Eulerian (current-rank) quantity. It cannot serve as a hard constraint on the program's Lagrangian variance profiles, but it is a strong, cheap diagnostic and indirect-inference target that decomposes any head-concentration mismatch into "gap too volatile" versus "ranks cross too rarely".

### Cited Findings
- Theorem 2.3 (verified in the PDF proofs, eqs. B.9-B.13): with alpha_k the time-averaged relative growth rate of whichever unit holds rank k, sigma_k^2 the time-averaged variance of the process log theta_(k) - log theta_(k+1), and kappa_k the asymptotic local-time rate of that gap at zero, alpha_k = (kappa_{k-1} - kappa_k)/2, kappa_k = -2(alpha_1+...+alpha_k), and for the stable version of the gap process E[log theta_(k) - log theta_(k+1)] = sigma_k^2 / (2 kappa_k) = sigma_k^2 / (-4(alpha_1+...+alpha_k)). The paper states that this approximates the true expected gap to the extent the stable (constant-drift reflected) version approximates the true gap process. — [Fernholz, "Empirical methods for dynamic power law distributions in the social sciences", arXiv 1602.00159; published as "Nonparametric methods and local-time-based estimation for dynamic power law distributions", J. Applied Econometrics 2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00159)
- The underlying processes may have arbitrary name-dependent, time-varying drifts and volatilities (continuous Itô processes with regularity conditions); the rank-indexed alpha_k and sigma_k are time-averaged functionals of the whole system, not structural rank-only coefficients. Stability requires alpha_1+...+alpha_k < 0 for k < N ("cross-sectional mean reversion"). Jumps are flagged as open. — [Fernholz 2016/2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00159)
- Estimation recipe: sigma_k^2 = time-average of squared changes in the gap between the units holding ranks k and k+1 at time t; local-time increments from the log ratio of the top-k sum under end-of-period ranking to the top-k sum of the start-of-period top-k names, scaled by 2 x (top-k share)/(rank-k share); kappa_k = Lambda_k(T)/T; alpha_k by differencing kappa; Gaussian-kernel smoothing across ranks with the number of passes (1-100) chosen to minimise in-sample squared deviation of predicted from observed relative sizes; 95% intervals from 10,000 bootstrap resamples of adjacent-period pairs. Application: 22 commodity prices, monthly 1980-2015; predicted versus average observed rank-size curve (squared deviation 0.143); rank-sorted "cheap vs expensive" portfolios as a sign test. — [Fernholz 2016/2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00159)
- No treatment of measurement error, sampling-interval effects or current-rank conditioning bias appears in the paper. — [Fernholz 2016/2017](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.00159)
- The published abstract states that rank-based out-of-sample forecasts of commodity prices beat random-walk forecasts at a one-month horizon; replication data are archived. — [ZBW Journal Data Archive, J. Appl. Econometrics replication record](https://journaldata.zbw.eu/dataset/ca2788da-21d5-4368-9832-b0b2cb6a3f02)
- Applications to concentration change: the rise in US bank-holding-company asset concentration is attributed to lower cross-sectional mean reversion, with idiosyncratic volatility moving differently across entity types; the framework describes the size distribution by exactly two rank-indexed factors, reversion (reduces concentration) and idiosyncratic volatility (raises it) (abstract-level). — [Fernholz & Koch, Dallas Fed WP 1604](https://www.dallasfed.org/research/papers/2016/wp1604); [Fernholz & Koch, AER P&P 2017](https://ricardofernholz.com/Research/bigBanksAER.pdf)
- Zipf characterisation: an Atlas family is Zipfian iff it is conservative (total size detrended/constant) and complete (replacement from outside the list is negligible given enough ranks); a conservative, complete quasi-Atlas family whose top weight is at most half the total is quasi-Zipfian (concave curve). — [Fernholz & Fernholz 2020](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.04285)

### Inferences
- Why it is not a hard constraint for the program. (1) Exactness needs the gap to behave like a constant-drift reflected Brownian motion; in a name-pinned head (homes far apart relative to fluctuations) adjacent ranks rarely collide, kappa_k is near zero and sigma_k^2/(2 kappa_k) overshoots the gap, which is in fact set by the difference of homes. (2) sigma_k^2 and kappa_k are conditional on current rank and at weekly sampling absorb transitory and measurement variance and noise-induced crossings; feeding them into the Lagrangian profiles would re-import the selection bias the program removed. (3) Student-t shocks violate the continuity assumption.
- Why it is still valuable. The same Eulerian functionals can be computed identically on data and on simulated panels, so selection and noise enter both sides equally (indirect-inference logic). Define the closure ratio R_k = (mean gap_k) / (sigma_k^2 / 2 kappa_k). R_k near 1 indicates exchangeable, Atlas-like behaviour at that rank; R_k well below 1 indicates name-pinned ranks. R_k by rank is a one-line characterisation of "how rank-based versus how name-based" a platform is, comparable across platforms and with the equity, bank, city and commodity values implied by the SPT literature. Since log(share_1/share_k) is the sum of the first k-1 gaps, a head-share mismatch decomposes rank by rank into excess simulated gap variance or deficient simulated crossing rate.
- An exact discrete-time counterpart exists without the Brownian approximation: for any stationary gap series G_t with finite second moment, E[G_t (G_{t+1} - G_t)] = -(1/2) E[(G_{t+1} - G_t)^2]. This is only the generic stationarity identity (already implicit in the program's refuted level-variance anchor); the added content of Fernholz's version is the link to the separately measurable collision rate. (My derivation; check before use.)
- Head-gap distribution. Rank-based theory predicts exponential head gaps; top share = 1/(1 + sum_k exp(-(xi_1+...+xi_k))) is convex in the gaps, so heavier-than-exponential simulated gaps inflate mean top-1 share even with correct mean gaps. Comparing the empirical and simulated distributions (not means) of the first few gaps is a direct test of the "lognormal amplitude x Student-t" explanation of R2.
- Lagrangian versus Eulerian ladder. The ladder of order statistics (time-mean of the k-th largest) exceeds the ladder of entity time-means sorted by permanent rank by an order-statistics premium that grows with fluctuation size. If homes are seeded from an Eulerian ladder the simulation double-counts that premium at the head; if seeded from the Lagrangian ladder, the premium (Eulerian minus Lagrangian, by rank) is itself a sharp target for the head fluctuation law. I could not determine from the fact sheet which seeding is used; flagging as a check, not a claim.
- Statistic-by-innovation interaction. If top-1 share is computed in levels, Student-t shocks to log activity have no finite exponential moment, so the population expectation of a share statistic under the model is formally unbounded and finite-sample values are driven by rare draws and scaled further by lognormal amplitude. That is a property of the scored functional, separate from whether movement moments prefer t tails. Gamma-mixed Gaussian families (Laplace/variance-gamma) have exponential tails and finite exponential moments. This does not re-propose tail surgery on the movement gate; it suggests checking the seed distribution of the share statistic (median versus mean, largest-draw contribution) before treating R2 as a dynamics failure.
- Era decomposition (R3). Fernholz-Koch attribute concentration changes to either reversion or volatility. The program's 4-8x era shifts in kappa alongside a stable ladder imply, through sigma^2/(2 kappa), that either the slow-component innovation variance moved proportionally or kappa is weakly identified. Reparameterising the slow component by its stationary variance V = sigma_perm^2/(2 kappa) and kappa, and testing era-invariance of V, would separate real non-stationarity from a ridge in (sigma_perm, kappa).

### Gaps
- The out-of-sample forecast design and loss numbers in the published J. Appl. Econometrics version could not be verified: the arXiv version read here contains only the in-sample fit and the portfolio sign test.
- No source was found that derives the gap identity for name-based mean-reverting (OU-home) systems or with observation noise.
- Fernholz & Koch (European Economic Review 2021, US versus European banks) was seen only in search summaries; two summaries disagree on the sign of the idiosyncratic-volatility change across samples.

## 3. Parsimonious scaling laws that could replace ~500 band values; tent-shaped growth distributions as variance mixtures; quantitative data collapse

### Takeaway
Three literatures (firm-growth volatility-size scaling, Taylor's fluctuation scaling, and growth-correlation scaling in online communication) describe the same one-exponent law, SD of log growth proportional to size^(-beta) with beta typically 0.1-0.25 and beta = 1/2 for pure sampling noise; this is the obvious low-dimensional replacement for rank-band profiles. The standard account of tent-shaped growth distributions is a Gaussian scale mixture, and the newest firm evidence has the same three-layer structure as the program's model (size-dependent mean volatility, a size-independent entity volatility multiplier, and residual within-entity fat tails). Collapse should be scored with a reduced chi-square style quality function and verified on unscaled data.

### Cited Findings
- Firm growth volatility falls as sigma proportional to S^(-beta); Compustat 1961-2020 quarterly sales, 24,233 firms: fitted exponent -0.204 (asymptotic s.e. 0.01), with the largest size bin above the power law. — [Moran, Secchi & Bouchaud, "Revisiting granular models of firm growth", arXiv 2404.15226 (preprint)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15226)
- Firm-specific volatility (mean absolute deviation of a firm's growth rates), rescaled by the size-conditional mean, has a size-independent distribution across 25 size bins (collapse judged visually); it is fitted better by a modified inverse-gamma with tail exponent about 4.6 than granular models allow (they predict a tail exponent between 1 and 2 and a low-diversification hump, which a bimodality test rejects); growth rates rescaled by firm-specific volatility remain fat-tailed at all sizes (fitted with a generalised stretched-exponential family). The authors conclude compositional/granular models cannot account for the growth statistics. — [Moran, Secchi & Bouchaud 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15226)
- Proportional-growth-of-units theory predicts a size-variance exponent that crosses over from 0 for very small to 1/2 for very large entities, with effective values about 0.14-0.2 for realistic parameters. — [Riccaboni, Pammolli, Buldyrev, Ponta & Stanley, PNAS 2008, arXiv 0904.1404](https://arxiv.org/abs/0904.1404)
- The same framework predicts a growth-rate density with an exponential (Laplace) body and power-law wings with density exponent 3, tested on products, firms and countries. — [Fu, Pammolli, Buldyrev, Riccaboni, Matia, Yamasaki & Stanley, PNAS 2005, arXiv physics/0512005](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0512005)
- Independent aggregation predicts beta = 1/2; the observed ~0.18 is the puzzle; power-law distributed sub-unit sizes give anomalous variance scaling and non-Gaussian limits. — [Wyart & Bouchaud, "Statistical models for company growth", arXiv cond-mat/0210479](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0210479)
- Additive replication with a heavy-tailed replication distribution gives Lévy-distributed growth fluctuations and the size scaling of the standard deviation; reported to improve collapse for birds, firms and mutual funds (abstract-level). — [Schwarzkopf, Axtell & Farmer, "The cause of universality in growth fluctuations", arXiv 1004.5397](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.5397)
- Laplace as a variance mixture: a normal scale mixture with exponential (gamma shape 1) mixing is Laplace; Bottazzi-Secchi obtain the Laplace from random partition of opportunities among firms; Subbotin fits to French and US data give shapes around or below the Laplace value; a variance-gamma (jump) reading has been proposed (search-summary level). — [Bottazzi & Secchi, LEM WP 2005/16](https://www.lem.sssup.it/WPLem/2005-16.html); [Arata, RIETI DP 14-E-033](https://www.rieti.go.jp/en/publications/summary/14060009.html)
- The size-variance relationship as an empirical input: the largest firms are least volatile, which damps granular aggregate effects to about 15% of US aggregate fluctuations. — [Yeh, Review of Economics and Statistics 107(3) (2025) 864-871](https://ideas.repec.org/a/tpr/restat/v107y2025i3p864-871.html) (the brief's "Yeh 2023" is this paper; earlier versions date from 2017-2021)
- Taylor's law / fluctuation scaling: SD proportional to mean^alpha with alpha mostly between 1/2 and 1; alpha = 1/2 when elements differ only in the number of equal-impact events (central limit), alpha = 1 under a dominant external driver, intermediate values from crossovers or from "impact inhomogeneity" (more active elements also have larger events); temporal and ensemble exponents need not agree. — [Eisler, Bartos & Kertész, Advances in Physics 57 (2008) 89-142, arXiv 0708.2053](https://arxiv.org/abs/0708.2053)
- A 2026 null-model paper argues Taylor's law is a crossover between sampling noise and latent heterogeneity and is largely insensitive to the latent distribution; Zipf and Heaps laws likewise emerge from heterogeneous rates plus finite sampling (abstract-level). — [Allegri, Nauta & De Domenico, arXiv 2607.03297](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.03297)
- Online activity: for members of two online communities, the SD of message-count growth conditional on prior activity decays with exponents 0.22 +/- 0.01 and 0.17 +/- 0.03, versus 1/2 after shuffling; the paper links the exponent to long-term correlation via beta = 1 - H (H the DFA fluctuation exponent). — [Rybski, Buldyrev, Havlin, Liljeros & Makse, PNAS 106 (2009) 12640-12645](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2722366)
- A Taylor-type relation appears in a 2025 ranking-dynamics application: SD of hourly inflow change scales as mean inflow^alpha with alpha between 0.84 and 0.95 across 60 Chinese cities. — [Wang, "Ranking dynamics of urban mobility", arXiv 2503.08418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08418)
- Macroecology has an almost isomorphic three-law description of abundance time series: per-species abundance fluctuations are gamma distributed, variance scales as mean squared (Taylor exponent 2, constant coefficient of variation), mean abundances are lognormal across species; all three follow from a stochastic logistic model with multiplicative environmental noise. Daily log abundance changes in gut microbiota are Laplace (exponents about 0.7-0.8) and their SD declines with mean abundance. — [Grilli, Nature Communications 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7506021/); [Ji, Sheth, Dixit, Tchourine & Vitkup, Nature Microbiology 2020 (preprint)](https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/370676v2)
- Quantitative collapse: Bhattacharjee & Seno define a residual-based measure of collapse whose minimisation yields exponents and error bars; Houdayer & Hartmann's quality function is a reduced chi-square against a master curve estimated from the other data sets (about 1 for a good collapse), implemented in pyfssa and Melchert's autoScale. — [Bhattacharjee & Seno, J. Phys. A 34 (2001) 6375, arXiv cond-mat/0102515](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0102515); [pyfssa documentation](https://pyfssa.readthedocs.io/en/stable/reference/fssa.html); [Melchert, arXiv 0910.5403](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/0910.5403)
- Caveat: low dispersion of rescaled data does not guarantee the collapse describes the original data; goodness should be evaluated on unscaled data. — [Bayesian machine scientist on the Nikuradse data, arXiv 2004.12163](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2004.12163)

### Inferences
- One exponent, three literatures. For log activity, Taylor's alpha, the firm-growth beta and Rybski's H are related by beta = 1 - alpha = 1 - H. A band profile sigma_c(z) can therefore be written log sigma_c = a_c - beta_c x (home log-level), two numbers per component (perm, trans, medium, obs), roughly 8-10 parameters instead of ~500.
- Sharp, falsifiable sub-hypotheses: (H1) beta_perm = beta_trans, the size-scaling counterpart of the b = 1 amplitude factorisation (both are statements that all dynamic components share one scale field); (H2) beta_obs is near 1/2 because replication noise is counting noise, with departures toward smaller values where events are clustered (Eisler-Kertész impact inhomogeneity); (H3) beta is common across platforms within the 0.1-0.25 band seen for firms and online communication. Failure of H3 is informative rather than damaging; H1 and H2 are the parsimony claims.
- Structural match to Moran-Secchi-Bouchaud: their decomposition is (size-conditional mean volatility) x (size-independent entity multiplier) x (fat-tailed standardised shock). The program has lognormal v_i for the second layer and Student-t for the third. Their two empirical findings line up with open residuals: the multiplier's upper tail is thin (inverse-gamma exponent about 4.6; a lognormal with large s puts more mass on extreme multipliers), relevant to R2; and standardised growth stays fat-tailed after removing entity volatility, parallel to the program's "empirical standardised tails fatter than simulated".
- Variance-mixture reading of the program's innovation law: pooled changes are Gaussian scale mixtures with mixing law = (lognormal, persistent across time, per entity) x (inverse-gamma, redrawn each period). The firm literature's Laplace body corresponds to gamma mixing; power-law wings to inverse-gamma mixing. A within-entity mixing variable that is persistent in time (volatility clustering at the entity level) is a third option the cited firm papers do not test and the program has not listed as tried (the refuted item was a common time-varying volatility factor).
- Estimation caution: s is measured as cross-entity dispersion of log change-variance; sample variances of heavy-tailed series are noisy, so raw dispersion over-states s by an amount that shrinks with panel length. Moran et al. guard against this with a robust scale (mean absolute deviation) and a minimum-observations filter. If the program's s is not already deconvolved for sampling noise, the bias is largest on the shortest panel (Facebook Era A, about 88 weeks), which is where the head is over-concentrated. (Conjecture; depends on implementation details I did not inspect.)
- Non-transfer: the firm beta of about 0.2 is explained by diversification across sub-units (and Moran et al. argue even that explanation fails); there is no reason for a page or subreddit to inherit the value. Riccaboni et al. predict a crossover in beta, so a single power law may need a two-regime form. Rank coordinates and size coordinates differ: on a Zipf-like ladder a power law in size is a power law in rank, but at the concave quasi-Zipf head it is not, so the scaling variable should be home log-level, not rank.
- Collapse methodology: the b = 1 amplitude collapse and any sigma(z) scaling should be reported with a Houdayer-Hartmann style quality statistic and bootstrap exponent intervals, and then validated on unscaled quantities (the out-of-sample movement gate with the reduced parameterisation). The firm-growth literature itself still relies on visual collapse, so this would exceed field practice.

### Gaps
- Stanley et al. (1996), Amaral et al. (1997) and Gabaix's granular paper were not re-read (known to the program); their exponents are quoted via later papers.
- No study was found that measures volatility-size scaling for web pages, subreddits, channels or Wikipedia articles with a separate measurement-noise instrument; Rybski et al. is the closest (individual users, not endpoints).
- Whether a persistent within-entity stochastic-volatility layer improves fit for attention data is untested in anything I found.
- Zaoli & Grilli (2021) on abrupt carrying-capacity shifts in microbiome series could not be verified (venue and the "jumps" claim both unconfirmed in this session).

## 4. Ranking-dynamics physics beyond Iñiguez et al. 2022 and Blumm et al. 2012: statistics a referee will expect, and how to benchmark against displacement + replacement

### Takeaway
The community's toolkit is descriptive and list-based: flux, turnover, rank-change profile, displacement kernel, rank diversity, and (in 2025-26 follow-ups) weighted rank change with influx/outflux/intra decomposition, lifetime, re-entry, run lengths, similarity decay and rank-turbulence divergence. The 2-parameter model is fitted to flux and turnover and judged qualitatively with no held-out validation, no element heterogeneity and no return of departed elements, so a like-for-like out-of-sample benchmark on its own statistics is feasible and likely favourable on memory statistics but currently unfavourable on flux at the head (R1).

### Cited Findings
- Statistics in Iñiguez et al.: rank flux F_t (probability an element enters or leaves the list at t), turnover o_t = N_t/N_0 (distinct elements ever seen relative to list size) and mean turnover rate, rank change C (probability the occupant of rank R changes between observations), and displacement probability P_{x,t} decomposed as exp(-nu t)(L_t + D_{x,t}) with a rank-independent Lévy term L_t = (1 - exp(-tau t))/N and a diffusion term obeying a Wright-Fisher-type equation. Regime weights: W_levy = exp(-nu)(1 - exp(-tau)), W_diff = exp(-nu) exp(-tau), W_repl = 1 - exp(-nu). — [Iñiguez, Pineda, Gershenson & Barabási, "Dynamics of ranking", Nature Communications 13, 1646 (2022)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8960905/)
- Model and fit: list of length N_0 inside a system of N elements (p = N_0/N); per step a random element is moved to a random rank with probability tau and replaced by a new element with probability nu. Mean flux F = 1 - exp(-nu)[p + (1-p) exp(-tau)]. tau and nu are obtained by numerically solving the flux and turnover-rate equations with the empirical values; rescaled parameters collapse on tau_r nu_r = 1 for open lists. No held-out validation; agreement of P_{x,t} and C is shown qualitatively, with bootstrapped simulations used to flag outlying datasets; a small bias in tau is acknowledged. 30 lists from minutes to centuries; five are closed. Acknowledged limits: randomness and stationarity assumed, no element-specific features, scores not modelled, flux rank-dependent in very open systems. — [Iñiguez et al. 2022](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8960905/)
- Replaced elements leave the system in the model's definition (they are swapped for new elements), i.e. there is no return channel. — [Iñiguez et al. 2022, arXiv version with SI](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13439)
- Code for these statistics and the model is public (Farranks). — [Farranks, Zenodo 5910806](https://zenodo.org/records/5910806)
- Rank diversity d(k) = number of distinct elements occupying rank k over the observation window divided by the number of observations; fitted by a cumulative Gaussian in log k with location mu (1.6-2.1) and width sigma (0.4-0.6) for six languages (Google Books 1800-2008); head/body/tail defined by mu +/- 2 sigma; fits judged by mean squared error; null model = scale-invariant Gaussian random walk with step SD proportional to current rank (single parameter, 0.0575 in one case), chosen over a Lorentzian because heavy tails produce unobserved long jumps. — [Cocho, Flores, Gershenson, Pineda & Sánchez, PLOS ONE 2015](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0121898)
- The same sigmoid and random-walk model reproduce rank diversity in sports and games rankings (abstract-level). — [Morales et al., EPJ Data Science 5, 33 (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1140/epjds/s13688-016-0096-y)
- 2025 follow-up with Iñiguez as co-author (Netflix/HBO/Disney+/Amazon top lists 2022; Spotify charts 2017-18; daily): weighted rank change c_{i,t} = |r_t - r_{t-1}| / min(r_t, r_{t-1}) with absent items assigned rank N_b + 1; aggregate split into influx, outflux and intra-list parts; flux fractions overall and by decile of change days; power spectrum of the aggregate series (weekly peak); item lifetime, first, last and peak rank and their correlations. Findings: influx dominates compositional change (e.g. Netflix influx fraction 0.56 versus outflux 0.09); peak rank correlates with lifetime more than first or last rank; lifetimes heavy-tailed. No generative or null model. — [Lee, Iñiguez, Jo & Park, "Ranking dynamics in movies and music", arXiv 2506.21944](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21944)
- 2025 application to urban mobility: turnover and turnover rate for hourly top-100 locations in 60 cities; no model fitted; cites Iñiguez et al. for the measures. — [Wang, arXiv 2503.08418](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.08418)
- 2026 Billboard study (Hot 100 and Billboard 200 over six to seven decades; accepted at Royal Society Open Science per the arXiv record): permanence distributions, re-entry frequency, uninterrupted run lengths, decay of similarity between charts at increasing lag, rank-turbulence divergence between consecutive charts (top positions change by reshuffling more than by turnover), entropy-based predictability of rank moves, trajectory clustering (abstract-level). — [Pessa, Flores, Perc & Ribeiro, arXiv 2607.11446](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11446)
- A 2024 preprint uses the open/closed distinction on 65 years of US charts and reports longer top-1 lifetimes and faster bottom-rank turnover over time (search-summary level). — [arXiv 2405.07574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07574)
- Rank clocks: long-run city and firm lists keep a stable rank-size form while membership churns; half-life of list membership and rank-shift distance statistics are the associated measures (search-summary level; formulas not verified). — [Batty, "Rank clocks", Nature 444 (2006) 592-596](https://ideas.repec.org/a/nat/nature/v444y2006i7119d10.1038_nature05302.html); [Batty, CASA Working Paper 152 (2010)](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/id/eprint/19246/)
- Ecology analogue linking a stationary abundance law to turnover: OU-fluctuating growth rates with immigration and self-limitation give a power-law section with exponent nu = 1 - 2 r_eff/gamma_eff (gamma = 2 sigma_r^2 tau), and turnover is summarised by decay of Bray-Curtis similarity with lag and by effective richness; validated on about 10,000 simulated communities, comparison with data qualitative. — [Mallmin, Traulsen & De Monte, arXiv 2505.01376; Ecology Letters 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.01376)

### Inferences
- Expected referee checklist: (1) F and turnover rate versus list size, and the platform's position in the (tau_r, nu_r) plane relative to the universal curve; (2) the rank-change profile C(R/N_0), which separates open from closed systems; (3) displacement kernel P_{x,t} at several lags; (4) rank diversity sigmoid parameters; (5) since 2025, influx/outflux/intra shares of weighted rank change, lifetime and peak-rank relations, re-entry and run-length distributions, similarity decay. All are computable from existing ranked panels with a declared list length (e.g. top K/2 of the K universe so that the buffer supplies the "outside").
- Benchmark design: fit (tau, nu) on each training window from F and turnover rate exactly as published; simulate; score both models on the same held-out statistics, including the program's movement-gate moments and the list statistics above, with bootstrap bands. This is stricter than the original paper's validation and removes the objection that the gate is home-grown.
- Where the 2-parameter model must fail by construction: no element memory or heterogeneity (rank autocorrelation at long lags, entity-specific persistence, amplitude heterogeneity); no return of departed elements (empirically 95-98% of absences are temporary per the fact sheet, so re-entry statistics are decisive); no scores (silent on shares, ladder, head concentration). Where it may beat the program's model: flux and turnover are matched by construction, while the program's simulation under-produces established departures by 2.3-2.7x; on the community's headline statistic the program currently loses, and that should be reported, not avoided.
- Rank diversity is a current-rank (Eulerian) occupancy statistic and will be inflated by transitory and measurement noise in exactly the way the program has documented; reporting it both raw and on noise-filtered ranks would be a small methodological contribution to that literature.
- Non-transfer: their timescale-free rescaling presumes stationarity and uniform sampling of elements; with era effects (R3) and censored instruments, flux and turnover should be computed on census-quality panels (Reddit) first.

### Gaps
- Bradley-Terry/Elo-type latent-strength dynamics and first-passage/occupation or lead-change statistics of rank were not searched (search budget exhausted). From memory, relevant starting points are Glickman's dynamic paired-comparison models and the Krapivsky-Redner and Clauset-Kogan-Redner work on lead changes; none is verified here and no claim is made about them.
- A systematic "cited by" list for Iñiguez et al. 2022 could not be retrieved; only four follow-ups were identified and others surely exist.
- The exact published forms of tau_r, nu_r and the turnover-rate equation were garbled in extraction and must be taken from the PDF before implementation.
- Blumm et al. (2012) and the Morales et al. (2016) full text were not re-read.

## 5. Random-growth theory of fast transitions and heterogeneity: which mechanisms correspond to persistent amplitude heterogeneity, and what they predict for the tail

### Takeaway
In random-growth theory the stationary tail responds very slowly to parameter changes (rate set by death/reset and drift-to-variance ratio; slower further into the tail), so a stationary-looking ladder is fully compatible with trending dynamic parameters. The mechanisms the literature adds to obtain fast or realistic dynamics are heterogeneity in types (persistent or switching growth regimes) and non-Gaussian, state-dependent persistent shocks; the program's v_i is type dependence in volatility, which that literature treats less than type dependence in mean growth, and the closest matches to the R1 phenomenon are switching/large-reversible-shock mechanisms rather than heavier i.i.d. tails.

### Cited Findings
- Baseline: log size follows dx = mu dt + sigma dW with death at rate delta and reinjection, and/or a reflecting barrier; stationary Pareto exponent with death only is zeta = (-mu + sqrt(mu^2 + 2 sigma^2 delta))/sigma^2. — [Gabaix, Lasry, Lions & Moll, "The dynamics of inequality", NBER WP 21363; Econometrica 84 (2016), DOI 10.3982/ECTA13569](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21363)
- Average speed of convergence (total-variation norm): lambda = delta without a reflecting barrier; lambda = (1/2)(mu^2/sigma^2) 1{mu<0} + delta with one; with barrier and no death lambda = sigma^2/(8 eta^2) where eta = 1/zeta, so more unequal stationary states converge more slowly. — [Gabaix et al. 2016, Proposition 1](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21363)
- Tail speed: the -xi-th moment (weighted norm) converges at lambda(xi) = mu xi - (sigma^2/2) xi^2 + delta, which falls as more weight is put on the upper tail; in the sigma = 0 (Steindl) case the asymptotic exponent takes infinite time to adjust and the local exponent does not move on impact. Calibration: delta = 1/30, sigma^2 rising 0.01 to 0.025, zeta 2.56 to 1.6, average half-life 20.8 years and longer in the tail, an order of magnitude too slow for the data. — [Gabaix et al. 2016, Proposition 2 and section 4.3](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21363)
- Their remedies: "type dependence" (some units are high-growth types) and "scale dependence" (shocks that scale all incomes, e.g. skill-price changes) (abstract-level for the formal statements). — [Gabaix et al. 2016](https://www.econometricsociety.org/doi/10.3982/ECTA13569)
- Markov-modulated random growth: with a Markov-switching type that shifts growth and reset probability, the size distribution has a Pareto upper tail whose exponent is the unique positive root of an equation in the spectral radius of a matrix-valued function, and an associated eigenvector gives the type composition of the upper tail (abstract-level). — [Beare & Toda, Econometrica 2022, arXiv 1712.01431](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.01431)
- Entrepreneurial growth with creative destruction: heterogeneous entrepreneurs' incomes grow exponentially until displaced; the Pareto exponent reflects growth versus destruction rates (abstract-level). — [Jones & Kim, NBER WP 20637; J. Political Economy 126(5) 2018](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20637)
- Slow aggregate convergence tied to the firm-size tail index; illustrative half-life about 44 years (search-summary level). — [Luttmer, Minneapolis Fed WP 696 / WP 748](https://www.minneapolisfed.org/research/wp/wp696.pdf)
- Double Pareto-lognormal: geometric Brownian motion observed at an exponentially distributed age gives a double Pareto law; with lognormal starting sizes the result is a product of double Pareto and lognormal, with Pareto behaviour in both tails and a lognormal body. — [Reed & Jorgensen, Commun. Statist. Theory Methods 33 (2004) 1733-1753 (author copy)](https://web.uvic.ca/~math-statistics/emeritus/wjreed/dPlN.3.pdf)
- Laplace/double-Pareto as the generic limit of geometric sums; in income data a mean-reverting diffusion with a stationary double Pareto law is preferred to a unit-root one (abstract-level). — [Toda, J. Economic Behavior & Organization 84 (2012) 364-381](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jeborg/v84y2012i1p364-381.html)
- Joint matching of a stationary distribution and mobility: a wealth model estimated by simulated method of moments targeting wealth shares in eight quantile groups together with wealth-mobility moments from a short panel (search-summary level). — [Benhabib, Bisin & Luo, American Economic Review 109(5) (2019) 1623-1647 (NBER WP 21721)](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/21721.html)
- Non-Gaussian persistent shocks in the closest large-panel analogue: earnings changes show negative skewness and very high kurtosis that vary with age and earnings level; impulse responses are asymmetric (for high earners positive changes are transitory and negative ones persistent, and the reverse for low earners); the preferred process has normal-mixture innovations in both the persistent and transitory components plus a state-dependent long-term non-employment shock; estimated by targeting a rich set of moments of changes. — [Guvenen, Karahan, Ozkan & Song, Econometrica 89(5) (2021) 2303-2339](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA14603)
- Stochastic logistic growth with multiplicative noise yields a gamma (not lognormal) stationary law around each entity's own carrying capacity and reproduces temporal patterns in microbial series. — [Grilli 2020](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7506021/)

### Inferences
- Correspondence table. Program's v_i (persistent lognormal amplitude on all components) = type dependence in volatility, which Gabaix et al. do not analyse (their types differ in mean growth) but Beare-Toda's framework covers: in a pure random-growth system the tail exponent would be set by the most tail-generating type and the upper tail would be populated disproportionately by high-volatility types. The program's rebirth at the buffer bottom = death/reinjection friction; the seeded ladder = no ladder genesis, so the Pareto-tail formulas are not predictions of the program's model.
- Prediction to test: under random growth, high-amplitude entities should be over-represented at the head; under entity-home dynamics amplitude and home are free to be independent or negatively related. The empirical joint law of amplitude and permanent rank at the head discriminates the two views and bears directly on R2 (a lognormal amplitude drawn independently of rank places some very high-v entities at the head in simulation).
- R3 reframed. Slow tail convergence means the ladder can look stationary for years while s or kappa trend; conversely, under the program's OU-home structure the level distribution adjusts at rate about 2 kappa, which is fast when kappa is large. The observed combination (stable ladder, trending s, 4-8x kappa shifts) should be checked against these two speeds; if implied adjustment is fast yet the ladder did not move, the shifts are more likely identification artefacts along the sigma_perm^2/kappa ridge.
- R1 reframed. The literature's devices for large reversible displacements are (i) Markov switching between growth regimes (Gabaix et al., Beare-Toda), (ii) normal-mixture innovations in the persistent component and a large state-dependent negative shock with gradual recovery (Guvenen et al.), and (iii) nonlinear reversion. A Gaussian AR(1)/OU slow component cannot generate rare large persistent excursions regardless of how heavy the fast shock's tails are, because fast shocks mean-revert quickly. The discriminating moment is how the kurtosis of standardised h-week changes decays with h: Gaussian persistent innovations imply rapid decay toward 3, mixture innovations imply slow decay.
- Nonlinear reversion as a zero-parameter alternative. OU in logs is the stochastic Gompertz model (lognormal stationary law); the stochastic logistic model has, in log coordinates, a restoring force that grows exponentially above home and saturates below it. It therefore predicts a left-skewed stationary log-level law (log-gamma): long, slowly recovering downward excursions of established entities and a thin upper tail. Qualitatively that is the R1 pattern (returnable departures from the top set) together with less head concentration (R2, Facebook sign), obtained with symmetric innovations. This differs from the refuted "skewed innovations" (skewness in shocks) because the asymmetry is in the drift. Guvenen et al.'s level- and sign-dependent persistence is the earnings-data counterpart.
- Validation precedent. Benhabib-Bisin-Luo and Guvenen et al. both estimate by simulated moments with distributional and mobility/change moments jointly; that is the same logic as the program's scorecard plus gate, and supports presenting the program's approach as indirect inference with pre-declared moments.
- Parametric ladder. A double Pareto-lognormal or quasi-Atlas (linear variance) description would replace the seeded empirical ladder by 3-4 parameters for cross-platform comparison; this is descriptive only (the program does not claim ladder genesis) and would not change dynamics.

### Gaps
- The formal definitions and propositions for type and scale dependence in Gabaix et al. (later sections) were not read; only the baseline speed results were verified from the text.
- I recall that Guvenen et al. condition moments on "recent earnings" (a multi-year average, i.e. a permanent-level proxy analogous to permanent rank), but the search did not confirm the wording; verify before citing as precedent for permanent-rank conditioning.
- No paper was found that analyses persistent heterogeneity in volatility (as opposed to mean growth) for stationary-tail maintenance in an attention or web context.
- Benhabib-Bisin(-Zhu) theoretical papers and Luttmer (2011) on fast-growing young firms were not read.

## 6. Published models of online attention that combine a stationary rank-size law with individual-level churn

### Takeaway
I found no published model that jointly reproduces a stationary rank-size law and entity-level movement for online endpoints with explicit measurement-noise handling and out-of-sample validation. Existing work is either rank-based simulation validated qualitatively (Hindman & Rogers), burst/rank-shift models validated on burst-size distributions (Ratkiewicz et al.), item-level growth models with heavy-tailed noise (Miotto et al.; Hawkes-type popularity models), or descriptive chart statistics (2025-26 follow-ups).

### Cited Findings
- Web traffic: a deliberately minimal simulation in which each of 300 sites changes randomly each day as a function of its current rank only; larger sites are more stable and traffic-change variance rises as rank worsens; "leakage" (probability of dropping out of the top list next day) is the churn statistic; from varied initial conditions the simulation converges to an approximately power-law distribution; reported validation is qualitative agreement (search-summary level, via the book; SSRN page returned 403). — [Hindman & Rogers, "The dynamics of web traffic", SSRN 1697382](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=1697382); [Hindman, The Internet Trap, Princeton University Press 2018](https://press.princeton.edu/books/hardcover/9780691159263/the-internet-trap)
- Wikipedia and Chilean web: popularity dynamics are bursty with fat-tailed burst magnitudes and inter-event times; model = preferential popularity growth plus random rank shifts in which a page is moved to a rank between its current position and the top; compared with observed burst statistics for Wikipedia (abstract-level). — [Ratkiewicz, Fortunato, Flammini, Menczer & Vespignani, Phys. Rev. Lett. 105, 158701 (2010), arXiv 1005.2704](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.2704)
- YouTube videos: average view gain proportional to current views (Gibrat), but fluctuations around the average are heavy-tailed; a stochastic differential equation with Lévy noise estimates the probability of an ordinary video becoming a big hit much better than proportional-growth models, which underestimate it (abstract-level). — [Miotto, Kantz & Altmann, Phys. Rev. E 95, 032311 (2017), arXiv 1609.06399](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.06399)
- Item-level Hawkes intensity models quantify endogenous virality and exogenous sensitivity per video; they predict item view counts, not rank dynamics across a population (search-summary level). — [Rizoiu et al., "Expecting to be HIP", WWW 2017](https://ssanner.github.io/papers/www17_hip.pdf)
- Online communication activity obeys growth-fluctuation scaling with long-term correlation (see section 3). — [Rybski et al. 2009](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2722366)
- Streaming and chart rankings (Netflix, Spotify, Billboard) are characterised descriptively in 2025-26 with no generative model. — [Lee et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.21944); [Pessa et al. 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.11446)
- Twitch: a measurement study reports that views by rank do not follow a classical Zipf line (search-summary level); no rank-dynamics model located. — [arXiv 1502.04666](https://arxiv.org/abs/1502.04666)

### Inferences
- In SPT terms the Hindman-Rogers simulation is a first-order (rank-only) model, and inherits the first-order ergodicity property that every site eventually visits every rank equally; the current program is the name-based generalisation of it, for the same reason Fernholz-Ichiba-Karatzas moved to second-order models. That lineage is a clean way to position the paper.
- Ratkiewicz et al.'s upward rank-shift kernel and Miotto et al.'s Lévy noise both attack the same gap the program faces from the other side (too few large moves), but with i.i.d. jump mechanisms and no return dynamics; neither addresses persistent-then-returning displacements (R1).
- The novelty claim that survives this search: joint conditional reproduction of the ladder and entity-level movement, with an independent noise instrument, permanent-rank estimation, and registered out-of-sample gates. The breadth gap (R7) relative to the 30-system comparator remains the main vulnerability.

### Gaps
- Work citing The Internet Trap's dynamics chapter could not be enumerated (no citation-index access).
- No model was found for subreddit, Wikipedia-article or app-store rank dynamics with stationary ladder plus churn; absence is not established because searching stopped when the budget ran out.
- Hindman & Rogers details are from a search summary of the book, not the paper text.

## Implications for the rank-diffusion model

Ranked by expected value (payoff x probability x cheapness of the first test). Each item states the residual addressed, the first cheap measurement, and where it may not transfer. All are my inferences from the cited sources; none has been tested on the program's data. Frozen specifications and thresholds are owner-gated; everything below is measure-first and would need pre-registration before any model change.

1. **Fluctuation-scaling parameterisation of the band profiles** — addresses P (and R3, R7). Replace each nonparametric profile by log sigma_c = a_c - beta_c x (home log-level), with pre-registered sub-hypotheses beta_perm = beta_trans (the size analogue of b = 1), beta_obs near 1/2, and a common beta across platforms as a stretch claim. First measurement: no refit needed; regress the existing ~500 band values on home log-level per component, report exponents with bootstrap intervals, a Houdayer-Hartmann collapse quality statistic, and the residual pattern (look for the Riccaboni crossover and head curvature). Then, as a registered experiment, run the movement gate with the reduced (about 10-parameter) profile set against the full set. Non-transfer risks: the firm exponent has no reason to carry over; the head of a concave ladder may need home level, not rank, as the scaling variable; Taylor exponents can be sampling-heterogeneity crossovers, which the program's independent sigma_obs instrument is unusually well placed to separate.

2. **Non-Gaussian innovations in the persistent component / large reversible shocks** — addresses R1 (and the fat standardised tails). Motivated by Guvenen et al.'s mixture-normal persistent shocks and state-dependent large negative shock, and by switching-type models (Gabaix et al.; Beare-Toda). First measurement: kurtosis and upper-quantile ratios of per-entity standardised h-week changes for h = 1, 4, 13, 26, 52 among established entities, data versus simulation. If empirical kurtosis decays slowly with h while the simulation's collapses toward Gaussian, the missing dynamics are in the slow component, not the fast shock. Add excursion statistics from the 2026 chart literature: spell lengths below the top-K/2 boundary, depth, and return rank. Non-transfer: earnings shocks have institutional causes; here the candidate causes are platform or instrument events, so check for clustering in calendar time before modelling as idiosyncratic.

3. **Nonlinear (logistic) versus linear (Gompertz/OU) home reversion** — addresses R1 and R2 with no added parameter. First measurement: for established entities on a census instrument, the binned drift function E[next change | current deviation from home] and the skewness of within-entity log-level residuals. OU predicts a linear, symmetric drift and symmetric residuals; the stochastic logistic form predicts strong pull above home, saturating pull below, and left-skewed (log-gamma) residuals. Transitory and measurement noise add a symmetric linear component, so asymmetry survives contamination. Non-transfer: the logistic form comes from population dynamics with a carrying capacity; asymmetry could also arise from zeros, floors or censoring, so restrict to strata where those are absent.

4. **Out-of-sample benchmark against the displacement + replacement model on the ranking community's own statistics** — addresses R7 and R4 and pre-empts the most likely referee request. First measurement: on each panel compute flux, turnover rate, rank-change profile, displacement kernel, rank diversity, influx/outflux/intra shares of weighted rank change, lifetime and re-entry statistics for a declared list (top K/2); fit (tau, nu) on training windows as published; score both models on held-out windows. Expect wins on memory statistics and re-entry (the 2-parameter model has no return channel) and a loss on head flux until R1 is fixed; report both. Non-transfer: their rescaling assumes stationarity; use census panels first.

5. **SPT/Fernholz rank-indexed diagnostics as indirect-inference targets for the head** — addresses R2. First measurement, on data and simulation identically, for ranks 1 to about 50: mean log-gap, gap variance, collision/leakage rate (Fernholz eq. 3.5 or the Itkin-Larsson top-k leakage estimator), the closure ratio R_k = mean gap / (gap variance / 2 x collision rate), the distribution of the first few gaps against an exponential reference, and the difference between the order-statistic ladder and the entity-mean ladder. This localises the head mismatch to specific ranks and to "too volatile" versus "too few crossings", and tests whether home seeding double-counts the order-statistics premium. Also examine the seed distribution of the share statistic itself (median versus mean, largest-draw contribution) given that log-scale Student-t shocks have no exponential moment. Non-transfer: the identity is exact only for constant-coefficient gap dynamics; treat it as a diagnostic, never as a constraint on Lagrangian parameters.

6. **Era decomposition and reparameterisation of the slow component** — addresses R3 (and R6). Following the Fernholz-Koch reversion-versus-volatility decomposition and the Gabaix et al. speed formulas: re-express the slow component by stationary variance V = sigma_perm^2/(2 kappa) and kappa; test era-invariance of V and of the Eulerian ratio gap variance / collision rate. If V is stable while kappa moves 4-8x, the non-stationarity is largely a weak-identification ridge and forecasts should be driven by V. First measurement: recompute from existing era estimates; no new data contact. Non-transfer: the speed formulas are for random growth with reset, not OU-home systems; use them only for orders of magnitude.

7. **Amplitude-law audit** — addresses R2 (and R3's s trend). (a) Deconvolve s for sampling noise in per-entity variance estimates (bias depends on kurtosis and panel length; compare a robust scale such as mean absolute deviation, as in Moran et al.); (b) compare the upper tail of estimated amplitudes among head entities with the fitted lognormal (the firm analogue is thin-tailed, inverse-gamma with exponent about 4.6); (c) test independence of amplitude and permanent rank at the head, which random-growth theory with volatility types would predict to fail in one direction. First measurement: all three from existing per-entity estimates. Non-transfer: the firm multiplier distribution is not a prior for attention data; it only shows that lognormal is not the default elsewhere.

8. **Exit machinery as rank-dependent on/off hazards fitted from event counts** — addresses R1's estimand mismatch. Baker-Smyth fit rank-dependent entry and exit intensities from event counts and report that turnover, not drift, stabilises the calibrated distribution; the analogous step here is a two-state presence process with permanent-rank-dependent absence and return hazards, replacing absence-as-death. First measurement: empirical absence-spell duration and return-rank distributions by permanent-rank stratum, compared with simulated ones. Non-transfer: equity delisting is permanent; here most absences are temporary, so the return hazard is the essential addition. Source is an unrefereed 2026 preprint seen at abstract level.

9. **Parametric ladder and universe conditions** — addresses P and R7 (descriptive). Fit a double Pareto-lognormal or quasi-Atlas (linear variance, constant drift) form to each platform's ladder for cross-platform comparison, and report Fernholz-Fernholz conservation and completeness diagnostics as part of universe justification (their example shows truncation to a short list breaks completeness). First measurement: quantile-function fit to existing ladders. No effect on dynamics; low risk, modest value.

10. **Cross-checks of persistence** — addresses R5 (low priority). Rybski et al.'s relation between the fluctuation-scaling exponent and the long-memory exponent, and Fernholz's suggestion that top-rank variance at different sampling intervals identifies Atlas parameters, both suggest reporting a per-entity long-memory estimate and an Eulerian variance-by-sampling-interval curve as extra descriptive rows. First measurement: DFA exponent by stratum on existing series. Likely confirmatory rather than decisive.

Items deliberately not proposed because the fact sheet lists them as refuted: finer rank bands, a common time-varying volatility factor, skewed innovations, rank-dependent t df, tail-shape surgery on the fast innovations, burst/near-tie head machinery. Items 2 and 3 differ from these in mechanism (persistent-component innovations; asymmetry in drift rather than in shocks), and item 5's exponential-moment point concerns the scored functional rather than the movement-gate fit.
