# Large-displacement mechanisms and their discriminating moment signatures (established-departure deficit)

_Researcher notes, 2026-10-08. Scope: fact-sheet residual 1 (established-departure deficit), with residuals 2, 3 and 5 as context. Read-only with respect to the repository: no project code was run, no data were touched, nothing under /Volumes was accessed. The shared web-search budget ran out part-way through; everything I could not verify against a source is listed under Gaps and is not used as a cited finding. Numbers quoted from the program are from `llm_fitting/MODEL_STATUS.md` (canonical) and are flagged as such._

Notation used throughout: X_it = log activity with the common factor removed; Δ_h X_it = X_i,t+h − X_it; "M0" = the current model (Gaussian OU home + AR(1) fast with Student-t shocks + optional Gaussian medium AR(1) + Spec-B noise, one lognormal amplitude per entity). Every measurement proposed below is meant to be computed with identical construction on the empirical dev panels and on M0 simulated panels (train-defined permanent-rank cohort, Lagrangian follow-up), so the comparison is always data vs M0, never data vs Gaussian.

## 1. What is the menu of candidate mechanisms, and what does the canonical literature actually establish about each?

### Takeaway
The closest solved analogue is the administrative-earnings literature: Guvenen–Karahan–Ozkan–Song show that extreme changes followed by moderate mean reversion cannot be produced by fully transitory or fully permanent shocks, and need non-Gaussian innovations in the persistent component plus a recurring, long-lasting but non-permanent "nonemployment" state. Before choosing among mechanisms, note that the program's record defines "returnable crossing" as a state (present below K, able to return), not as an observed return: the duration and return-time distribution of established-entity excursions has not been measured, and that measurement decides which half of the menu is even relevant.

#### Heavy-tailed and mixture persistent shocks
- Guvenen, Karahan, Ozkan & Song (GKOS): in US Social Security panel data, one-year log earnings changes have SD 0.51, skewness −1.07, kurtosis 14.93; five-year changes have SD 0.78, skewness −1.25, kurtosis 9.51 (staff-report version, revised Sept 2019; the published Econometrica version may differ in detail). — [GKOS, FRBNY Staff Report 710](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf); published as [Econometrica 89(5) 2021](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA14603)
- GKOS nonparametric impulse responses: for the median recent-earnings group, a +100 log-point change loses about half within 10 years, nearly all of that in the first year; a −100 log-point change recovers about 25% in year one and about 50% within 10 years. Small changes (under 10 log points) look very persistent while large ones mean-revert substantially. Persistence is sign- and position-dependent (for the top 1%, only 20–25% of declines revert in 10 years but about 80% of increases do; the reverse at the bottom). — [GKOS §4.1](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- GKOS state that these asymmetries are hard to see in a covariance matrix because one statistic pools large, small, positive and negative changes. — [GKOS introduction](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- GKOS benchmark process: AR(1) persistent component with two-normal mixture innovations; i.i.d. two-normal mixture transitory shock; heterogeneous income profiles; a nonemployment shock with logistic incidence in age and the persistent state and an exponentially distributed duration truncated at a full year. Estimated by targeting cross-sectional moments (SD, Kelley skewness, Crow–Siddiqui kurtosis of one- and five-year changes), impulse-response moments, lifecycle growth and the nonemployment CDF. — [GKOS §6.1](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- GKOS estimates: ρ = 0.958; with probability 21.9% the persistent innovation comes from N(−0.15, 0.46) and otherwise from N(0.041, 0.14), giving persistent-innovation skewness −1.10 and kurtosis about 8; full-year nonemployment risk falls from 17.8% per year in the bottom recent-earnings decile to 4.9% at the median and 0.6% in the top decile. — [GKOS §6.3](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- GKOS model-comparison lessons: (i) a uniform nonemployment risk acts as a large but fully transitory shock and barely improves the skewness fit; (ii) cross-sectional higher moments can be produced by either heterogeneous nonemployment risk or normal mixtures, but the nonlinear persistence in the impulse responses needs both; (iii) extreme changes followed by moderate mean reversion are not explained by fully transitory or permanent shocks, and nonemployment shocks with long-lasting but non-permanent effects fit better; (iv) a nonemployment shock can be mimicked by a mixture component with a very large negative mean and small variance; (v) in a two-AR(1) variant the components have persistence 0.98 and 0.82. — [GKOS §6.2–6.3](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- GKOS footnote on symmetry: any transitory shock (including a transitory nonemployment spell) cannot generate skewness in changes, because each spell contributes one negative and one equal positive change, stretching both tails. — [GKOS footnote 4](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- GKOS on the supply margin: in PSID data, wage changes are at least as important as hours changes even in the tails (an earnings decline of about 165 log points comes with an average wage decline of about 101 log points), and extreme changes coincide with nonemployment, job or occupation change, or health shocks. — [GKOS introduction](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- Barndorff-Nielsen & Shephard (BNS): an OU process driven by a Lévy process with positive increments moves up entirely by jumps and then decays exponentially; if square-integrable its autocorrelation is exp(−λ|u|) whatever the driving Lévy process; superposing independent OU processes with different rates gives an autocorrelation that is a weighted sum of exponentials, which they need (four components) to fit empirical autocorrelations with a steep initial decay and a slow tail. — [BNS, Nuffield WP version of JRSS-B 63:167–241](https://www.nuff.ox.ac.uk/economics/papers/1999/w9/read.pdf); [journal record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jorssb/v63y2001i2p167-241.html)
- Superpositions of OU-type processes keep jumps and can generate long memory, whereas fractional OU-type processes have no jumps; a Gamma mixing distribution over reversion rates gives polynomially decaying autocovariance. — [Multivariate supOU processes, arXiv 1101.0068](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1101.0068); [Moment based estimation of supOU processes, arXiv 1305.1470](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1305.1470) (both known to me through search summaries of the full text, not read end to end)
- Arata: Laplace-distributed firm growth plus independent growth shocks implies, via infinite divisibility, a pure-jump (variance-gamma) Lévy process rather than Brownian accumulation of small shocks; for listed Japanese firms a positive shock above 3% arrives about once every two years and one of 8% about once a decade. The column does not examine how the growth distribution changes with horizon. — [Arata, VoxEU column](https://cepr.org/voxeu/columns/firm-growth-dynamics-importance-large-jumps); [JEDC 103:63–82, 2019](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jedc.2019.01.009)
- Arata, Miyakawa & Mori (2023) report that the growth path of high-growth firms is characterized by a single large jump rather than gradual increase. — [RIETI DP 23-E-087 summary](https://www.rieti.go.jp/en/publications/summary/23120008.html)
- Anderson, Branch, Cooper & Dulvy: across 609 animal population time series, heavy-tailed process noise ("black-swan" events) appears in about 4% of populations, and about 86% of those events are crashes; the authors recommend heavy-tailed, downward-skewed (skewed Student-t) process noise for forecasting. — [PNAS 114:3252–3257, 2017](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611525114)

#### Nonlinear persistence
- Arellano, Blundell & Bonhomme (ABB) define nonlinear persistence as the derivative of the conditional quantile function of the persistent component with respect to its lag, ρ(η_{t−1}, τ), so persistence of the earnings history depends on the rank τ of the new shock; their framework allows "“unusual” shocks to wipe out the memory of past shocks". — [ABB, CEMFI WP 1606](https://www.cemfi.es/ftp/wp/1606.pdf); published as [Econometrica 85(3) 2017](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA13795)
- ABB estimates: persistence of the persistent component is near 1 when high-earnings households receive good shocks or low-earnings households receive bad shocks, and falls to about 0.6–0.8 when bad shocks hit high earners or good shocks hit low earners (0.3–0.4 in a descriptive quantile autoregression on raw earnings, in both PSID and Norwegian register data). — [ABB §3 and §6](https://www.cemfi.es/ftp/wp/1606.pdf)
- ABB interpret a large negative shock to a high earner as cancelling the persistence of a good history (the previous history matters less afterwards), i.e. a level-resetting event rather than a reversible one. — [ABB introduction](https://www.cemfi.es/ftp/wp/1606.pdf)
- Koenker & Xiao's quantile autoregression lets autoregressive coefficients be monotone functions of a single uniform variable, so conditioning variables shift location, scale and shape of the conditional distribution; the companion unit-root QAR test has a non-standard limit and gains power under non-Gaussian disturbances. — [Koenker & Xiao 2006, JASA](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000000672); [Koenker & Xiao 2004](https://experts.illinois.edu/en/publications/unit-root-quantile-autoregression-inference/)

#### Regime switching, level shifts and the long-memory confusion
- Diebold & Inoue: a mixture (level-shift) model with break probability p = O(T^(2d−2)) behaves as I(d) in the variance-of-partial-sums sense; a two-state Markov-switching mean with staying probabilities 1 − c·T^(−δ) is I(min(δ0, δ1)); with fixed transition probabilities the Markov-switching model is I(0) but can still show high persistence at short lags. They argue that in such settings "structural change" and "long memory" are two labels for one phenomenon and that long memory can remain a useful shorthand for prediction. — [Diebold & Inoue, PIER WP 01-006](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/filevault/working-papers/01-006.pdf); [NBER technical WP 264](https://nber.org/papers/t0264)
- The same paper recalls two other routes to long memory: cross-sectional aggregation of AR(1) units with heterogeneous coefficients (Granger 1980), and aggregation of on/off sources with heavy-tailed on and off durations (Mandelbrot, Taqqu), plus Parke's error-duration model where long memory arises when shock durations have infinite variance. — [Diebold & Inoue §2](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/sites/default/files/filevault/working-papers/01-006.pdf)
- Granger & Hyung: occasional breaks and long memory are hard to tell apart in absolute S&P 500 returns. — [J. Empirical Finance 11(3):399–421](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v11y2004i3p399-421.html)
- Perron & Qu: under a simple mixture model for level shifts they derive the behaviour of the autocorrelation function, periodogram and log-periodogram estimate of d, find that log-squared returns (including daily S&P 500, 1928–2002) match short memory plus level shifts rather than fractional integration, and propose a simple test. — [Perron & Qu, BU WP 2008-004 / JBES 28(2):275–290](https://ideas.repec.org/p/bos/wpaper/wp2008-004.html)
- On–off intermittency: near the onset threshold the distribution of quiescent ("laminar") phase durations follows a −3/2 power law over several decades; away from threshold the histogram tends toward an exponential shape. — [Heagy, Platt & Hammel 1994, PRE 49:1140 (record only; full text not read)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/9961322); [electroconvection experiment, arXiv patt-sol/9902004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/patt-sol/9902004); [APS DPP 2016 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dpp/2016/up10/9/)

#### Volatility clustering and aggregation
- Aggregational Gaussianity is a stylized fact of asset returns: the return distribution moves toward Gaussian as the horizon lengthens while volatility (absolute or squared returns) stays positively autocorrelated with slow decay. — [Cont 2001, Quantitative Finance 1(2):223–236](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v1y2001i2p223-236.html)
- A 2023 re-test on Dow 30 intraday data finds one-minute kurtosis between about 10 and above 1,000, falling with horizon but remaining above the Gaussian benchmark at 60 minutes. — [Revisiting Cont's stylized facts](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.07738v2)
- An index example of the horizon profile: kurtosis 8.38 daily, 5.71 weekly, 5.29 monthly, 2.58 quarterly. — [Mexican IPC stylized facts](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1412.3126)
- Drost & Nijman: GARCH is not closed under temporal aggregation in its strong form; weak GARCH is, and the low-frequency parameters depend on the high-frequency kurtosis. — [Econometrica 61(4):909–927](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/1993/07/01/temporal-aggregation-garch-processes)
- Borland & Bouchaud's multi-timescale feedback volatility model reproduces long-memory volatility and slow convergence of returns to Gaussian. — [arXiv physics/0507073](https://arxiv.org/abs/physics/0507073)

#### Jump tests
- BNS (2006): compare realized variance with realized bipower variation; under infill asymptotics the difference isolates the jump contribution, and most detected exchange-rate jumps coincide with macro announcements. — [BNS, testing for jumps with bipower variation](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~ofrcinfo/pages_fineconpapers/2004fe01.htm)
- Aït-Sahalia & Jacod (2009): a ratio of higher-power variations at two sampling frequencies converges to 1 if jumps are present and to a known constant otherwise, for all Itô semimartingales, with no coefficient estimates needed. — [Annals of Statistics 37(1):184–222](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.aos/1232115932)

#### Cross-sectional dependence
- Bai (2009): interactive fixed effects (unit-specific loadings on unobserved common factors) for large N and T; two-way fixed effects are inconsistent when regressors correlate with the interactive term. — [Econometrica 77(4):1229–1279](https://econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/2009/07/01/panel-data-models-interactive-fixed-effects)
- Pesaran (2015): the CD statistic (average pairwise residual correlation) has as implicit null weak cross-sectional dependence, with the admissible exponent of dependence depending on the relative growth of N and T; Monte Carlo size is correct for exponents in [0, 1/4] absent major error asymmetry. — [Pesaran, Testing weak cross-sectional dependence in large panels (IZA DP 6432)](https://www.iza.org/en/publications/dp/6432); [recent LM-type alternatives](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.14387)
- Bae, Karolyi & Stulz: count "co-exceedances" (joint occurrences of extreme returns on the same day) and explain the counts with multinomial logit; motivated by the fact that correlation-based contagion measures are distorted by heteroskedasticity. — [NBER WP 7913 / RFS 2003](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7913)

#### Self-excitation and attention bursts
- Crane & Sornette: about 90% of roughly five million YouTube videos are low-activity or Poisson-like; the bursty 10% relax as power laws whose exponents cluster in classes predicted by a self-excited model with memory exponent θ = 0.4 ± 0.1: exogenous subcritical 1+θ ≈ 1.4, exogenous critical 1−θ ≈ 0.6, endogenous critical 1−2θ ≈ 0.2, with precursory growth before endogenous peaks. Data are daily view counts. — [PNAS 105:15649–15653](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2572957)
- Matsubara et al. (SpikeM): a seven-parameter rise-and-fall model with a power-law fall (influence exponent fixed at −1.5) and explicit periodicity parameters (period, phase, strength), with weekly or yearly cycles noted as extensions. — [SpikeM, KDD 2012](https://www.dm.sanken.osaka-u.ac.jp/~yasushi/publications/spikeM.pdf)
- Rizoiu et al.: a Hawkes intensity process linking exogenous promotion to endogenous response, parameterized by sensitivity to promotion and inherent virality; reduces forecast error by 28.6% against history-only methods on heavily tweeted videos. — [arXiv 1602.06033 (WWW 2017)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.06033)
- Ratkiewicz et al.: Wikipedia and a national web space show popularity bursts with fat-tailed magnitude and inter-event time distributions; a minimal model adds random exogenous popularity shifts to preferential growth. — [PRL 105:158701](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.2704)

#### Mobility matrices and heterogeneity
- Blumen, Kogan & McCarthy's mover–stayer observation: observed multi-step transition matrices have larger diagonals than powers of the one-step matrix predict, which heterogeneity formulations explain; the Anderson–Goodman chi-square tests time-homogeneity, but a simulation study found its size and power poor for the homogeneity test. — secondary sources: [lecture notes on social-mobility models](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jmontgom/socialmobility.pdf); [Tan & Yılmaz 2002](https://research.hub.ku.edu.tr/entities/publication/be38ecd8-6d81-4861-98f9-90ea31a158a2/full) (the 1955 and 1957 originals were not read)
- Shorrocks' mobility index uses only the diagonal of the transition matrix, (n − trace)/(n − 1). — [Shorrocks 1978](https://www.econometricsociety.org/publications/econometrica/1978/09/01/measurement-mobility)

#### Absence and return estimands
- Estimating a never-return ("cure") fraction requires follow-up long enough that returns after the end of observation are improbable; classical sufficient-follow-up tests have poor size, and a 2024 proposal tests a relaxed, quantile-based notion with a shape-constrained density estimator and bootstrap. — [Yuen & Musta, arXiv 2403.16832](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.16832)
- Pushshift Reddit dumps for 2005 to mid-2017 contain documented gaps (comments and submissions missing in unpredictable places), with the highest risk for analyses that follow units over time. — [Gaffney & Matias 2018, PLoS ONE](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6034852)

### Inferences

Each item says how the mechanism maps onto this model and what would differ from an already-refuted fix. These are my inferences unless a source is named.

- **Reframing forced by the event definition.** A cohort member is "established" by train-window permanent rank (≤ K/2 or K/4) and "departs" by a one-week in→out transition at rank K during a scoring window that starts after the train window. Three different things produce such events: (i) reversible excursions from an intact home (jumps, regimes, seasonality); (ii) flicker by entities whose current level has drifted to the neighbourhood of rank K since the cohort was defined (slow dispersion, lifecycle, level shifts, era change); (iii) one-way declines that stay present below K. "≥90–98% returnable" (crossing rather than absence) does not separate them. Reading the deficit as "rare, large, persistent-but-reversible moves" is therefore a hypothesis until excursion durations and cohort drift are measured. This matters because (i) calls for a non-Gaussian persistent or regime component, while (ii) and (iii) call for more long-horizon dispersion of homes, which is fact-sheet residual 3 and the "excess low-frequency structure" already on record.
- **M1, heavy-tailed or jump innovations in the existing home (Lévy-driven OU; GKOS persistent mixture; ecology's t process noise).** Second moments are untouched if variance is held fixed, so every γ_k and D(h) moment the program fits is blind to it (BNS: same exponential autocorrelation for any driving Lévy process); only higher-order or path statistics can license it. Different from the refuted "heavier fast tails": the tail mass lives at the home's timescale, so a large move persists for about 1/κ rather than reverting at rate φ. Parameter cost can be zero (reuse the fast-channel df for home innovations, a restriction in the spirit of b = 1) or one. Risk: symmetric heavy home tails add upward excursions too, which would aggravate the FB top-1 overshoot (residual 2); a one-sided law costs an asymmetry parameter.
- **M2, a separate medium-lived fat-tailed component.** GKOS's two-AR(1) variant (0.98 and 0.82) is this. In M0 it means making the existing optional ξ2 non-Gaussian. Caveat from residual 5: sim VR13 already exceeds empirical, so any added medium-horizon variance must be offset by removing Gaussian variance elsewhere; a shape-only swap at fixed variance is the freedom-removing version.
- **M3, a reversible two-state activity regime (GKOS nonemployment shock; Markov or semi-Markov; scheduled or seasonal variant).** This is the mechanism GKOS found indispensable for extreme changes with moderate mean reversion, and the one whose incidence falls steeply with position (0.6% per year at the top decile vs 17.8% at the bottom), mirroring a rank-dependent hazard. It is naturally one-sided for established entities (there is no symmetric "super-active" state), so it raises departures without adding upward head transits, and by GKOS's symmetry argument it leaves one-week skew near zero, which is what the program measured at the head. It also absorbs the estimand fix: the "off" state is temporary absence or depressed activity with the home retained. Cost: two hazards (onset, return), both directly measurable from an excursion catalogue; depth is free only if the off state is observed (zero or near-zero posts).
- **M4, entity-level stochastic volatility.** Different from the refuted common volatility factor because it is idiosyncratic. On the brief's question: time-varying entity volatility does produce bursts of large moves and fat unconditional per-entity tails, but it does not leave the per-entity kurtosis moment untouched. One-week kurtosis is exactly what it raises, so with the df already identified from within-entity kurtosis, SV can only substitute for part of the t-kurtosis. What changes is the temporal arrangement (clustered rather than isolated large moves) and the horizon profile (tails persist to the volatility memory). It raises established departures only if the volatility state scales the persistent innovations as well (a dynamic version of b = 1); if it scales only the fast channel it produces more flicker, which the sim already over-produces.
- **M5, nonlinear persistence (ABB, QAR, threshold models).** The ABB sign is the wrong one for a reversible story: a bad shock to a high-position unit erases its history, which predicts low return probability. If crossing excursions do return, the relevant nonlinearity is GKOS's "large changes are more transitory than small ones", which a linear mixture of components with different tail weights and persistence reproduces without any state-dependent coefficient. ABB's apparatus is best used as a measurement (persistence by initial position × shock quantile), not as a generative fix: its flexible quantile model has many coefficients.
- **M6, long memory, non-reverting level shifts, lifecycle.** A Gaussian fractional process changes second moments only and cannot produce fat per-entity tails, so it is a candidate for residual 5 and for cohort drift, not for a tail deficit. Per Diebold–Inoue, rare level shifts and long memory are observationally close in second moments; what separates them is non-Gaussianity of long differences and the bandwidth profile of d (Perron–Qu). Different from the refuted constant-drift lifecycle demeaning: stochastic, entity-specific, non-monotone shifts rather than a deterministic drift.
- **M7, cross-sectional block shocks.** With only one common factor removed at unit loading, heterogeneous loadings or community factors remain in the "idiosyncratic" components. Holding each entity's marginal law fixed, moving variance from idiosyncratic to block form should not raise the mean departure rate: for an entity against a fixed threshold the marginal law is unchanged, and when a block containing many near-threshold entities moves together the threshold moves with them, protecting relative ranks. What block shocks change is the dispersion of weekly departure counts and the validity of entity-bootstrap bands. A block shock raises the mean rate only if it is itself heavy-tailed or regime-like for a subset (for example an algorithm change that demotes one category), in which case it is M1 or M3 with common timing.
- **M8, self-excited bursts and rise-and-fall episodes.** Bursts are upward and time-irreversible (fast rise, slow power-law fall), so they are the wrong sign for own-activity drops of established entities. They matter for the departure statistic in one way: an entity whose train-window permanent rank reflects a long burst episode is "established" by construction and departs when the episode ends (case iii above). On weekly data with T ≤ 213, a relaxation t^(−0.6) falls to about 0.21 by week 13 and 0.09 by week 52, which a sum of two or three exponentials reproduces closely (BNS's superposition point), so power-law relaxation is not separately identifiable from stacked OU components at this resolution; the exogenous-subcritical class (exponent about 1.4) is gone within a week or two and belongs to the fast channel.
- **M9, artefacts and non-behavioural events.** Instrument gaps, moderation events (a community going private, restricted or quarantined) and construction choices (unfrozen universe letting post-train entrants push incumbents down; era change between train and scoring windows) all generate large reversible drops or extra exits. By the program's own ordering (identification failure, then measurement artefact, then noise, then structure) these are cheap to rule out first. The in-sample permanent-rank cohort result (§2z-z, about 2× with same-era parameters) suggests era change is not the whole deficit, but that design carried survival conditioning.
- **M10, estimand fix alone.** Retaining identity and home through an absence (rebirth reserved for genuinely new ids) converts simulated deaths into returnable states at zero parameter cost. It addresses composition (14–52% deaths) and the shell return deficit, not the 2.3–3.4× count deficit.

### Gaps

The supply-side explanation is therefore unsupported by external literature here and has to be measured in-panel.

## 2. Which observable signatures discriminate among the mechanisms in a panel like ours?

### Takeaway
Second moments cannot discriminate (a jump-driven home has the same autocovariances as a Gaussian one), so the licence has to come from four families of statistics that the program has not yet recorded: how tail thickness scales with horizon, how persistence depends on the size and sign of a move, the shape and duration law of excursions, and whether large moves cluster in time, sign, calendar or community. The single most diagnostic contrast is recovery shape plus the depth–duration relation, which separates jump-and-relax from switch-off-and-on.

### Cited Findings
- Tail thickness need not thin at the Gaussian rate: GKOS kurtosis is 14.93 at one year and still 9.51 at five years, and they target quantile-based measures (Kelley skewness, Crow–Siddiqui kurtosis) because fourth moments are fragile. — [GKOS Figure 1 and §3](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- Persistence by size and sign is measured nonparametrically by plotting the average change from t to t+k against the size of the change from t−1 to t, within groups defined by prior position. — [GKOS §4.1](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- Persistence by position × shock rank is measured by the derivative of conditional quantile functions (series quantile regression with Hermite polynomials for the descriptive version). — [ABB §2–3](https://www.cemfi.es/ftp/wp/1606.pdf)
- Reversible spells generate paired, equal and opposite changes and therefore symmetric change distributions. — [GKOS footnote 4](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)
- A jump-driven OU path shows abrupt moves followed by exponential decay with the same exponential autocorrelation as a Gaussian OU. — [BNS](https://www.nuff.ox.ac.uk/economics/papers/1999/w9/read.pdf)
- The average shape of an excursion (signal between departure from and first return to a level) obeys a scaling form whose function is nearly independent of the increment distribution but sensitive to temporal correlation; for uncorrelated walks it is a semicircle, and linear damping destroys the scaling and flattens long excursions. — [Baldassarri, Colaiori & Castellano, PRL 90:060601](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0301068); [Colaiori et al. 2004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/cond-mat/0402285)
- Quiescent-phase durations: −3/2 power law at the on–off intermittency threshold, exponential away from it. — [electroconvection experiment, arXiv patt-sol/9902004](https://arxiv.org/pdf/patt-sol/9902004); [APS DPP 2016 abstract](https://meetings-archive.aps.org/dpp/2016/up10/9/)
- Level shifts versus long memory: the estimate of d from the log-periodogram, the autocorrelation function and the periodogram behave differently under short memory plus level shifts than under fractional integration. — [Perron & Qu](https://ideas.repec.org/p/bos/wpaper/wp2008-004.html)
- Jump functionals: realized variance minus bipower variation, and ratios of higher-power variations across sampling frequencies; both justified by infill asymptotics. — [BNS jump test](https://users.ox.ac.uk/~ofrcinfo/pages_fineconpapers/2004fe01.htm); [Aït-Sahalia & Jacod](https://projecteuclid.org/euclid.aos/1232115932)
- Calendar clustering of extremes: co-exceedance counts; residual dependence: CD statistic. — [Bae, Karolyi & Stulz](https://www.nber.org/papers/w7913); [Pesaran](https://www.iza.org/en/publications/dp/6432)
- Multi-step mobility: observed h-step diagonals above the h-th power of the one-step matrix signal heterogeneity or memory. — [mobility-model notes](https://users.ssc.wisc.edu/~jmontgom/socialmobility.pdf)
- Burst relaxation classes and precursory growth distinguish endogenous from exogenous bursts on daily data. — [Crane & Sornette](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2572957)
- Volatility clustering is diagnosed by slowly decaying autocorrelation of absolute or squared changes with near-zero autocorrelation of signed changes; a high-volatility cluster contains large moves of both signs. — [Cont 2001](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v1y2001i2p223-236.html)

### Inferences

Derivations are mine (standard cumulant and first-passage arguments) and should be checked by simulation on M0 before use.

- **S1, tail thickness versus horizon (cumulant-scaling test).** For independent components, cumulants add: c4[Δ_h X] = c4[Δ_h home] + c4[Δ_h fast] + c4[Δ_h noise]. In M0 only the fast channel is non-Gaussian and its difference saturates within a few weeks, so the fourth cumulant of Δ_h X should be flat in h once φ^h ≈ 0 and kurtosis should fall like 1/Var(Δ_h)². Three alternatives give three different scalings against V(h) = Var(Δ_h X): fast-only non-Gaussianity gives c4 ∝ V⁰; independent-increment (jump or regime) non-Gaussianity in a persistent component gives c4 ∝ h up to that component's relaxation time and a plateau after it (kurtosis falls like 1/h, then stops falling); a scale mixture that persists across the horizon (stochastic volatility with long memory, or time-varying amplitude) gives c4 ∝ V² (kurtosis flat in h). For a compound-Poisson-driven OU with jump rate λ and reversion κ, my calculation gives a long-horizon excess-kurtosis floor of (κ/λ) × (jump kurtosis)/2: rare jumps relative to the reversion rate leave long differences permanently non-Gaussian. For a two-state level regime with small occupancy, c4 ≈ 2p(h)d⁴ with p(h) ∝ 1 − exp(−h/dwell): the same linear-then-plateau form with the dwell time as the scale. Because the fitted t df may be near 4 (where fourth moments diverge), the practical statistic is a quantile ratio (the program's q99/q90, Crow–Siddiqui) computed at h = 1, 2, 4, 8, 13, 26, 52 for data and sim, and read as the emp-minus-sim gap. Illustration from GKOS: if annual changes were independent increments, excess kurtosis at five years would be about 11.9/5 ≈ 2.4 (kurtosis ≈ 5.4) against the observed 9.51.
- **S2, skewness of changes versus skewness of level deviations.** Square-wave absences give symmetric one-week changes but strongly left-skewed, bimodal deviations of the level from the entity's own median. Jump-and-relax with downward jumps gives left-skewed one-week changes (one big drop, many small recoveries) that fade with h. Upward bursts give the mirror image. The measured near-zero one-week skew at the head is therefore mild evidence against sharp-drop/slow-recovery and is uninformative against regimes. This is what differs from the refuted skewed-innovation fix: the asymmetry to look for is in levels and in time-reversal, not in i.i.d. innovations.
- **S3, persistence after large versus small moves.** Define the retained fraction R(k | size, sign, band) = E[X_{t+k} − X_{t−1}] / E[X_t − X_{t−1}] within bins of the one-week change. M0 predicts large moves are less persistent than small ones, reverting at rate φ, because only the fast channel has fat tails. A jump-driven home predicts large moves retained at exp(−κk), so persistence becomes U-shaped in size. A medium jump component predicts reversion on its own timescale. A regime predicts R(k) equal to the survival function of the dwell time, so the averaged curve cannot distinguish a regime from a medium jump component; path-level shape (S6) is needed. If volatility scales all components, R is independent of size.
- **S4, clustering of |Δ| and the signed-pair statistic.** Autocorrelation of |Δ_1| within entity (lags 1–26, emp vs sim) detects stochastic volatility. Among pairs of large moves by the same entity within L weeks, the share with opposite sign is about one half under stochastic volatility, near one under a regime (drop then matching rise), and pairs are simply rare under isolated jumps with gradual recovery. Across entities, correlation of |Δ| within communities tests block volatility.
- **S5, excursion duration law and the depth–duration relation.** After a jump to depth d below home, an OU relaxation crosses back over a margin c after about (1/κ)·ln(d/c): duration rises with the log of depth, with slope 1/κ, where κ is the already-fitted home rate (M1) or a separate faster rate (M2). A Markov regime gives geometric durations independent of depth and a flat return hazard; a semi-Markov or scheduled regime gives peaked or multimodal durations and calendar alignment. Purely diffusive excursions (M0 with more persistent variance, or a near-unit-root home) give a −3/2 power law cut off near 1/κ, the same law the intermittency literature reports at threshold. A mixture of very short and long durations (bimodal) indicates fast flicker plus a regime. Never-return shares are right-censored; with at most 77 scoring weeks the follow-up is short relative to any slow κ, so a "cure fraction" should be reported with the sufficient-follow-up caveat.
- **S6, onset and recovery shape.** "Lumpiness" = largest single weekly step during recovery divided by excursion depth (same for onset). Regimes and outages: near 1 on both sides and time-reversible. Jump-and-relax: near 1 at onset, about 1 − exp(−κ) per week in recovery, time-irreversible. Diffusive or volatility-driven excursions: low on both sides. Bursts: irreversible on the up side. A third-moment time-reversal statistic gives the same information in one number; the daily panels can resolve whether an onset happens within a day.
- **S7, calendar and community co-occurrence.** Variance-to-mean ratio of weekly onset counts against the M0 sim (week-block bootstrap); concentration of an entity's onsets on the same weeks of the year; per-entity spectral power at 52 weeks and D(26) versus D(52); co-exceedance within community labels. Idiosyncratic jump or regime mechanisms predict near-Poisson counts; block factors predict overdispersion within communities; instrument artefacts predict overdispersion across unrelated communities; scheduled hiatuses predict annual periodicity.
- **S8, supply versus per-item engagement.** Where both counts exist, split each excursion's log drop into Δlog(items posted) and Δlog(engagement per item). A supply hiatus loads on item count (possibly to zero); demotion or audience loss loads on per-item engagement with posting intact. This has less bite on Reddit, where posting volume is itself an audience outcome, than on single-owner pages. GKOS's PSID result is a caution that the supply margin need not dominate even in the tails.
- **S9, long-horizon dispersion and memory.** D(h) for h beyond the fitted maximum of 52 weeks on the established cohort, and the dispersion of scoring-window median rank around train permanent rank. If empirical dispersion keeps growing where the sim saturates, the deficit is at least partly cohort drift (M6), and the level-shift-versus-long-memory question is settled by whether long differences are non-Gaussian and whether pooled log-periodogram estimates of d move with bandwidth.
- **Mobility matrices and Shorrocks indices.** These mostly discriminate rank-only Markov models from latent-home models, which the program has already done in other language. Two cells are informative for this residual: the far-off-diagonal mass (established class to outside K) by horizon, and the two-time probability of being outside K at both t and t+h for cohort members, which is the excursion-duration information in transition-matrix form and uses the event vocabulary already in `exit_audit.py`. Formal time-homogeneity tests are likely to reject for era reasons alone and have documented power problems, so they add little.
- **Jump tests.** Infill asymptotics do not apply at weekly frequency with 88–213 points, and M0 is itself non-Gaussian in discrete time, so the tests' critical values are not usable. The functionals are still useful as descriptive statistics compared against M0: the bipower-to-realized-variance ratio per entity, and the ratio of fourth-power variation at two sampling intervals, which is the cumulant-scaling test of S1 under another name. Daily data add day-resolution onset shape but carry the Spec-B noise floor and day-of-week structure.

### Gaps
- I did not find a source giving closed-form horizon profiles of kurtosis for Lévy-driven OU increments or for two-state regimes; the scalings above are my derivations.
- The first-return exponent for long-memory (fractional) processes and the exact OU return-time law were not verified against a source.
- No source was found that adapts bipower or power-variation jump tests to low-frequency panels with measurement noise; the adaptation is a proposal.
- Whether the program's q99/q90 statistic is computed on positive changes only, and at which horizons it has been run on sim panels, should be read from `anatomy_measure.py`; I inferred a one-week, positive-side construction from the recorded values.

## 3. Synthesis: (a) mechanism × signature table and (b) measure-first plan

### Takeaway
A nine-step (0–8), model-free measurement sequence on the existing dev panels can separate the candidates before any model code is written; the first step (decompose the existing exit events into excursions from intact homes versus drift of the cohort toward the cut) costs almost nothing and determines whether a reversible large-displacement component is the right family at all. If it is, the cheapest licensed fixes are the zero-parameter estimand correction plus either a shape-only change to home innovations with a shared df (zero or one parameter) or a two-hazard reversible regime (two parameters, both read directly off the excursion catalogue).

### Cited Findings
- The pairing of non-Gaussian persistent innovations with a rank-dependent, long-lasting but non-permanent off state is what a sequence of incrementally richer specifications estimated on administrative data selected, and each piece was tied to a named moment set (cross-sectional higher moments, impulse responses, spell incidence). — [GKOS §6.2](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr710.pdf)

- The program's rules that bind the plan: measure before coding; suspect identification failure and measurement artefact before missing structure; prefer fixes that remove freedom; a new latent component needs a moment it alone can produce; identical event definitions on both panels; Wikipedia is never used to choose between candidates. — `.claude/skills/stochastic-modeling/SKILL.md` §2, §5; `llm_fitting/MODEL_STATUS.md` §2z-z

#### (b) Measure-first plan (ordered; cheapest and most decisive first)

General conditions for every step: dev panels only (FB Era A, Reddit comments and submissions); cohort by train-window absence-penalized permanent rank with the frozen train-only universe; identical code path on empirical and M0-simulated panels (≥ 20–30 seeds, common random numbers, entity and week-block bootstrap); predictions written down before running; results appended as findings whichever way they fall; Wikipedia untouched.

0. **Decompose the existing exit events (no new statistics needed).** For each P7/§2z-ac event record the entity's margin to the cut the week before, its scoring-window median rank versus train permanent rank, and whether the exit is the first of an episode or a re-exit within a few weeks. Report, for data and sim: episodes per cohort-week, exits per episode, share of exits from "drifters" (scoring-window median near or below rank K), share from "excursionists" (median well inside), share one-way. Add a within-train split (cohort on the first half of the train window, scored on the second) to remove era change, and confirm that post-train entrants cannot push incumbents (frozen membership). *Licenses:* if the missing events are mostly drifter flicker or one-way declines, the residual is long-horizon under-dispersion of homes (go to step 7; M6 family; ties to residual 3) and no reversible-jump component is licensed. If they are mostly distinct excursions from intact homes, continue. If the deficit shrinks materially in the within-train split, part of it is parameter non-stationarity.
1. **Excursion catalogue.** For excursionists: depth (shortfall from own pre-event 13-week median, in log units and in entity-SD units), duration to first re-entry (Kaplan–Meier, right-censored), return hazard by elapsed weeks, depth–duration regression, onset and recovery lumpiness, time-reversal asymmetry. *Licenses:* duration rising with log depth at slope near the fitted 1/κ, gradual recovery → M1 (no new timescale). Same shape with a faster slope → M2. Duration independent of depth, flat hazard, lumpy recovery → M3. Peaked durations → M3s (go to step 6). Short diffusive excursions with −3/2-like law → no new component; the issue is variance allocation or threshold geometry.
2. **Horizon profile of tails.** Downside and upside q99/q90 (and Crow–Siddiqui, Kelley) of per-entity standardized Δ_h at h = 1, 2, 4, 8, 13, 26, 52 for the established cohort and for the strata already used in §2z-x. *Licenses:* gap gone by h ≈ 4 → the excess tail is fast-shaped; nothing persistent is licensed (consider M4f or allocation). Gap growing roughly linearly and then flat → non-Gaussian persistent or regime component, with the plateau horizon giving its timescale. Gap flat from h = 1 → scale mixing over time (M4, or time-varying amplitude, which also connects to the s trend in residual 3). Downside-only gap → one-sided mechanism (M3, or one-sided M1).
3. **Persistence by size and sign.** Retained-fraction curves R(k | size bin, sign, permanent-rank band), k = 1–26, GKOS-style; optionally quantile-autoregression slopes by position × shock quantile. *Licenses:* empirical large moves retained longer than simulated at k = 4–13 → a medium- or long-lived large-move channel exists (M1, M2 or M3; steps 1 and 4 choose). Indistinguishable from sim → no new dynamics licensed by this route. Retention near 1 with no decay → M5/M6 (history reset).
4. **Clustering and signed pairs.** Autocorrelation of |Δ_1| within entity; opposite-sign share among paired large moves by lag; within-community correlation of |Δ|. *Licenses:* slow |Δ| autocorrelation with sign share near one half → M4 (then check with step 2 whether it reaches the persistent component). Sign share near one at a characteristic lag → M3. Neither → isolated jumps (M1/M2).
5. **Supply decomposition.** For catalogued excursions, the share of the log drop carried by item count versus per-item engagement, and the frequency of zero-item weeks. *Licenses:* supply-dominated drops → M3 as an activity regime whose off-state depth is observed rather than estimated. Engagement-dominated drops with posting intact → demand or algorithmic shock (M1/M2, or M7 if clustered).
6. **Calendar and community structure.** Variance-to-mean ratio of weekly onset counts versus sim; week-of-year concentration per entity; per-entity annual spectral power and D(26) versus D(52); co-exceedance within community labels; residual CD. *Licenses:* annual periodicity → a deterministic, measured seasonal profile (no stochastic parameters) removed before estimation, which may also relieve κ identification from long differences. Community overdispersion → block factor or community-week demeaning; this changes bands and the effective sample size but not, by itself, the mean rate. Cross-community clustering on specific dates → artefact (M9); fix the data, not the model.
7. **Long-horizon dispersion.** D(h) for h = 52 up to what T allows on the established cohort, dispersion of scoring-window median rank around train permanent rank, non-Gaussianity of the longest differences, and the bandwidth profile of pooled log-periodogram d. *Licenses:* empirical dispersion still growing where sim saturates, Gaussian long differences → slower or fractional home (a change that can remove parameters if one memory exponent replaces κ plus the medium component). Growing with fat-tailed long differences → rare level shifts. Saturating as in sim → drift is not the explanation.
8. **Daily-resolution checks (optional).** Day-level onset shape for catalogued excursions; bipower-to-realized-variance and fourth-power-variation ratios as descriptive functionals, low-count days guarded. *Licenses:* within-day onsets support regimes or artefacts over relaxations; otherwise confirmatory only.
