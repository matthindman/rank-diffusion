**What the current design is (read from the repo, so the critique is aimed at the right target).** Gate v1 scores a 7-element pooled vector (median |dRank| at h=1/4/13, RACF1, coll1/5/20) by mean relative error with a denominator floor (MOM_FLOOR=0.02, moments with |emp| below it are excluded), against the train-window movement distribution asserted for the test window ("historical mobility", called persistence). ## 1. Proper scoring rules for our targets; scoring a population-distribution prediction vs per-entity predictive distributions; is "relative error of pooled moments" defensible as a primary?

### Takeaway
The pooled-moment gate is a legitimate check of *marginal calibration of the population movement law* on a few functionals, but it is not a proper score of a forecast, it cannot reward conditional (entity-level) information, and it is blind to tails, direction, exits and joint dependence. The principled population-level version is a proper divergence (integrated quadratic distance) or a proper score on the model's predictive distribution of each pooled statistic; the principled entity-level version is CRPS/RPS on rank with a censored exit state, plus Brier/log scores for top-k events and threshold-weighted variants for the head.

### Cited Findings
- A scoring rule is proper if the expected score is optimised by the true distribution and strictly proper if uniquely so. CRPS, the logarithmic score, the energy score and the interval (Winkler) score are the standard instances, and CRPS equals the integral of Brier scores over all thresholds. `[M]` — [Gneiting & Raftery 2007, JASA 102:359–378](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437)
- Point forecasts must be scored with a scoring function that is consistent for the functional requested (absolute error for the median, pinball loss for quantiles). Under absolute *percentage* error the optimal point forecast is not the median but a "median of order −1" of the predictive distribution, i.e. a value biased below the median. `[M]` — [Gneiting 2011, JASA 106:746–762](https://doi.org/10.1198/jasa.2011.r10138)
- Scaling errors by an in-sample naive error (MASE) avoids the undefined or exploding ratios that percentage errors give when the realised value is near zero. `[M]` — [Hyndman & Koehler 2006, IJF 22:679–688](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2006.03.001)
- Climate models are evaluated by comparing the probability distribution of model output with the distribution of observations. The divergence used should be *proper*; score divergences derived from proper scoring rules are proper, with the integrated quadratic distance (IQD, the CRPS divergence) and Kullback–Leibler singled out, and several commonly used divergences fail to be proper. `[V]` — [Thorarinsdottir, Gneiting & Gissibl 2013, SIAM/ASA JUQ 1:522–534](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.5927)
- For models that can only be simulated, a proper score can be built from an estimator of a summary statistic: propriety is inherited under a measurable transformation of the data, the score is computed by averaging over simulated realisations, and it is sensitive only to errors in the chosen statistic. `[V]` — [Heinrich-Mertsching, Thorarinsdottir, Guttorp & Schneider 2024, Scand. J. Stat. 51:1533–1566](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.11803)
- Restricting evaluation to cases selected on the *realised outcome* (e.g. only extreme observations) is incompatible with the assumptions of proper scoring and discredits skilful forecasts ("forecaster's dilemma"); proper weighted scoring rules are the decision-theoretically justified alternative. `[M]` — [Lerch, Thorarinsdottir, Ravazzolo & Gneiting 2017, Statistical Science 32:106–127](https://doi.org/10.1214/16-STS588)
- Threshold-weighted and quantile-weighted CRPS put emphasis on regions of interest while staying proper; weighting the log score by the outcome region is improper. `[M]` — [Gneiting & Ranjan 2011, JBES 29:411–422](https://doi.org/10.1198/jbes.2010.08110)
- The threshold-weighted CRPS is a kernel score. That gives ensemble-ready formulas and threshold-weighted energy and variogram scores. Threshold weighting is implemented by passing forecasts and observations through a "chaining function"; for an indicator weight at t, v(x)=max(x,t) censors forecast and observation at t, akin to the censored likelihood score of Diks et al. `[V]` — [Allen, Ginsbourger & Ziegel 2023, SIAM/ASA JUQ 11:906–940](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.12732)
- Software: weighted scores (twCRPS, owCRPS, vrCRPS) are available for simulated/ensemble forecasts in R `scoringRules` and Python `scoringrules`. `[V]` — [Allen, "Weighted scoringRules", arXiv:2305.07312](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.07312); [scoringrules documentation](https://scoringrules.readthedocs.io/en/latest/weighted_scores.html)
- The standard ensemble CRPS is biased against small ensembles; "fair" scores correct for ensemble size so that an ensemble exchangeable with the observation is optimal. `[M]` — [Ferro 2014, QJRMS 140:1917–1923](https://doi.org/10.1002/qj.2270)
- The weighted interval score (WIS) approximates CRPS from a set of central prediction intervals and decomposes into dispersion, over-prediction and under-prediction penalties. `[M]` — [Bracher, Ray, Gneiting & Reich 2021, PLoS Comput. Biol. 17:e1008618](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008618)
- CRPS on the natural scale gives most weight to observations with large uncertainty, which can produce unintuitive rankings; a locally scale-invariant alternative (SCRPS) exists, and scoring after a log transform is recommended when relative errors matter. `[V/M]` — [Bolin & Wallin, "Local scale invariance and robustness of proper scoring rules", arXiv:1912.05642](https://arxiv.org/abs/1912.05642); [Bosse et al. 2023, PLoS Comput. Biol. 19:e1011393](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1011393)
- Multivariate scores: the variogram score was introduced because the energy score is weakly sensitive to misspecified correlations. `[M]` — [Scheuerer & Hamill 2015, MWR 143:1321–1334](https://doi.org/10.1175/MWR-D-14-00269.1)
- The literature on the energy score's discrimination ability conflicts: Alexander et al. find it discriminates worse than variogram scores (p=0.5 best in most settings); Ziel & Berk find it performs comparably well when paired with a Diebold–Mariano test and recommend it as the main multivariate measure. The variogram score is proper but not strictly proper. `[V]` — [Alexander, Coulon, Han & Meng, arXiv:2101.12693](https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.12693); contradicted in emphasis by [Ziel & Berk 2019, arXiv:1910.07325](https://arxiv.org/abs/1910.07325)
- Propriety does not guarantee discrimination at realistic sample sizes: a power analysis over dimension d, number of realisations n and forecast-sample size m, for five proper multivariate scores and 19 error types, "reveal[s] critical shortcomings" in common practice. `[V]` — [Marcotte, Zantedeschi, Drouin & Chapados 2023, ICML, PMLR 202:23958–24004](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v202/marcotte23a.html)
- When two forecasts are both imperfect (misspecified or based on different information), their ranking can depend on which consistent scoring function is used, even in population; Murphy diagrams check dominance across the whole class of consistent scoring functions. `[M]` — [Patton 2020, JBES 38:796–809](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.2019.1585256); [Ehm, Gneiting, Jordan & Krüger 2016, JRSS-B 78:505–562](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssb.12154)
- Ranking-valued forecasts: I found theory for probabilistic top-list predictions and for elicitability of rankings with ties, plus the ranked probability score for ordinal categories, but no canonical proper score for full-permutation forecasts. `[V for title and topic; author (Resin) recalled]` — ["From Classification Accuracy to Proper Scoring Rules: Elicitability of Probabilistic Top List Predictions", arXiv:2301.11797](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.11797)
- A 2025–2026 review covers characterisation results, the main families of proper scoring rules and their dual use in estimation and forecast evaluation. `[V]` — [Waghmare & Ziegel, arXiv:2504.01781 (v4, May 2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.01781)

### Inferences
- **What Gate v1 is, in scoring-rule terms.** It is a minimum-distance comparison of a few functionals of the pooled displacement distribution. By Heinrich-Mertsching et al., scoring through summary statistics can be made proper, but Gate v1 does not do that: it compares point values by relative error instead of scoring the model's *predictive distribution* of each statistic. Three specific weaknesses follow.
  1. *The loss is of APE type* (error divided by the realised value). By Gneiting (2011) such a loss is minimised by a forecast shaded below the centre of the predictive distribution of the realised statistic, so the gate mildly rewards under-prediction of noisy moments. Replacing `|sim−emp|/|emp|` with `|log(sim/emp)|` (consistent for the median of the statistic) or with a MASE-style scaling by the train-window split-to-split variability removes this.
  2. *MOM_FLOOR drops moments based on the realised value.* The composition of the score therefore depends on the outcome, which is the selection-on-outcome pattern Lerch et al. warn about. A fixed composition with an additive floor in the denominator (or the MASE-style scale) avoids it.
  3. *Medians are insensitive to the tail.* The program's own recorded p90 under-dispersion (e.g. 27→25, 41→35, 65→51 in the E2 readout) is invisible to a median-based gate.
- **Why "CRPS skill ≈ 0" may be uninformative as currently computed.** For any single common forecast CDF F scored against n outcomes with empirical CDF Ĝ, mean CRPS = ∫(F−Ĝ)²dx + ∫Ĝ(1−Ĝ)dx, i.e. IQD(F,Ĝ) plus an uncertainty term that is the same for every forecast. If the model and the baseline are both scored as *pooled* distributions (the record calls the PIT "pooled" and the CRPS "ensemble"), the skill score is (IQD_base − IQD_model)/(IQD_base + UNC), which is close to zero whenever both pooled distributions are roughly right, whatever their entity-level content. This follows from the threshold decomposition in Gneiting & Raftery and the CRPS divergence in Thorarinsdottir et al.; I did not inspect `--dist-scores` to confirm which case applies. If pooled, report IQD skill (1 − IQD_model/IQD_base) and do not read the near-zero CRPS skill as evidence about entity-level predictability.
- **Possible ensemble-size handicap.** For a calibrated m-member ensemble the standard ensemble CRPS is inflated by a factor (1 + 1/m) relative to the infinite-ensemble value (from the algebra behind Ferro 2014). At m=20 that is 5%. If each model forecast rests on 20 simulated draws while the baseline uses thousands of train-window displacements, the comparison is biased against the model by an amount larger than the reported skills (−0.004 to +0.005). Check ensemble sizes in the code and use fair CRPS or equalised sample sizes.
- **Principled population-level score (what the gate is trying to be).** (a) IQD between the model's pooled predictive displacement CDF and the realised one, by horizon and by permanent-rank stratum, with skill relative to the baseline's IQD. (b) For each pooled statistic S (median, p90, RACF, collision rates, top-k turnover), the model's seeds give a predictive distribution of S; score the realised S with CRPS and check its PIT across origins. The baseline's predictive distribution of S is its block-bootstrap distribution over train sub-windows. This is the proper analogue of "relative error of pooled moments".
- **Principled entity-level score.** Rank is bounded and integer, so CRPS on rank is an unnormalised ranked probability score and equals the sum over rank thresholds k of the Brier score for the event "rank at t+h ≤ k". This single object unifies the distributional score and the top-k event scores. Score on log-rank (a monotone transform keeps propriety) and average within pre-registered permanent-rank strata, then across strata with equal weight, so the many tail entities do not swamp the head. Use twCRPS with weight on head thresholds for the head claims.
- **Exits.** Survivor-conditional scoring is proper for the conditional law given survival, but it removes exactly the component where the model is known to be wrong (established-departure deficit 2.3–2.7x). Put exits into the outcome space as a censored state (rank := K+B+1, i.e. the chaining function v(x)=min(x, K+B+1) of Allen et al., or the censored-likelihood idea of Diks et al.). Because 95–98% of empirical absences are temporary, censoring is also the better description than death.
- **What is lost by pooled moments only.** Identity (who moves), conditional structure (resolution), tails, direction (|d| discards sign), exits, joint dependence across entities (common shocks), and calibration of the statistic's own uncertainty. What is kept: an interpretable, low-variance check that the model reproduces the population mobility law out of sample, which is the paper's goal-2 claim at population level.
- **Multivariate scores are not a workable primary here.** The joint outcome has thousands of dimensions and one realisation per origin; Marcotte et al. and Alexander et al. imply negligible power. Use low-dimensional functionals of the joint (point above), a variogram score on a pre-registered random subsample of within-band entity pairs to test co-movement, and energy scores on small random patches (d≈5–20) as descriptive items only.
- **Verdict on the brief's question.** "Relative error of pooled moments" is defensible as a *registered legacy primary for a population-level adequacy claim*, not as evidence of predictive skill. It should be supplemented by one proper population-level score and one proper entity-level score, both with pre-specified margins.

### Gaps
- No source found that defines a canonical proper score for permutation-valued (full ranking) forecasts; the notes above use marginal rank scores plus joint functionals instead.
- I did not verify how `--dist-scores` constructs the model and baseline predictive samples (pooled vs per-entity; ensemble sizes; fair vs standard CRPS). The two inferences that depend on it are flagged.
- Holzmann & Klar (2017, AoAS) on focusing scores on regions of interest and Diks, Panchenko & van Dijk (2011, J. Econometrics 163:215–230) on censored likelihood scores are cited from memory or second-hand.

## 2. Calibration diagnostics

### Takeaway
A flat pooled PIT histogram establishes at most probabilistic calibration on average; it does not establish conditional calibration and can hide offsetting errors across rank strata. State of the art is stratified coverage by horizon and stratum, CORP reliability diagrams with miscalibration/discrimination/uncertainty decompositions, and tests that respect dependence or are sequentially valid.

### Cited Findings
- The goal of probabilistic forecasting is to maximise sharpness subject to calibration. Probabilistic, exceedance and marginal calibration are distinct; marginal calibration compares the average predictive CDF with the empirical CDF of the observations; a climatological forecaster is calibrated in these senses but not sharp, and a uniform PIT does not identify the ideal forecaster. `[M]` — [Gneiting, Balabdaoui & Raftery 2007, JRSS-B 69:243–268](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9868.2007.00587.x)
- A uniform rank histogram is necessary but not sufficient for reliability: it can arise from offsetting conditional biases, and non-uniform shapes are ambiguous between bias, dispersion errors and sampling of heterogeneous regimes. `[M]` — [Hamill 2001, MWR 129:550–560](https://doi.org/10.1175/1520-0493%282001%29129%3C0550:IORHFV%3E2.0.CO;2)
- For correct one-step-ahead densities the PIT series is i.i.d. uniform; multi-step PITs are serially dependent, so naive uniformity tests are invalid at h>1. `[M]` — [Diebold, Gunther & Tay 1998, Int. Econ. Rev. 39:863–883](https://doi.org/10.2307/2527342)
- For count or other discrete outcomes the PIT must be randomised or replaced by its non-randomised uniform version. `[M]` — [Czado, Gneiting & Held 2009, Biometrics 65:1254–1261](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1541-0420.2009.01191.x)
- CORP reliability diagrams (isotonic regression via pool-adjacent-violators) are consistent, optimally binned and reproducible, and give a score decomposition S = MCB − DSC + UNC. `[M]` — [Dimitriadis, Gneiting & Jordan 2021, PNAS 118:e2016191118](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2016191118)
- The CORP idea extends to real-valued outcomes: a hierarchy of calibration notions, "conditional T-calibration" for a functional T (mean, quantile, expectile), T-reliability diagrams, and a coefficient of determination (DSC − MCB)/UNC that generalises R². `[V]` — [Gneiting & Resin 2023, Electron. J. Stat. 17:3226–3286](https://doi.org/10.1214/23-EJS2180)
- The mean CRPS has an isotonicity-based decomposition into non-negative miscalibration, discrimination and uncertainty components for the full predictive distribution, shown in MCB–DSC plots. `[V]` — [Arnold, Walz, Ziegel & Gneiting 2024, Electron. J. Stat. 18:4992–5044](https://doi.org/10.1214/24-EJS2316)
- The same decomposition has been extended to the interval score, to assess conditional calibration of interval forecasts. `[V]` — [arXiv:2508.18034 (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.18034)
- Classical calibration tests are valid only for a fixed evaluation period; e-value tests of probabilistic calibration stay valid under optional stopping and have competitive power. `[V]` — [Arnold, Henzi & Ziegel 2023, Ann. Appl. Stat. 17:1909–1935](https://doi.org/10.1214/22-AOAS1697)
- Interval forecasts should be tested for conditional coverage (independence of hits) as well as unconditional coverage. `[M]` — [Christoffersen 1998, Int. Econ. Rev. 39:841–862](https://doi.org/10.2307/2527341)

### Inferences
- The gate itself is a marginal-calibration check (average predictive law vs realised law). By Gneiting et al. (2007), the train-window mobility baseline is a climatological forecaster and is marginally calibrated by construction when mobility is stationary. A tie on pooled moments is therefore the *expected* result for any adequate model under stationarity; the model can win only when mobility shifts and its structure extrapolates better than the raw train window (which is what happened in the registered E2 block, 0.032 vs 0.171).
- The recorded pooled coverages (e.g. 0.17/0.54/0.89 against nominal .10/.50/.90) should be reported as numbers with intervals clustered by week, and not summarised as "near-nominal" without a pre-specified tolerance. A 7-point excess below the 10% quantile is a material lower-tail miscalibration if it survives clustering.
- Stratify every calibration diagnostic by horizon (1/4/13), permanent-rank band (train-only), era, and amplitude (temperament) tercile. Hamill's point applies directly: a head that is over-dispersed and a tail that is under-dispersed can pool to a flat PIT.
- The MCB/DSC/UNC decomposition answers the question the pooled gate cannot: does the model have *discrimination* (DSC>0) beyond the rank-conditional baseline? A model that conditions on filtered home level and amplitude should show DSC above a baseline that conditions on current rank only; if it does not, the entity-level states carry no out-of-sample information at that horizon. That is a publishable finding either way.
- Gate v1's "coverage" clause counts non-rejection of a point null (model median inside the empirical median's sampling CI) as success. It gets *easier* to pass with less data and harder with more, and with integer medians it is knife-edged. It should be kept for continuity and replaced in any v2 by an equivalence bound (section 6).
- Report sharpness (mean width of central 50% and 90% intervals by stratum) next to coverage, for model and baseline.

- Knüppel (2015, JBES 33:270–281) on calibration tests for multi-step density forecasts is cited from memory only.

## 3. Baselines and the meaning of skill

### Takeaway
A referee will expect a ladder of baselines from no-change through a nonparametric rank-bin transition kernel, simple per-entity and pooled time-series models, a flexible global learner used as a predictability ceiling, and the published ranking-dynamics comparator. A structural model that ties a strong benchmark is positioned by showing where the predictability ceiling is, that it transports where the benchmark cannot, and that it gets untargeted patterns right.

### Cited Findings
- Simple methods (mean, naive, seasonal naive, drift) are the benchmarks any new method must be compared with, and time-series cross-validation with a rolling origin is the standard evaluation. `[M]` — [Hyndman & Athanasopoulos, FPP3 §5.2](https://otexts.com/fpp3/simple-methods.html); [FPP3 §5.10](https://otexts.com/fpp3/tscv.html)
- In the PNAS evaluation of COVID-19 mortality forecasts, 27 models were compared with a naive baseline; two-thirds of models beat the baseline, the multi-model ensemble was the most consistently accurate, and probabilistic error at 20 weeks was three to five times that at one week. `[V]` The score was the weighted interval score, reported relative to the baseline, and the baseline was a no-change median with spread taken from past changes. `[M]` — [Cramer et al. 2022, PNAS 119:e2113561119](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2113561119)
- In the Fragile Families challenge, 160 teams using thousands of predictors produced predictions that were not very accurate and only slightly better than a simple four-variable benchmark model. `[M]` — [Salganik et al. 2020, PNAS 117:8398–8403](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1915006117)
- For cascade size on Twitter, the best models explain less than half the variance, and simulations show that even small uncertainty about content quality puts realistic bounds on predictability close to what is achieved empirically. `[V]` — [Martin, Hofman, Sharma, Anderson & Watts 2016, WWW, pp. 683–694](https://arxiv.org/abs/1602.01013)
- Across four social platforms, popularity prediction that "peeks" at early adoption is accurate, but almost all the power comes from the speed of early adoption; other features are inconsistent across domains, and controlling for early speed lowers accuracy, so high predictive accuracy coexists with little understanding. `[V]` — [Shulman, Sharma & Cosley 2016, ICWSM](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.09436)
- Explanatory power and predictive power are different properties requiring different modelling and evaluation choices, and should be reported separately. `[M]` — [Shmueli 2010, Statistical Science 25:289–310](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-STS330)
- Psychology (and social science generally) should give prediction more weight, because models optimised for explanation routinely overfit and generalise poorly. `[M]` — [Yarkoni & Westfall 2017, Perspect. Psychol. Sci. 12:1100–1122](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691617693393)
- Social science needs common standards for prediction and a better characterisation of the theoretical limits to predictive accuracy. `[M]` — [Hofman, Sharma & Watts 2017, Science 355:486–488](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aal3856)
- Research can be placed on two axes, explanatory focus and attention to testing predictions; the under-occupied quadrant is "integrative modelling", which uses causal/structural understanding to predict outcomes not yet observed. `[V]` — [Hofman et al. 2021, Nature 595:181–188](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-021-03659-0)
- M4: combinations and a hybrid statistical–neural method were most accurate, and pure ML submissions did poorly against statistical benchmarks. M5: tree-based global models (LightGBM) dominated and clearly beat the statistical benchmarks. `[M]` — [Makridakis, Spiliotis & Assimakopoulos 2020, IJF 36:54–74](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2019.04.014); [Makridakis et al. 2022, IJF 38:1346–1364](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2021.11.013)
- Global (cross-learned) models can match or beat local per-series models even on heterogeneous collections; methods should be classified as local vs global and not as "statistical vs ML". `[M]` — [Januschowski et al. 2020, IJF 36:167–177](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2019.05.008); [Montero-Manso & Hyndman 2021, IJF 37:1632–1653](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijforecast.2021.03.004)
- The closest published comparator describes rank dynamics in about 30 systems with a two-parameter (displacement and replacement) model. `[M]` — [Iñiguez, Pineda, Gershenson & Barabási 2022, Nat. Commun. 13:1646](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-29256-x)
- Optimal linear prediction pools give positive weight to models that are individually inferior when they carry distinct information; forecast-encompassing tests ask the same question for point forecasts. `[M]` — [Geweke & Amisano 2011, J. Econometrics 164:130–141](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2011.02.017); [Harvey, Leybourne & Newbold 1998, JBES 16:254–259](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1998.10524759)

### Inferences
- **Forecast value added.** FVA is the practitioner term (Gilliland; see Gaps) for the change in accuracy that each step of a forecasting process contributes relative to a naive forecast. Applied here it is the ablation ladder B6 below: each model component earns its place by a measured, interval-qualified improvement over the model without it.
- **Baseline set a referee would expect** (all fitted on the train window only, all scored by the same scores):
  - B0 no-change in rank, made probabilistic by attaching the train-window h-step displacement distribution (the Forecast Hub style baseline). In unconditional form this is the current "historical mobility" baseline.
  - B1 empirical rank-bin transition kernel, estimated directly at each horizon h. This is the natural nonparametric benchmark and, under stationarity and a Markov-in-rank world, the best rank-only forecast. Also report the one-step kernel raised to the h-th power: its failure at long horizons is the classical mover–stayer result in mobility research and is the non-parametric counterpart of the model's amplitude ("temperament") heterogeneity.
  - B2 per-entity local level / ETS(A,N,N) / ARIMA(0,1,1) / Theta on log activity, simulated jointly to ranks.
  - B3 pooled AR(p) on log levels with entity effects and empirical-Bayes shrinkage.
  - B4 a global gradient-boosted quantile or distributional learner on lags, rolling volatility, rank and age. Its role is to estimate the *predictability ceiling*, not to be beaten.
  - B5 the Iñiguez et al. two-parameter model, fitted on the train window and run through the same gate.
  - B6 the model's own ablation ladder (no amplitude heterogeneity; Gaussian innovations; single band; no medium-scale AR), reported as an FVA table with intervals.
- **Positioning a model that ties persistence.** Four separable claims, each with its own evidence: (i) *ceiling*: if B4 also fails to beat B1, the tie is with the attainable limit (Martin et al.; Salganik et al.), and the right statement is "no method we tried extracts entity-level predictability beyond rank-conditional mobility at horizon h"; (ii) *completeness*: if B4 does beat B1, report the share of the B1-to-B4 gap the model closes; (iii) *transport*: the benchmark needs target-era data, while the model with parameters frozen in another era can be tested out of era and out of platform, which is where its registered success lies; (iv) *untargeted patterns*: the b≈1 amplitude collapse on an unseen metric is evidence of the Shmueli "explanatory" kind and should not be mixed into the predictive ledger.
- **Combination as a test of added information.** Estimate the optimal log-score or CRPS pool weight on the model against B1. A weight clearly above zero means the model carries information the benchmark lacks even when average scores tie. This is the density-forecast analogue of encompassing and is the most direct answer to "ties persistence, so what does it add?".
- Report skill relative to *two* references: the no-information reference (unconditional pooled mobility) and the strong reference (B1). "Skill ≈ 0 against B1" and "skill > 0 against the unconditional reference" are both needed to interpret a non-inferiority margin (section 6).
- The parsimony comparison a referee will ask for is B5 vs the model on identical scores: a ~500-value model should be shown on an accuracy–complexity frontier against a 2-parameter one.

### Gaps

## 4. Rolling-origin design, embargo and leakage

### Takeaway
Rolling-origin evaluation with re-estimation at each origin is the accepted design for non-stationary series. The remaining risks for this program are leakage through anything computed on the full panel (universe, permanent rank, standardisation, era labels, spec choices) and treating a handful of overlapping origins as independent evidence.

### Cited Findings
- Out-of-sample tests should use multiple (rolling) origins, recalibrate at each origin, keep an adequate test period and report by horizon; single-origin results are unreliable. `[M]` — [Tashman 2000, IJF 16:437–450](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-2070(00)00065-0)
- For stationary series, blocked cross-validation performs well and the theoretical objections to CV had little practical effect in their experiments. `[M]` — [Bergmeir & Benítez 2012, Information Sciences 191:192–213](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ins.2011.12.028)
- K-fold CV is valid for purely autoregressive models when the residuals are uncorrelated. `[M]` — [Bergmeir, Hyndman & Koo 2018, CSDA 120:70–83](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.csda.2017.11.003)
- On real-world, potentially non-stationary series, repeated holdout over multiple testing periods gave the most accurate performance estimates; CV variants did better only on stationary synthetic series. `[M]` — [Cerqueira, Torgo & Mozetič 2020, Machine Learning 109:1997–2028](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10994-020-05910-7)
- Guidance for data scientists on forecast evaluation covers data partitioning, error measures and significance testing, and links non-stationarity and non-normality to common evaluation mistakes. `[V]` — [Hewamalage, Ackermann & Bergmeir 2023, Data Min. Knowl. Disc. 37:788–832](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10618-022-00894-5)
- A survey found leakage errors in 17 fields affecting 294 papers and gives a taxonomy of eight leakage types, including pre-processing on train and test together, feature selection on the full data, temporal leakage and non-independence between train and test. `[V]` — [Kapoor & Narayanan 2023, Patterns 4:100804](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2023.100804)
- Choosing the estimation/evaluation window after seeing results invalidates inference; tests robust to window choice exist. `[M]` — [Inoue & Rossi 2012, JBES 30:432–453](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.2012.693850)
- With overlapping h-step forecasts, loss differentials are serially correlated up to lag h−1 by construction, and the long-run variance estimate can be negative in small samples. `[V via search summary; the link-to-paper mapping is inferred]` — [Harvey, Leybourne & Whitehouse 2017, IJF 33:833–847](https://nottingham-repository.worktribe.com/output/892023)
- Purging and embargoing observations around the train/test boundary is the standard remedy when labels span time intervals. `[M]` — [López de Prado 2018, *Advances in Financial Machine Learning*, Wiley](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Advances+in+Financial+Machine+Learning-p-9781119482086)

### Inferences
- **Leakage audit for this model** (the program has already closed membership and week-boundary leaks; the remaining list, in Kapoor–Narayanan terms):
  - universe selection and K: must be train-only at each origin (closed);
  - permanent rank and band assignment z: a time average is a full-sample quantity, so bands used for test entities must come from the train window;
  - standardisation and the common per-period factor: any centring or scaling must use train-window statistics;
  - long-difference moments D(h) up to 52 weeks: training targets must end at the origin;
  - instrument-era definitions: drawn with knowledge of the whole series, so declare them as fixed design inputs;
  - the spec stack (flags such as lag depth, two-scale, t-tails): chosen over many sessions with the same splits in view, which is hyper-parameter leakage through the analyst (section 8).
- **Embargo.** In a pure past-to-future rolling origin the embargo matters less than in CV: its job is to make sure no training target straddles the origin and to separate test blocks. Use non-overlapping test blocks with a gap of at least the longest scored horizon (13 weeks) between the end of one block's targets and the start of the next.
- **How many origins.** No closed-form power result was found. The practical answer is a design analysis by simulation (section 5): generate panels from the fitted model and from named misspecified alternatives, run the whole gate, and report P(pass | model true), P(pass | alternative true) and P(baseline passes its own gate).
- **Origins are cheap for entity-level scores.** Parameters need not be re-estimated at every forecast origin: with parameters frozen per training block and the filter updated weekly, h=1 forecasts can be issued every week of the test block. That turns five pooled-moment observations into 50–100 weekly loss differentials, each already averaged over thousands of entities.
- Reddit comments (T≈213 weeks) can support at most about 8 non-overlapping 13-week test blocks after a 100-week minimum training window (fewer if gaps are inserted between blocks); Facebook Era A (≈88 weeks) cannot, so its uncertainty statements will be weaker and should say so.

### Gaps
- No source found giving power or sample-size formulas for the number of rolling origins in panel forecast comparisons.
- A September 2026 preprint ([arXiv:2609.27912](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.27912)) reportedly notes that distribution-free tests across a few origins can only reach an "all-same-sign floor"; seen through a search summary only.

## 5. Statistical comparison of predictive performance; honest uncertainty with about five origins

### Takeaway
Compare *forecasting methods* on loss differentials with dependence-robust inference, and control for the number of specifications tried. With five origins, between-split SDs are not standard errors: use block-level t or randomisation inference with few clusters, fixed-smoothing Diebold–Mariano on a longer weekly series of entity-averaged losses, and anytime-valid e-processes for evidence that accumulates over sessions and new data.

### Cited Findings
- The Diebold–Mariano test compares the expected loss of two forecast series through a HAC t-statistic on the loss differential. `[M]` — [Diebold & Mariano 1995, JBES 13:253–263](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.1995.10524599)
- The DM test was meant for comparing forecasts, not models. For comparing models, full-sample procedures are simpler and more powerful, and pseudo-out-of-sample analysis is not automatic insurance against overfitting, though it remains useful for showing comparative performance in particular historical episodes. `[M]` — [Diebold 2015, JBES 33:1–9](https://doi.org/10.1080/07350015.2014.983236)
- Conditional predictive ability tests evaluate the forecasting *method* (model, estimator and window together), allow nested and non-nested comparisons under a fixed-length estimation window, and test whether the loss differential is predictable from current information. `[M]` — [Giacomini & White 2006, Econometrica 74:1545–1578](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00718.x)
- For nested models the larger model's out-of-sample MSPE is inflated by estimation noise under the null and standard DM critical values do not apply; adjusted statistics exist. `[M]` — [Clark & McCracken 2001, J. Econometrics 105:85–110](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4076(01)00071-9); [Clark & West 2007, J. Econometrics 138:291–311](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2006.05.023)
- Common out-of-sample tests of equal accuracy for nested models are asymptotically equivalent to a difference of two Wald statistics, which exposes weaknesses in using them for inference about nested models. `[V]` — [Hansen & Timmermann 2015, Econometrica 83:2485–2505](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA10581)
- The model confidence set returns the set of models that contains the best with given confidence; its size reflects how informative the data are. `[M]` — [Hansen, Lunde & Nason 2011, Econometrica 79:453–497](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA5771)
- The reality check and the superior-predictive-ability test ask whether the best of many searched specifications beats a benchmark after accounting for the search. `[M]` — [White 2000, Econometrica 68:1097–1126](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00152); [Hansen 2005, JBES 23:365–380](https://doi.org/10.1198/073500105000000063)
- Multi-horizon comparison: "uniform" SPA requires superiority at every horizon and "average" SPA lets horizons offset; a 2023 working paper reports serious size distortions in the uniform test's bootstrap and proposes normal critical values. `[V]` — [Quaedvlieg 2021, JBES 39:40–53](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/jnlbes/v39y2021i1p40-53.html); critiqued by [Monschang, Trede & Wilfling 2023 (working paper)](https://www.wiwi.uni-muenster.de/cqe/sites/cqe/files/CQE_Paper/cqe_wp_106_2023.pdf)
- Small samples: fixed-smoothing (fixed-b) asymptotics give DM tests with correct size with only a few out-of-sample observations, and with them some apparent skill of professional forecasters over a random walk disappears; extended to density forecasts in 2024. `[V]` — [Coroneo & Iacone 2020, J. Appl. Econometrics 35:391–409](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.2756)
- The small-sample DM correction refers the statistic to a t distribution. `[M]` — [Harvey, Leybourne & Newbold 1997, IJF 13:281–291](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0169-2070(96)00719-4)
- Few clusters: estimate within q≥2 groups and apply an ordinary t-test to the q estimates; the test is valid (conservative) at the 5% level under heterogeneous variances if the group estimates are approximately independent and Gaussian. `[V]` — [Ibragimov & Müller 2010, JBES 28:453–468](https://collaborate.princeton.edu/en/publications/t-statistic-based-correlation-and-heterogeneity-robust-inference/)
- Approximate randomisation (sign-change) tests are valid with a fixed, small number of clusters, "even as small as five", with arbitrary dependence within clusters; the target must be identified within each cluster. `[V]` — [Canay, Romano & Shaikh 2017, Econometrica 85:1013–1030](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/emetrp/v85y2017ip1013-1030.html); [Cai, Canay, Kim & Shaikh, arXiv:2102.09058](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.09058)
- Panel versions of equal-predictive-ability tests exist for overall and clustered nulls under weak and strong cross-sectional dependence (R package `pEPA`), and for common-factor structures where a strong common component in loss differentials can invalidate a cross-sectional CLT. `[V]` — [Akgun, Pirotte, Urga & Yang 2024, IJF 40:202–228](https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.02803); [Qu, Timmermann & Zhu 2024, IJF 40:918–941](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/intfor/v40y2024i3p918-941.html)
- Block bootstraps for dependent data: moving blocks and the stationary bootstrap; for panels, resampling time blocks of whole cross-sections is robust to cross-sectional dependence of unknown form; the wild cluster bootstrap improves inference with few clusters. `[M]` — [Künsch 1989, Ann. Stat. 17:1217–1241](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1176347265); [Politis & Romano 1994, JASA 89:1303–1313](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1994.10476870); [Gonçalves 2011, Econometric Theory 27:1048–1082](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266466610000630); [Cameron, Gelbach & Miller 2008, REStat 90:414–427](https://doi.org/10.1162/rest.90.3.414)
- E-values for comparing probability forecasts of binary events are valid in finite samples without a model of the data-generating process and allow optional stopping. `[V]` — [Henzi & Ziegel 2022, Biometrika 109:647–663](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asab047)
- Confidence sequences and e-processes for the average score difference between two sequential forecasters need no distributional assumptions; the main theorems cover bounded scores; R package `seqcomp`. `[V]` — [Choe & Ramdas 2024, Operations Research 72:1368–1387](https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00115)
- Sequential model confidence sets give time-uniform, non-asymptotic coverage (accepted at JRSS-B, March 2026, per the search summary). `[V]` — [Arnold, Gavrilopoulos, Schulz & Ziegel, arXiv:2404.18678](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.18678)

### Inferences
- **Declare the estimand of each interval.** An entity bootstrap inside one test block answers "how well do we know this block's score?" (conditional on the period). It does not answer "would this hold in another period?", which needs variation across blocks. The record's "0.123 ± 0.033 vs 0.145 ± 0.031" style numbers are between-split SDs of each arm; the quantity that matters is the *paired* difference per split and its uncertainty across splits.
- **What five blocks can and cannot show.** With q independent blocks a sign-flip randomisation test has a smallest attainable one-sided p of 1/2^q: 0.031 at q=5, so a two-sided 5% rejection is impossible (floor 0.0625) and q≥6 is needed. The Ibragimov–Müller t-test has 4 degrees of freedom at q=5 (critical values 2.776 two-sided, 2.132 one-sided at 5%). Both are honest and low-powered. Pre-register q≥8 non-overlapping blocks where the panel allows.
- **Get power from the weekly dimension.** For entity-level scores, form the weekly cross-sectional mean loss differential d_t (averaging over entities absorbs cross-sectional dependence into one time series), then apply a fixed-b DM test to {d_t} at h=1 and to non-overlapping targets at h=4 and 13. This is the "overall" panel test of Qu–Timmermann–Zhu in its simplest form and follows Giacomini–White in treating frozen-parameter-plus-filter as the method under test.
- **Nested comparisons.** The local-level model and the ablations are nested in the model. If the claim is about the population model ("amplitude heterogeneity matters"), use Clark–West type adjustments or, following Diebold (2015), full-sample evidence. If the claim is about the finite-sample forecasting method, use Giacomini–White. State which.
- **Data-snooping correction is feasible here.** The append-only results log enumerates every specification that has been put through the gate. That list is the universe for a White/Hansen SPA test of "best spec vs baseline", or at minimum for a specification curve of gate outcomes (section 8). Few modelling programs can do this, and it would convert a liability (many gate evaluations) into evidence.
- **Anytime-valid monitoring fits the program's workflow.** Scores on bounded ranks (normalised CRPS, RPS, Brier) satisfy the boundedness condition in Choe & Ramdas. An e-process on the weekly loss differential lets evidence accumulate across sessions and as Wikipedia weeks arrive, with optional stopping and continuation, which a fixed-n DM test does not permit.
- **Bootstrap choice for descriptive intervals.** Resample entities as clusters (keeping each entity's whole displacement history) and weeks in moving blocks; the two-way version handles common shocks. Caveat: resampling entities breaks the permutation constraint on ranks in a closed universe, so use it for marginal statistics only. The registered IID bootstrap stays as the v1 letter; the clustered interval should be the primary in any v2.
- Separate Monte Carlo error (seeds) from sampling error (data) in every reported interval; 20 seeds fix the former only.

## 6. Equivalence and non-inferiority: making "at par" testable

### Takeaway
"At par" should be a pre-specified equivalence or non-inferiority claim tested with a confidence bound against a margin, not a description of a non-significant or small point difference. I found no published application of TOST to Diebold–Mariano loss differentials, so the construction below is standard equivalence logic applied to the forecast-comparison statistic.

### Cited Findings
- Failing to reject a null of no difference is not evidence of equivalence. The two one-sided tests (TOST) procedure rejects effects at least as large as a pre-specified smallest effect size of interest (SESOI), and equivalence at α=0.05 corresponds to the 90% CI lying inside the bounds. `[M]` — [Lakens 2017, Soc. Psychol. Personal. Sci. 8:355–362](https://doi.org/10.1177/1948550617697177); [Schuirmann 1987, J. Pharmacokinet. Biopharm. 15:657–680](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF01068419)
- Ways to justify a SESOI ex ante include theoretical predictions, practical or resource limits, benchmarks from prior studies, and the smallest effect an earlier study could detect. `[M]` — [Lakens, Scheel & Isager 2018, AMPPS 1:259–269](https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245918770963)
- The Bayesian analogue declares practical equivalence when the 95% highest-density interval falls inside a region of practical equivalence (ROPE) fixed in advance. `[M]` — [Kruschke 2018, AMPPS 1:270–280](https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245918771304)
- Regulatory non-inferiority practice: the margin must be pre-specified; M1 is the whole effect of the active control over placebo, estimated conservatively from historical evidence; M2 is the largest clinically acceptable loss and is normally a fraction of M1; the comparison is valid only if the trial has assay sensitivity and the historical effect still holds (constancy). `[M]` — [FDA 2016, Non-Inferiority Clinical Trials to Establish Effectiveness](https://www.fda.gov/media/78504/download)
- Moving from a non-inferiority conclusion to a superiority test in the same trial needs no multiplicity adjustment when the order is pre-specified. `[M]` — [EMA, Points to consider on switching between superiority and non-inferiority](https://www.ema.europa.eu/en/switching-between-superiority-non-inferiority-scientific-guideline)
- A June 2026 preprint frames comparison of prediction-performance metrics under clustered data as a non-inferiority test against a pre-specified margin Δ. `[V, search summary only]` — [arXiv:2606.03656](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03656)
- The standard DM implementations (R `forecast::dm.test`, statsmodels) test only the equal-accuracy null. `[V]` — [forecast::dm.test documentation](https://pkg.robjhyndman.com/forecast/reference/dm.test.html)

### Inferences
- **The registered rule is a point-estimate non-inferiority rule.** "model rel err ≤ baseline + 0.05" has a margin but no confidence bound, so it does not control the probability of declaring non-inferiority when the model is truly worse by more than 0.05. The margin is also large relative to the baseline's own error (0.05 against roughly 0.15–0.17 is about 30%). Keep it as the v1 letter; do not describe a v1 pass as "statistically non-inferior".
- **Testable version.** Let d be the per-block (or per-week) paired difference in score, model minus benchmark, lower is better. Non-inferiority: the upper bound of the one-sided 95% interval for E[d] is below +δ. Equivalence ("at par"): the 90% interval lies inside (−δ, +δ). Superiority: the upper bound is below 0. Use the block-level t (df=q−1) or fixed-b DM variance from section 5 for the bound.
- **Feasibility with five blocks (illustration, not a result).** If the paired split differences on Facebook had SD 0.04 around the recorded mean difference of −0.022, the one-sided upper bound would be −0.022 + 2.132·(0.04/√5) = +0.016, inside a 0.05 margin. The paired-difference SD is not in the record I read and must be computed; on Instagram, where split SDs are 0.19–0.31, no such claim would be supportable.
- **Choosing δ ex ante (two defensible routes; fix one before Wikipedia contact).**
  1. *Preserved-fraction (regulatory) logic.* M1 = the strong benchmark's advantage over the no-information reference (unconditional pooled mobility, or no-change), taken at its lower confidence bound from already-seen platforms. δ = a stated fraction of M1 (50% is the conventional default). Non-inferiority within δ then implies the model retains at least half of what the benchmark achieves over the trivial reference. This also forces the assay-sensitivity check: if the benchmark does not beat the trivial reference on the new platform, the comparison is uninformative and must be reported as such.
  2. *Noise-floor SESOI.* δ = the benchmark's own replication variability, i.e. the typical score difference between the benchmark trained on two adjacent eras and tested on the same block. Differences smaller than the benchmark's instability across eras are not practically meaningful.
  Take the smaller of the two.
- **Sequence.** Pre-specify non-inferiority first, then superiority, per horizon in the order h=1, 4, 13 (fixed-sequence testing; stop at the first failure for confirmatory purposes and report the rest descriptively).
- A Bayesian ROPE statement on the same d is an acceptable alternative if the program prefers posterior language, but the ROPE must be the same pre-registered δ.

### Gaps
- No primary source applying TOST or non-inferiority testing to Diebold–Mariano-type loss differentials in the forecasting literature was found; the 2026 clustered-data preprint is the closest and was seen only as a search summary.
- The FDA and EMA URLs are from memory.

## 7. Validating generative/explanatory adequacy as distinct from forecasting

### Takeaway
Fit to patterns used in estimation is verification; only patterns not used in building or tuning the model corroborate it, and the weight of a good fit depends on what the model could not have fitted. The strongest evidence is transport to a new era or platform under frozen parameters. A scorecard should be presented as a labelled residual map with bands, not as a count.

### Cited Findings
- Posterior predictive assessment compares realised discrepancy measures with their distribution under replicated data from the fitted model. `[M]` — [Gelman, Meng & Stern 1996, Statistica Sinica 6:733–807](http://www3.stat.sinica.edu.tw/statistica/j6n4/j6n41/j6n41.htm)
- In a Bayesian workflow, prior predictive checks show what the model can generate before seeing data, and predictive checks are most informative for statistics that are not directly tied to fitted parameters. `[M]` — [Gabry, Simpson, Vehtari, Betancourt & Gelman 2019, JRSS-A 182:389–402](https://doi.org/10.1111/rssa.12378)
- A close fit by a model with free parameters is not persuasive by itself: the reader also needs to know how flexible the theory is (what it rules out), how variable the data are, and whether plausible alternative outcomes would have contradicted the theory. `[V]` — [Roberts & Pashler 2000, Psychological Review 107:358–367](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.107.2.358)
- A claim is warranted only to the extent it has passed a test that would probably have detected its falsity (severity). `[M]` — [Mayo 2018, *Statistical Inference as Severe Testing*, CUP](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781107286184)
- Pattern-oriented modelling uses multiple patterns observed at different scales and hierarchical levels as filters on model structure and parameters; matching several weak patterns jointly is more discriminating than one strong pattern, and independent secondary predictions are the strongest validation. `[M]` — [Grimm et al. 2005, Science 310:987–991](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1116681); [Grimm & Railsback 2012, Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B 367:298–310](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2011.0180)
- "Evaludation" separates *model output verification* (agreement with the patterns used to build and calibrate the model) from *model output corroboration* (agreement with independent data or patterns not used in development); TRACE documents give a standard format for recording both. `[M]` — [Augusiak, Van den Brink & Grimm 2014, Ecol. Model. 280:117–128](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2013.11.009); [Grimm et al. 2014, Ecol. Model. 280:129–139](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ecolmodel.2014.01.018)
- In agent-based economics the usual procedure is "indirect calibration", which starts from stylised facts the modeller wants to reproduce; the validation literature offers a taxonomy and a critical discussion of this and alternative approaches. `[V]` — [Fagiolo, Moneta & Windrum 2007, Computational Economics 30:195–226](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10614-007-9104-4)
- Structural estimation should report which moments drive which parameters and separate targeted from other moments. `[M]` — [Andrews, Gentzkow & Shapiro 2017, QJE 132:1553–1592](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjx023); [Nakamura & Steinsson 2018, J. Econ. Perspect. 32(3):59–86](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.32.3.59)
- A proposed "really risky" registered modelling report asks authors to state prior predictions before data and reviewers to set in advance which data patterns would count as a poor fit. `[V via search summary; the DOI-to-paper mapping is inferred]` — [Vanpaemel 2019, Comput. Brain Behav. 2:218–222](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s42113-019-00056-9)
- Optimising a measure degrades it as a measure. `[M]` — [Thomas & Uminsky 2022, Patterns 3:100476](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.patter.2022.100476); [Manheim & Garrabrant, arXiv:1803.04585](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.04585)

### Inferences
- **Label every scorecard row.** Each of the 15 rows is either *targeted* (a function of the autocovariances γ0..γ6, long-difference variances, kurtosis, or dispersion moments used in estimation; agreement is verification) or *untargeted* (churn, boundary flux, rank ACF beyond fitted lags, collision rates, head shares, departure rates; agreement is corroboration). A referee will discount the first group to near zero, so the paper should do so first.
- **Give each row a severity statement.** For each untargeted row report the same statistic for B1, B5 and the ablations, and for surrogate (phase-randomised) panels. A row that every comparator passes has no discriminating power and should be marked as such. This is Roberts & Pashler's third question made operational.
- **Show what the architecture cannot produce.** Simulate from the model family at parameter values drawn from a declared range (a prior-predictive or parameter-space exercise) and report the range of each scorecard statistic. With ~500 band values the concern is that almost any moment profile is reachable; the answer is a measured restrictiveness, not an assertion.
- **Do not headline a count.** "12–15 of 15" invites both misreading and optimisation. Present the card as estimates with sampling and Monte Carlo bands, list the known failures (departure deficit, head law, 13-week variance ratio) in the same table, freeze the card definition by date/hash, and disclose its revision history (including the band-alignment bug). Keeping it non-adjudicating, as the program already does, is the main protection against Goodhart effects; the labelling and comparator columns are the rest.
- **Rank the evidence explicitly.** From weakest to strongest: in-sample targeted fit; in-sample untargeted fit; rolling-origin out-of-sample on a repeatedly used panel; frozen-parameter test on an untouched block (E2); frozen-structure test on a new metric or platform with pre-registered predictions (the submissions backtest; Wikipedia). The paper's central claims should rest on the last two tiers.
- **Pattern-oriented reading of the frontier residuals.** The departure deficit and the head-law reversal are failed untargeted patterns at a different level (entity trajectories; stationary head) from the fitted ones (band-level change moments). In POM terms they are the patterns that discriminate between candidate structures, so they belong in the validation section as filters the current structure does not pass, not in a limitations paragraph.
- A TRACE-style supplement (problem formulation, model description, data evaluation, implementation verification, output verification, sensitivity analysis, output corroboration) maps closely onto what MODEL_STATUS already records and would be a recognisable format for reviewers from ecology and complex systems.

## 8. Preregistration and researcher degrees of freedom for modelling; adaptive overfitting to a reused gate

### Takeaway
Modelling-specific preregistration fixes the model, estimation, evaluation metric, baselines, decision rule and prior knowledge of the data, and keeps a "postregistration" log of exploration. The program already does most of this. The exposed flank is the repeated use of the same rolling-origin splits over many sessions, for which the protections are untouched lockboxes, a ledger of gate evaluations, a multiplicity-aware summary of everything tried, and third-party timestamps.

### Cited Findings
- Robust modelling practice includes preregistering models and evaluation criteria, "postregistration" (a record of exploratory model development), registered modelling reports, and testing generalisation; commentaries dispute how far these fit the goals of modelling. `[V]` — [Lee et al. 2019, Comput. Brain Behav. 2:141–153](https://doi.org/10.1007/s42113-019-00029-y)
- The first preregistration template for cognitive modelling covers "model application" (a model used as a measurement tool to test hypotheses about its parameters) and catalogues modelling-specific degrees of freedom. `[V]` — [Crüwell & Evans 2021, R. Soc. Open Sci. 8:210155](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsos.210155)
- For predictive modelling, a lightweight two-stage preregistration is proposed: once when the problem is defined and again after training, when the model and the evaluation are frozen and before the test set is touched; motivated by data-dependent decisions and unintended test-set reuse. `[V, via abstract and secondary summary]` — [Hofman, Chatzimparmpas, Sharma, Watts & Hullman, arXiv:2311.18807](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18807)
- Preregistration of secondary-data analyses must disclose prior knowledge of the data and can use holdout samples and split-sample designs. `[M]` — [Van den Akker et al. 2021, Meta-Psychology 5](https://doi.org/10.15626/MP.2020.2625); [Weston, Ritchie, Rohrer & Przybylski 2019, AMPPS 2:214–227](https://doi.org/10.1177/2515245919848684)
- Data-dependent analysis choices inflate false positives even without conscious fishing. `[M]` — [Gelman & Loken 2013, "The garden of forking paths"](http://www.stat.columbia.edu/~gelman/research/unpublished/p_hacking.pdf)
- Specification-curve analysis reports the result under every defensible specification and tests them jointly; multiverse analysis does the same for data-processing choices. `[M]` — [Simonsohn, Simmons & Nelson 2020, Nat. Hum. Behav. 4:1208–1214](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0912-z); [Steegen, Tuerlinckx, Gelman & Vanpaemel 2016, Perspect. Psychol. Sci. 11:702–712](https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691616658637)
- Adaptive reuse of a holdout set invalidates its estimates; the "reusable holdout" (Thresholdout) limits what the analyst learns from each query so that many adaptive queries remain valid. `[V]` — [Dwork et al. 2015, Science 349:636–638](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaa9375)
- Empirically, adaptive overfitting on large benchmarks is modest: over 100 Kaggle competitions show little evidence of substantial overfitting to public leaderboards, and new ImageNet/CIFAR test sets show accuracy drops attributed to distribution shift, with model rankings preserved. `[V]` — [Roelofs et al. 2019, NeurIPS](https://papers.nips.cc/paper/9117-a-meta-analysis-of-overfitting-in-machine-learning); [Recht, Roelofs, Schmidt & Shankar 2019, ICML](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/recht19a)
- Blind analysis hides the result (by noise, offsets or scrambling) until the analysis is fixed. Blinding keeps flexibility that strict preregistration removes, while still breaking the feedback from outcome to analysis. `[M/V]` — [MacCoun & Perlmutter 2015, Nature 526:187–189](https://doi.org/10.1038/526187a); [Dutilh, Sarafoglou & Wagenmakers 2021, Synthese 198:5745–5772](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11229-019-02456-7)
- In a blinded many-teams study, 17 modelling teams analysing the same 14 data sets reached broadly similar conclusions, but modeller degrees of freedom affected inferences and simpler approaches were often as accurate as complex ones. `[V]` — [Dutilh et al. 2019, Psychon. Bull. Rev. 26:1051–1069](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6449220/)
- Preregistration deviations are common and mostly unreported; a deviation table is recommended, and a deviation should be judged by whether it raises or lowers the severity of the test and the validity of the inference. `[V]` — [Willroth & Atherton 2024, AMPPS 7(1)](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459231213802); [Lakens 2024, Collabra: Psychology 10:117094](https://doi.org/10.1525/collabra.117094)

### Inferences
- **The reassuring ML evidence does not transfer.** Roelofs et al. and Recht et al. study holdouts with 10^4–10^5 independent test points. Gate v1 has about five dependent origins and seven moments, and the split-to-split SD of the score (0.03–0.06) is the same size as the differences being adjudicated (0.123 vs 0.145; 0.164 vs 0.168). As an illustration, the expected maximum of 20 independent N(0, 0.03²) draws is about 1.87 × 0.03 ≈ 0.056, which exceeds the 0.05 at-par margin. Correlated specifications reduce this, but the order of magnitude says that the best of a few dozen gate-evaluated specifications on the same splits can look at par or better by selection alone.
- **What protects against it.**
  1. *Lockboxes.* The registered frozen-parameter block (E2) and the Wikipedia panel are the only evidence free of adaptive reuse. Weight claims accordingly, and never report a lockbox result that was looked at more than once.
  2. *Gate ledger.* Count and publish the number of times each panel's gate has been evaluated, per specification, from the append-only log.
  3. *Multiplicity-aware summary.* Run a reality-check/SPA test or a specification curve over every specification in the log (section 5).
  4. *Fresh splits.* On already-used panels, define a new set of origins (offset by a fixed number of weeks decided now) and use each new origin once.
  5. *Blinding for new data.* For Wikipedia, develop and debug the pipeline on a blinded or scrambled version (entity labels permuted across weeks, or a noise offset on scores) and unblind once.
- **Prior-knowledge statement for Wikipedia.** The panel is described as "acquired/piloted, not yet analysed". The mini-protocol should say exactly what the pilot exposed (which statistics, which weeks, which entities), following the secondary-data templates.
- **Timestamps.** Git commits in a private repository are not independent timestamps. Deposit the frozen protocol, parameter hashes and code hash with a third party (OSF, Zenodo or equivalent) before data contact.
- **Template fit.** Crüwell & Evans cover model application, not model development or comparison, so their template transfers only in part. The program's amendments already serve as Lee et al.'s postregistration. What is missing relative to these templates is (a) an explicit list of analyst degrees of freedom still open at registration, and (b) a deviation table in the Willroth–Atherton format with a severity judgement for each amendment in Lakens' sense (the amendments that tightened execution raise severity; any that changed a criterion would lower it).
- **Adversarial review is not adversarial collaboration.** Cross-model review catches implementation and logic errors. It does not supply an opponent with a stake in a rival theory. Inviting an external critic (for instance someone working with the Iñiguez et al. model) to co-specify the Wikipedia test, including which outcomes would count against each model, would be the stronger design.

### Gaps
- Kahneman's adversarial-collaboration papers and the "red team" proposal (Lakens 2020, Nature 581:121) are recalled but not verified in this session.
- I found no published study of adaptive overfitting specifically for rolling-origin time-series evaluation reused across many modelling iterations; the argument above is by analogy plus arithmetic.
- The content of the Hofman et al. (2023) template was seen only through the abstract and a secondary summary.

## 9. Reporting standards a PNAS / Nature-portfolio statistics reviewer would look for

### Takeaway
Every headline number needs a sample size, a variability measure and an interval; every test needs its statistic, degrees of freedom and exact p-value; and the paper needs an explicit account of what was registered, what deviated and what is exploratory. Forecasting and ML-based-science checklists add leakage statements, baseline justification and full code/data release.

### Cited Findings
- The Nature Portfolio Reporting Summary requires, for each analysis, the exact sample size as a discrete number and unit, whether measurements are repeated or independent, the test and its sidedness, assumptions and multiple-comparison corrections, a measure of central tendency with variation or uncertainty (e.g. confidence intervals), and for null-hypothesis tests the statistic, effect sizes, degrees of freedom and exact P values. `[V, from hosted copies of the April 2023 form]` — [Nature Portfolio Reporting Summary (copy)](https://assets-eu.researchsquare.com/files/rs-3891149/v1/30e69bcfec332d9f84fb2c06.pdf)
- REFORMS is a 32-item consensus checklist for ML-based science from 19 researchers; it asks authors to justify the absence of each main source of leakage and covers data, pre-processing, modelling, evaluation and generalisability. `[V]` — [Kapoor et al. 2024, Science Advances 10:eadk3452](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adk3452); [REFORMS project page](https://reforms.cs.princeton.edu/)
- EPIFORGE 2020 is a reporting guideline for forecasting and prediction research (developed for epidemics) covering data, model, uncertainty, evaluation method and comparison with baselines. `[M]` — [Pollett et al. 2021, PLoS Medicine 18:e1003793](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pmed.1003793)
- TRIPOD+AI updates the reporting guideline for prediction models, including participant flow and separate reporting of development and evaluation. `[M]` — [Collins et al. 2024, BMJ 385:e078378](https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-078378)
- The PNAS forecast-evaluation exemplar reports scores relative to a baseline by horizon, with interval coverage, and states which models met inclusion criteria. `[V for the baseline comparison and horizon results; M for the rest]` — [Cramer et al. 2022, PNAS 119:e2113561119](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2113561119)
- A deviation-disclosure table is the recommended format for reporting departures from a preregistration. `[V]` — [Willroth & Atherton 2024, AMPPS 7(1)](https://doi.org/10.1177/25152459231213802)

### Inferences
- **Flow diagram.** A CONSORT-style figure for analyses: hypotheses registered → executed → passed / failed / not adjudicated (with reason) → amendments (dated, before or after data contact) → exploratory follow-ups. The program's verdict lists (E1–E5; P4–P11) already contain this information.
- **Headline table.** For each platform and horizon: n entities, n weeks, n origins/blocks, model score, benchmark score, paired difference with block-level interval, non-inferiority/equivalence verdict against the stated margin, and the number of prior evaluations of that panel.
- **Robustness tables.** Specification curve over solver conventions and spec stacks; sensitivity to K, to the bootstrap scheme, to the number of seeds, to the split schedule; scores by permanent-rank stratum.
- **Replication package.** Frozen parameter files with hashes, seeds, the exact commands, the membership files, and synthetic panels on which the full gate can be re-run without restricted data (CrowdTangle data cannot be redistributed, so a synthetic end-to-end path matters more than usual).
- **Language discipline.** Reserve "predicts" for out-of-sample statements with a proper score or a registered test behind them, "reproduces" for untargeted in-sample patterns, and "fits" for targeted ones.

### Gaps
- PNAS's own author guidelines on statistics, data and code availability were not retrieved in this session; confirm against the current Information for Authors.
- The Nature Reporting Summary was read from third-party hosted copies of the April 2023 form, not from nature.com.

## Implications for the rank-diffusion model

_Everything below is a proposal for the owner. It changes no frozen specification, threshold or protocol; any adoption would need a dated amendment registered before Wikipedia contact._

### (a) Proposed evaluation suite v2 (freezable)

**Estimands (declare all three).**
- E-pop: the cross-sectional distribution of h-step displacement in log-rank among train-defined universe members, by train-only permanent-rank stratum, with exit as a censored state.
- E-ent: the predictive distribution of each entity's rank at t+h given information at t.
- E-event: the probability that an entity in the top-k at t is outside it (i) at t+h and (ii) at any time within h weeks, for k = K/4 and K/2, plus the corresponding entry probabilities.

**Legacy primary (unchanged): Gate v1**, computed and reported verbatim on every panel, old and new.

**Primary P1, population law (the proper version of the gate).** IQD between the model's pooled predictive CDF of signed log-rank displacement and the realised CDF, at h = 1, 4, 13, exits censored at K+B+1, averaged with equal weight over pre-registered strata (head, upper-middle, lower-middle, tail by train-only permanent rank). Report IQD skill = 1 − IQD_model/IQD_B1. Companion: CRPS of each realised pooled statistic (median, p90, RACF1, coll1/5/20, top-k turnover) under the model's seed-ensemble predictive distribution of that statistic, with fair-CRPS correction.

**Primary P2, entity level.** Mean fair CRPS of log-rank at t+h from per-entity predictive samples (equal sample sizes for model and benchmark), exits censored, stratum-balanced, h = 1, 4, 13. Weekly origins inside each test block with parameters frozen per block.

**Decision rule for P1 and P2.** Fixed sequence h=1 → 4 → 13; at each horizon non-inferiority to B1 at margin δ, then superiority. Inference on paired differences: Ibragimov–Müller t across q non-overlapping blocks (q≥8 where the panel allows, otherwise state q and its attainable p-floor) and, for P2 at h=1, a fixed-b Diebold–Mariano test on the weekly entity-averaged loss differential. "At par" is claimed only if the two-sided 90% interval lies inside (−δ, +δ).

**Margin.** δ = min{ 50% of the lower confidence bound of B1's advantage over the unconditional-mobility reference; B1's adjacent-era replication difference }, both estimated on already-seen platforms and frozen as numbers before Wikipedia contact. Include the assay-sensitivity check (B1 must beat the unconditional reference on the new platform, or the comparison is declared uninformative).

**Secondary (registered, do not gate).**
- Brier and log scores for E-event with CORP reliability diagrams and MCB/DSC/UNC.
- twCRPS with weight on head thresholds (k ≤ K/2).
- Pinball loss at 0.1/0.5/0.9 and coverage plus mean width of central 50/80/95% intervals by horizon and stratum, intervals clustered by week.
- CRPS decomposition (MCB, DSC, UNC) for model and B1.
- Optimal CRPS/log-score pool weight on the model against B1.
- Completeness: share of the B1-to-B4 gap closed by the model, or a statement that B4 does not beat B1.
- Model confidence set over B0–B6 and the model; average-SPA across horizons.
- An e-process on the weekly loss differential for sequential accumulation.
- A reality-check/SPA or specification-curve summary over every specification in the results log.

**Descriptive (no thresholds).** The 15-row card with targeted/untargeted labels, comparator columns and bands; stratified randomised-PIT histograms; variogram score on a pre-registered sample of within-band pairs; small-patch energy scores; W1 with its reference scale; Murphy diagrams.

**Baselines.** B0 no-change with empirical displacement spread; B1 direct h-step rank-bin transition kernel (and the powered one-step kernel as a Markov check); B2 per-entity local level/ETS/ARIMA(0,1,1)/Theta; B3 pooled shrunk AR; B4 global gradient-boosted quantile learner (ceiling); B5 Iñiguez et al. two-parameter model; B6 ablation ladder.

**Before freezing.** (i) Confirm in code how `--dist-scores` builds predictive samples (pooled vs per-entity, ensemble sizes, fair vs standard CRPS). (ii) Run a simulated operating-characteristics study on synthetic panels and already-seen platforms: P(pass | model true), P(pass | each ablation true), P(B1 passes), for v1 and v2. (iii) Replace nothing in v1; add an `|log(sim/emp)|` and a train-scaled variant of the moment error as descriptive columns so the APE issue is visible. (iv) Deposit the protocol with a third-party timestamp.

### (b) What must stay unchanged so earlier registered results remain interpretable
- The Gate v1 definition in full: the scored vector (dRank1/4/13, RACF1, coll1/5/20), the relative-error formula, MOM_FLOOR = 0.02, survivor conditioning, the `+0.05` margin, the ≥60% median-in-CI clause with the IID bootstrap, 20 seeds and 2000 bootstrap draws, the ≥5-split rule, and the train-only membership rule.
- All frozen parameter sets, membership files and their hashes; the frozen confirmation protocol and its amendments; the recorded verdicts (E1–E5; P4–P11), which are never re-adjudicated under v2.
- The tier hierarchy for everything already registered: v2 scores computed on previously seen panels are labelled retrospective/exploratory and carry no confirmatory weight, because those panels have been evaluated many times.
- The 15-row card definition (new labels and comparator columns are additive).
- The definition of the historical-mobility baseline used in v1, kept as a named baseline (it is B0 in pooled form) even after B1–B6 are added.
- On Wikipedia, v1 and v2 are both computed, so the new platform is comparable with the old ones on the old measure (a bridge), and no v2 threshold is derived from Wikipedia data.
