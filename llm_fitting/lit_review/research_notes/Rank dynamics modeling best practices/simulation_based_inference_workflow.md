## 1. SMM / indirect inference best practice — moments, weighting, simulation count, non-smooth (rank) objectives, and the discipline of targeted vs untargeted moments

### Takeaway
The program's closed-form, equally weighted minimum distance on autocovariances is already the form the covariance-structure literature converged on; the missing pieces are formal sampling theory, sensitivity reporting, and a principled rule for when a simulated (rank-level) moment may be promoted to an estimation target. The literature treats untargeted moments as a guard against specification search rather than as wasted information, so promotion should be rare, pre-registered, restricted to parameters that level moments cannot identify, and never applied to the Tier-0 gate statistics.

### Cited Findings

**Foundations and what they demonstrate**
- McFadden introduced the method of simulated moments: replacing an intractable moment with an unbiased simulator keeps the estimator consistent for a fixed number of draws because simulation error averages out across observations, at the cost of a variance inflation of order (1 + 1/S). — [McFadden 1989, Econometrica 57(5):995–1026](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913621) **[M]**
- Pakes & Pollard gave the asymptotics for simulation estimators whose objective is discontinuous in the parameters; the argument relies on stochastic equicontinuity and on holding the underlying random draws fixed as the parameter varies (the theoretical basis for common random numbers). — [Pakes & Pollard 1989, Econometrica 57(5):1027–1057](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913622) **[M]**
- For dynamic (time-series) models, Duffie & Singleton and Lee & Ingram showed SMM is consistent and asymptotically normal with covariance inflated by (1 + 1/tau), tau = ratio of simulated to observed sample length. — [Duffie & Singleton 1993, Econometrica 61(4):929–952](https://doi.org/10.2307/2951768) **[M]**; [Lee & Ingram 1991, J. Econometrics 47:197–205](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-4076(91)90098-X) **[M]**
- Indirect inference generalises SMM: match the parameters of an auxiliary model (not necessarily correctly specified) estimated on real and simulated data; the same (1 + 1/H) simulation inflation applies. — [Gouriéroux, Monfort & Renault 1993, J. Appl. Econometrics 8:S85–S118](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.3950080507) **[M]**; [Smith 1993, J. Appl. Econometrics 8:S63–S84](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.3950080506) **[M]**
- Gallant & Tauchen's EMM answers "which moments": use the score of a rich auxiliary (semi-nonparametric) model as the moment vector; if the auxiliary model nests the structural model the estimator attains ML efficiency. — [Gallant & Tauchen 1996, Econometric Theory 12(4):657–681](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0266466600006976) **[M]**
- Monte Carlo comparisons of SMM / II / EMM find none dominates; performance depends on the auxiliary statistics chosen. — [Michaelides & Ng 2000, J. Econometrics 96(2):231–266](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4076(99)00058-5) **[M]**

**Weighting**
- Altonji & Segal: optimally weighted minimum distance for covariance structures is seriously biased in small samples (and in fairly large samples for badly behaved distributions) because sampling error in the second moments is correlated with sampling error in the weight matrix; equally weighted MD generally has lower RMSE; a split-sample "independently weighted" fix is unbiased but usually still dominated by equal weighting. — [Altonji & Segal 1996, JBES 14(3):353–366; NBER t0156](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0156) **[V, abstract]**
- Honoré, Jørgensen & de Paula note that diagonal weighting by inverse moment variances is the common applied practice motivated by Altonji–Segal, and that identity weights are not invariant to moment units. — [Honoré, Jørgensen & de Paula 2020, J. Appl. Econometrics 35(7):797–813 (arXiv:1907.02101, p. 6 fn. 3)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02101) **[V, full text pp. 1–9]**
- A current working paper (R&R at REStud as of its January 2026 version) proposes cross-fitting plus a regularised (graphical-lasso) weight matrix for MD, motivated by and applied to earnings-dynamics models, and reports simulation gains over the usual weighting schemes. — [Cheng, Sánchez-Becerra & Shephard, "How to Weight in Moments Matching" (2023; rev. 2026)](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/node/15540) **[V, abstract/listing only]**
- Cocci & Plagborg-Møller: when the correlation structure across empirical moments is unknown (e.g., moments from different data sets), valid worst-case standard errors and confidence intervals can be built from the individual moment variances alone; in the over-identified case the weighting that minimises worst-case variance reduces to a moment-selection problem; over-identification tests are also provided. — [Cocci & Plagborg-Møller 2025, Review of Economic Studies 92(5):2952–2978](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdae099) **[V, abstract]**

**Non-smooth objectives (the rank problem)**
- Generalized indirect inference smooths the simulated discrete outcome (a smoothed function of latent utilities, bandwidth shrinking to zero) so gradient methods work while consistency and the II limit distribution are retained; Monte Carlo shows near-ML efficiency with a rich auxiliary model. — [Bruins, Duffy, Keane & Smith (arXiv:1507.06115; J. Econometrics 205(1):177–203, 2018 **[M] for journal details**)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1507.06115) **[V, abstract]**
- An alternative removes the discontinuity by a change of variables in the simulation algorithm, avoiding kernel smoothing and bandwidth choice. — [Frazier, Oka & Zhu 2019, J. Econometrics 212(2):623–645](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2019.06.003) **[V, abstract]**
- Chernozhukov & Hong's Laplace-type (quasi-Bayesian) estimators treat exp(−n·Q_n(theta)) times a prior as a quasi-posterior and compute its mean/median by MCMC, avoiding optimisation of non-smooth or non-convex criteria; quasi-posterior quantiles are valid confidence intervals only when the generalised information equality holds (efficient weighting) — otherwise a sandwich correction is required. — [Chernozhukov & Hong 2003, J. Econometrics 115(2):293–346](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4076(03)00100-3) **[M]**
- Forneron & Ng place SMM/II, ABC and the Laplace-type estimator in one framework and compare their bias properties. — [Forneron & Ng 2018, J. Econometrics 205(1):112–139](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2018.03.007) **[M]**
- Creel's Monte Carlo evidence: MCMC-based (Chernozhukov–Hong-style) SMM confidence intervals can have coverage far from nominal with over-identifying raw statistics; coverage was correct in the cases studied when the statistics were first reduced by a neural net to exactly-identifying "neural moments" and a continuously-updated criterion was used. — [Creel 2021, Econometrics 9(4):35](https://ddd.uab.cat/record/249868?ln=en) **[V, via search summary]**
- Bayesian optimisation for likelihood-free inference (BOLFI) models the discrepancy surface with a Gaussian process and was reported to cut the number of simulations by orders of magnitude. — [Gutmann & Corander 2016, JMLR 17(125)](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-017.html) **[M]**

**Targeted vs untargeted moments, holdouts, calibration critiques**
- Hansen & Heckman's critique of calibration: parameters imported or matched without accounting for sampling error, and the split between "calibration" moments and "verification" moments, need explicit statistical justification. — [Hansen & Heckman 1996, JEP 10(1):87–104](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.10.1.87) **[M]**; the position being criticised: [Kydland & Prescott 1996, JEP 10(1):69–85](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.10.1.69) **[M]**
- Schorfheide & Wolpin: from a pure Bayesian standpoint holdout samples are suboptimal (use all data for posterior model probabilities), but in a principal–agent setting where the modeller can data-mine, a holdout is a rational device to discourage specification search; they study how large the holdout should be. — [Schorfheide & Wolpin, "To Hold Out or Not to Hold Out" (NBER w19565; Research in Economics 70(2):332–345, 2016)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19565) **[V, abstract]**; [Schorfheide & Wolpin 2012, AER P&P 102(3):477–481](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.102.3.477) **[V, listing]**
- Canonical holdout validations of structural models: estimate on controls / pre-period and predict the withheld experimental or non-random holdout group. — [Todd & Wolpin 2006, AER 96(5):1384–1417](https://doi.org/10.1257/aer.96.5.1384) **[M]**; [Keane & Wolpin 2007, IER 48(4):1351–1378](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-2354.2007.00465.x) **[M]**; overview of validation by untargeted moments and holdouts: [Low & Meghir 2017, JEP 31(2):33–58](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.31.2.33) **[M]**
- Dridi, Guay & Renault formalise calibration as indirect inference with "partial encompassing": a deliberately misspecified structural model should be asked to match only the features it is designed to explain, with nuisance features handled separately. — [Dridi, Guay & Renault 2007, J. Econometrics 136(2):397–430](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2005.11.003) **[M]**
- Nakamura & Steinsson argue for targeting "identified moments" — statistics that are sharply informative about one mechanism and portable across models — rather than large unstructured moment sets. — [Nakamura & Steinsson 2018, JEP 32(3):59–86](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.32.3.59) **[M]**
- Hnatkovska, Marmer & Tang give Vuong-type tests for comparing misspecified calibrated models by minimum-distance fit, explicitly covering the case where parameters are fitted to one set of moments and the model is evaluated on another set. — [Hnatkovska, Marmer & Tang 2012, J. Econometrics 169(1):131–138](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2012.01.007) **[V, abstract]**
- Practical SMM guidance from corporate finance: justify each moment by a comparative-static plot of moment vs parameter; SMM test statistics can have poor finite-sample behaviour; estimates are sensitive to moment choice, simulation count and weighting when benchmarked against ML. — [Strebulaev & Whited 2012, Found. Trends Finance 6(1–2)](https://doi.org/10.1561/0500000035) **[M]**; [Bazdresch, Kahn & Whited 2018, RFS 31(1):322–361](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhx080) **[M]**; [Eisenhauer, Heckman & Mosso 2015, IER 56(2):331–357](https://doi.org/10.1111/iere.12107) **[M]**

**Closest methodological precedent: permanent/transitory earnings processes**
- The earnings-dynamics literature estimates permanent + transitory (+ measurement error) variance components by equally weighted MD on autocovariances — the same estimator class as this program. A well-documented pathology is that fitting moments in levels vs in growth rates gives sharply different permanent/transitory variances. — [Heathcote, Perri & Violante 2010, RED 13(1):15–51](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.red.2009.10.010) **[M]**
- Daly, Hryshko & Manovskii trace that levels-vs-differences divergence to observations at the start and end of contiguous spells (lower mean, higher variance next to missing observations); a small modification of the canonical process that accounts for those edge observations largely reconciles the estimates. — [Daly, Hryshko & Manovskii, NBER w22938 (IER 63(1):95–124, 2022)](https://www.nber.org/papers/w22938) **[V, abstract]**
- When Gaussian permanent/transitory models fail on tails, that literature moved to SMM on quantile-type tail moments: Guvenen, Ozkan & Song targeted the mean, P10, P50 and P90 of 1-, 3- and 5-year log earnings changes (372 moments) with an identity weight matrix. — [Guvenen, Ozkan & Song 2014 JPE appendix, as reported in search summary](https://nbviewer.jupyter.org/github/serdarozkan/papers/blob/master/gos_2014_appendixc.pdf) **[V, via search summary only]**; the 2021 Econometrica paper fits processes of increasing generality (normal-mixture innovations in persistent and transitory components plus nonemployment shocks) to a rich set of higher-order and impulse-response moments. — [Guvenen, Karahan, Ozkan & Song 2021, Econometrica 89(5):2303–2339](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA14603) **[V, abstract; exact target list not verified]**
- Heterogeneity in variances across units (the analogue of the amplitude v_i) is handled in that literature by simulated minimum distance / indirect inference on unit-level auxiliary statistics. — [Browning, Ejrnæs & Alvarez 2010, REStud 77(4):1353–1381](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-937X.2010.00612.x) **[M]**; [Meghir & Pistaferri 2004, Econometrica 72(1):1–32](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2004.00476.x) **[M]**

### Inferences
- **Current practice is defensible and should be described in these terms in the paper**: "equally weighted minimum distance on a covariance structure (Chamberlain/Abowd–Card tradition), equal weights per Altonji–Segal". The "weighting audit found nil effect" result is what Altonji–Segal would predict when moments are many and precisely estimated.
- **Simulation count is not the binding constraint for estimation.** With the program's >= 20 seeds the (1 + 1/S) inflation is <= 5%. The >= 20-seed rule matters for head statistics because their seed distribution is heavy-tailed, which is a different problem (non-Gaussian summary distribution) from the SMM inflation factor.
- **Rank non-smoothness is milder here than in discrete-choice SMM.** Pooled rank statistics average over N·T ~ 10^5–10^6 entity-weeks, so under common random numbers the simulated objective is a step function with very fine steps; the practical obstacle is simulation noise and heavy-tailed head statistics, not discontinuity. Derivative-free search or a Chernozhukov–Hong quasi-posterior on a low-dimensional parameter block with CRNs should suffice; soft-rank smoothing is optional (see §9). This is an inference, not something a cited source shows for rank panels.
- **A promotion rule consistent with "declared moments, no tuning"** (synthesising Schorfheide–Wolpin, Dridi–Guay–Renault, Nakamura–Steinsson, Hnatkovska et al.):
  1. Tier-0 gate statistics (held-out |dRank| quantiles, rank ACF, head collision rates) are never estimation targets — they are the holdout that disciplines specification search.
  2. A rank-level statistic may be promoted only to identify a parameter of a NEW mechanism that no latent-level moment identifies, declared before data contact, one statistic per parameter where possible (just-identified, so no weighting freedom).
  3. Prefer a latent-level analogue first. For the established-departure deficit the natural declared moments are level-based tail statistics by permanent-rank band — exceedance rates and P10/P90 of h-week log changes, and the conditional reversal of large long differences — exactly the Guvenen-style quantile moments. Rank-level departure and return rates then stay untargeted.
  4. If a rank-level statistic must be promoted (e.g., boundary flux for the exit/rebirth machinery), estimate it on the training window only and keep the same statistic's held-out value in the gate; report its AGS informativeness for every parameter (§2).
- **Statistics ranked by suitability for promotion** (my assessment): established-departure and return rates — best handled via level tail moments, keep rank version as validation; boundary flux — legitimate target for the entry/exit block (it is the estimand of that block); displacement quantiles — do not promote (they are the gate); head shares — do not promote (dominated by a handful of entities, non-Gaussian sampling distribution, sign of the residual differs by platform).
- **Levels-vs-differences is a free specification test the program does not yet run.** The estimator uses change autocovariances and long-difference variances; the earnings literature shows that a disagreement between level-based and difference-based fits is diagnostic of mis-modelled spell edges and of the persistent component. The Daly–Hryshko–Manovskii mechanism (anomalous observations adjacent to gaps) maps directly onto weeks adjacent to temporary absences, which the fact sheet identifies as 95–98% of empirical absences.

### Gaps
- No source was found that develops SMM theory specifically for rank functionals of a simulated cross-section; the smoothness claim above is reasoning by analogy.
- The exact targeted-moment list and estimator in Guvenen et al. (2021) could not be confirmed (search results conflicted on SMM vs GMM); verify before citing as precedent.
- Cheng, Sánchez-Becerra & Shephard was seen only as a listing/abstract; its finite-sample claims were not checked.
- I could not verify the published text of Hansen & Heckman, Kydland & Prescott, Dridi et al., Low & Meghir in this session (all [M]).

## 2. Identification and sensitivity diagnostics for moment-based estimation; a per-parameter "which moments identify this" table

### Takeaway
A full sensitivity/identification report is nearly free for this model because the estimator is closed-form and (conditionally) linear: the Andrews–Gentzkow–Shapiro sensitivity matrix is the NNLS pseudo-inverse on the active set. Reporting it, together with drop-a-moment-group precision measures, the informativeness of each validation statistic, and weak-identification-robust sets for the two nonlinear parameters (kappa, phi), would convert "every parameter comes from a declared moment" from an assertion into a table.

### Cited Findings
- AGS sensitivity: for a minimum-distance/GMM estimator with moment Jacobian G and weight W, Lambda = −(G'WG)^(-1)G'W; under local misspecification of the moments the first-order asymptotic bias of the estimator is Lambda times the moment perturbation, and the asymptotic variance under correct specification is Lambda·S·Lambda'. Lambda is computable at negligible cost. — [Andrews, Gentzkow & Shapiro 2017, QJE 132(4):1553–1592](https://doi.org/10.1093/qje/qjx023) **[V, abstract and secondary restatements]**; formula confirmed in [Honoré et al., arXiv:1907.02101, p. 3](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02101) **[V, full text]**
- AGS informativeness: the informativeness Delta of a vector of descriptive statistics for a structural estimate is the R-squared from regressing the estimate on the statistics under their joint asymptotic distribution; the worst-case bias under local misspecification that respects the model's link to those statistics shrinks by the factor sqrt(1 − Delta); authors recommend reporting it. — [Andrews, Gentzkow & Shapiro 2020, Econometrica 88(6):2231–2258](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA16768) **[V, abstract and published comments]**
- Honoré–Jørgensen–de Paula measures (all from quantities already computed for standard errors): M2 = derivative of the efficient-GMM variance with respect to noise in moment k; M3 = the same holding the actual (non-optimal) weights fixed, equal to M1·O_kk·M1'; M4 = change in asymptotic variance from dropping moment k (or a group) with remaining weights unchanged; M5 = the same with optimal re-weighting; M6 = derivative with respect to the weight on moment k (zero at optimal weights, so it shows whether a chosen weight is too low or too high). Scaled versions are elasticities / relative variance changes. Extremely large M4 signals that the parameter is not identified without that moment. — [Honoré, Jørgensen & de Paula 2020, arXiv:1907.02101, pp. 3–6](https://arxiv.org/abs/1907.02101) **[V, full text]**
- Armstrong & Kolesár: confidence intervals that remain valid when moments are locally misspecified within a user-specified set, with the optimal weighting trading precision against each moment's potential misspecification (so a suspect moment is down-weighted rather than dropped). — [Armstrong & Kolesár 2021, Quantitative Economics 12(1):77–108](https://doi.org/10.3982/QE1609) **[V, abstract/secondary]**
- New (Aug–Sep 2026) preprint: with many over-identifying moments contaminated by small exchangeable specification errors, the mean and variance of those errors can be estimated and used for a bias-corrected and empirical-Bayes GMM with misspecification-aware inference. — [Kline 2026, arXiv:2608.23925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.23925) **[V, abstract]**
- Local identification checks without data: rank of the Jacobian of the map from structural parameters to model-implied first/second moments (Iskrev), or rank conditions on the spectral density / minimal state-space representation (Komunjer & Ng). — [Iskrev 2010, J. Monetary Economics 57(2):189–202](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoneco.2009.12.007) **[M]**; [Komunjer & Ng 2011, Econometrica 79(6):1995–2032](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA8916) **[M]**
- Flat or ridge-shaped objectives as the signature of weak/partial identification, with the recommendation to plot the objective in each direction. — [Canova & Sala 2009, J. Monetary Economics 56(4):431–449](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmoneco.2009.03.014) **[M]**
- Forneron's quasi-Jacobian: the slope of a linear approximation to the moments over an estimate of the identified set is asymptotically singular under local or global identification failure and equals the usual Jacobian under strong identification; its eigenvalues detect failure and indicate its span; a chi-square subvector test follows. — [Forneron 2024, J. Econometrics 238(1):105552 (arXiv:1907.13093)](https://export.arxiv.org/abs/1907.13093) **[V, abstract]**
- Weak-identification-robust inference: confidence sets obtained by inverting the continuously-updated objective (S-sets) are valid regardless of identification strength; conditional QLR-type tests extend this to nonlinear GMM. — [Stock & Wright 2000, Econometrica 68(5):1055–1096](https://doi.org/10.1111/1468-0262.00151) **[M]**; [Andrews & Mikusheva 2016, Econometrica 84(4):1571–1612](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA12868) **[M]**
- Profile-likelihood identifiability analysis (structural vs practical non-identifiability read off the shape of profiles) and the "sloppy model" observation that parameter sensitivities in multi-parameter dynamical models span many orders of magnitude. — [Raue et al. 2009, Bioinformatics 25(15):1923–1929](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp358) **[M]**; [Gutenkunst et al. 2007, PLoS Comput. Biol. 3(10):e189](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.0030189) **[M]**
- Heavy tails break the standard sampling theory for autocovariances: with infinite fourth moments, sample autocovariances/autocorrelations have non-Gaussian limits and slower rates. — [Davis & Mikosch 1998, Annals of Statistics 26(5):2049–2080](https://doi.org/10.1214/aos/1024691368) **[M]**
- A preprint on earnings growth (title: "Non-Existent Moments of Earnings Growth") works with quantile-based Kelly skewness and Crow–Siddiqui kurtosis in place of moment-based measures. — [arXiv:2203.08014](https://arxiv.org/abs/2203.08014) **[V, title and definitions via search snippet only]**

### Inferences
**Recipe for the per-parameter identification table (all steps closed-form or cheap for this model).**
1. **Write the moment map.** For band z, conditional on (kappa_z, phi), the change autocovariances gamma_k(z) and long-difference variances D_h(z) are linear in (sigma_perm^2(z), sigma_trans^2(z), sigma_obs^2(z)) times E[v^2]. Stack them: m(theta) = A(kappa, phi)·beta. G = [A, dA/dkappa·beta, dA/dphi·beta] is analytic.
2. **Estimate S, the covariance of the sample moments**, by a two-way (entity-block and week-block) bootstrap by permanent-rank band. Because the fast innovations are Student-t with possibly fewer than four finite moments, do not rely on the plug-in fourth-moment formula; report the bootstrap and, as a robustness check, a subsampling/m-out-of-n version (Davis–Mikosch).
3. **Sensitivity matrix.** Lambda = −(G'WG)^(-1)G'W with the actual (identity/diagonal) W, restricted to the NNLS active set. Report Lambda scaled so entry (j,k) is "change in parameter j, in its own SE units, per 1-SE change in moment k". This is the "which moments identify this" table. Group columns as {gamma_0}, {gamma_1..gamma_6}, {D(h) short}, {D(h) long}, {Spec-B daily-replication floor}, {cross-entity dispersion of log change-variance}, {within-entity tail statistic}.
4. **Precision attribution.** Honoré E4 (drop-a-group) for each group above: by how much does each parameter's variance rise if that group is removed? Parameters whose E4 explodes when one group is dropped are identified by that group alone — expected for kappa (long D(h)) and sigma_obs (Spec-B).
5. **Local identification strength.** Singular values and condition number of the scaled G per band; flag bands where the smallest singular value direction mixes sigma_perm and kappa (the classic persistence-vs-innovation ridge) or sigma_trans and sigma_obs (resolved only by the instrument).
6. **Nonlinear parameters: profile and robust sets.** For kappa_z and phi, compute the concentrated MD objective on a grid (NNLS inner solve) and report (a) the profile curve, (b) the S-set {theta : n·Q(theta) <= chi-square critical value} using bootstrap S. A flat profile or an S-set spanning an order of magnitude is the "flat SSE" diagnosis stated as a confidence set.
7. **Informativeness of validation statistics.** By simulation (parametric bootstrap from the fitted model, re-estimating each replicate), regress each parameter estimate on the vector of Tier-0/Tier-1 rank statistics and report Delta (AGS 2020). Low Delta for the gate statistics is the desirable outcome — it documents that the gate is close to an independent check; high Delta would mean the gate is nearly a restatement of estimation moments.
8. **Local-misspecification bias budget.** Feed the known residuals through Lambda: e.g., the 13-week variance-ratio residual (0.03–0.05) implies a first-order shift in kappa and sigma_perm of Lambda·delta. This turns each documented residual into an implied parameter bias, in the Armstrong–Kolesár spirit.

**Model-specific readings.**
- kappa is identified almost entirely from long-difference variances. On Facebook Era A (T ~ 88) D(52) has fewer than two non-overlapping windows per entity, so kappa is likely weakly identified there; the reported 4–8x era shifts in kappa should be re-examined against S-sets before being interpreted as non-stationarity. (Inference; no source tests this on these data.)
- The t degrees of freedom is currently taken from within-entity kurtosis. Kurtosis does not exist for df <= 4 and its estimator is extremely noisy for df slightly above 4, so this declared moment is the weakest link in the identification table; a quantile-ratio tail statistic (Crow–Siddiqui type) or the score of a t likelihood is the standard remedy. Changing a declared moment is owner-gated.
- Two-step structure: sigma_obs is pinned in a first step from different data (daily). Standard errors for the second-step parameters should include first-step uncertainty; because cross-source moment correlations are hard to estimate, Cocci–Plagborg-Møller worst-case standard errors apply directly.
- NNLS active constraints put some band values on the boundary (zero variance); at those bands the asymptotic distribution is non-normal and Lambda is not defined in the usual way — report the share of bands at the boundary and treat them separately (the program already knows the pile-up literature).

## 3. Likelihood-based alternatives for the latent-level model (ignoring ranks): robust/score-driven filters, particle methods, iterated filtering, Whittle, composite likelihood, and variance random effects at N ~ 10^4

### Takeaway
A latent-level panel quasi-likelihood is a worthwhile cross-check on the MD estimates — it uses all lags, handles missing weeks exactly, and gives standard errors — but the ecology comparison literature warns that state-space likelihoods are the more fragile option under misspecification, so it should be a cross-check and a forecasting-filter upgrade, not the adjudicator. At this scale the feasible routes are a Kalman/Whittle quasi-likelihood with the amplitude integrated by one-dimensional quadrature, or Laplace-approximate marginal likelihood (TMB-style); full MCMC or particle methods over ~10^6–10^7 latent states are not needed and not advisable.

### Cited Findings
- Score-driven (GAS/DCS) models update time-varying parameters by the scaled score of the conditional density; they are observation-driven, so the likelihood is available in closed form. — [Creal, Koopman & Lucas 2013, J. ### Inferences
- **What a latent-level likelihood would look like here.** Conditional on v_i and band parameters, entity i is a scalar-observation linear state-space model with 2–3 AR(1) states. The amplitude enters only as a scale, so the per-entity marginal likelihood is a one-dimensional integral over log v_i: L_i = integral of KF-likelihood(y_i | v) dLogNormal(v; s). With ~20 Gauss–Hermite nodes this is ~N·T·20 ~ 4·10^7 scalar Kalman steps per likelihood evaluation — seconds on a CPU. That is marginal-likelihood empirical Bayes for s, with posterior modes of v_i as a by-product. No MCMC, no Laplace approximation, no latent-state sampling needed.
- **Equivalent frequency-domain form.** The spectrum of the change series is a sum of three known rational spectra scaled by v_i^2 plus the differenced-white-noise spectrum of eps. gamma_0..gamma_6 and D(h) are linear functionals of that spectrum; a (debiased) Whittle likelihood uses the whole periodogram instead of ~15 functionals. With T = 88–213 it offers 44–106 frequencies per entity, pooled within band.
- **What it buys:** (i) an efficiency benchmark — if QML and MD standard errors are similar, MD is not leaving information on the table; (ii) exact handling of missing weeks (the Kalman filter skips them), which matters because 95–98% of absences are temporary and edge observations are a known source of bias in MD on differences (§1); (iii) a likelihood-ratio route for nested profile restrictions (§7); (iv) filtered states with honest uncertainty for forecasts.
- **What it risks:** a Gaussian quasi-likelihood is dominated by Student-t outliers; variance components pile up at zero; under misspecification the likelihood surface can be multimodal (Fasiolo et al.). A full t-likelihood needs either scale-mixture augmentation (state dimension explodes) or a score-driven approximation. The likelihood must still be organised by PERMANENT rank band — banding by current rank would re-introduce the program's Eulerian selection bias.
- **Forecasting filter upgrade is the higher-value half.** The scalar steady-state Kalman filter used for conditional forecasts treats a t-distributed spike as information about the home level. A Harvey–Luati t-score filter down-weights spikes automatically. Since proper-score skill versus persistence is ~0 (frontier item 4), replacing the forecast filter is a clean, single-change experiment whose parameters (df, steady-state gains) are still derived from the declared moments.
- **Scale limits (my reading, not benchmarked):** Laplace/TMB on the joint latent-state model would have ~N·T·(2–3) ~ 4–8·10^6 random effects — at the top of TMB's stated design range but block-separable by entity; feasible, yet unnecessary given the quadrature route. Full-Bayes HMC over all latent states (Stan/NumPyro) is impractical; HMC over (hyperparameters + 10^4 amplitudes) with states marginalised by the Kalman filter is feasible but slow and adds little over empirical Bayes. PMCMC and panel iterated filtering are designed for nonlinear, low-N panels (tens of units) and are the wrong tool for a conditionally linear model with 10^4 units. INLA is not a natural fit for a scale random effect multiplying the state innovations.
- **Parameter non-stationarity (frontier item 3):** score-driven recursions are the standard likelihood-based way to let a variance-type parameter drift. The program has already refuted a common time-varying volatility factor, so this is noted only as the right framework if a drifting amplitude spread s_t is ever re-opened with new evidence.

### Gaps
- No published benchmark was found for hierarchical scale-random-effect state-space models at N ~ 10^4, T ~ 200; the feasibility statements are back-of-envelope.
- I did not verify NumPyro/Stan/INLA scale limits against documentation in this session.
- Harvey–Luati full text (equations) not retrieved; the redescending-response description comes from the working paper and a secondary discussion.

## 4. Neural simulation-based inference, with emphasis on misspecification

### Takeaway
For a model that is known to be misspecified in specific, documented ways, the neural-SBI literature itself says standard NPE/NLE cannot be relied on: networks extrapolate when observed summaries fall outside the simulated distribution, and posteriors become overconfident or erratic. Neural SBI is clearly inferior to transparent moment matching for this program's core estimation (closed-form moments, ~500 separable parameters, identification transparency as a deliverable); its legitimate uses are narrow — amortised posteriors on DECLARED summaries for low-dimensional mechanism blocks, robust variants whose adjustment parameters localise misspecification, and cheap simulation-based calibration.

### Cited Findings

**State of the toolkit**
- Review of the field (NPE/NLE/NRE, amortised vs sequential). — [Cranmer, Brehmer & Louppe 2020, PNAS 117(48):30055–30062](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1912789117) **[M]**
- The `sbi` package (PyTorch) was substantially extended; the 2025 JOSS paper covers releases >= 0.23 and documents inference methods, architectures, samplers and diagnostics. — [Boelts, Deistler et al. 2025, JOSS 10(108):7754](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.07754) **[V]**; original: [Tejero-Cantero et al. 2020, JOSS 5(52):2505](https://doi.org/10.21105/joss.02505) **[V, cited in the 2025 record]**
- A 2025 practitioner guide from the sbi developers: check before training that the observation lies within the prior-predictive range of the summaries; fit an unconditional density to simulations and flag observations with very low density; hand-crafted summaries built from domain knowledge that the model can reproduce help against misspecification; posterior predictive checks are necessary but not sufficient; global coverage checks (expected coverage, SBC, TARP) are necessary but not sufficient and can be passed by the prior; local checks (L-C2ST, posterior SBC) are more powerful but costlier; most successful applications used at least a few thousand simulations; across 106 published applications there was no significant correlation between parameter count and simulation count; "dozens of parameters" is treated as the hard regime. **Declared summaries vs learned summaries; economics practice**

### Inferences
- **Where neural SBI is clearly inferior to transparent moment matching for this program** (each point tied to a cited limitation):
  1. *Known, structured misspecification* (departure deficit, head law, drifting s). Standard NPE/NLE have no theory here and fail empirically (Cannon; Kelly et al.); the MD estimator's pseudo-true value, by contrast, is explicit — it is whatever reproduces the declared autocovariances — and its sensitivity to each residual is computable (§2).
  2. *Parameter dimension.* ~500 band values is far beyond the "dozens of parameters" regime. MD exploits separability by band; a network would have to learn it.
  3. *Closed-form moments exist.* SBI's reason for being is an intractable likelihood/moment map; for the latent-level block the map is analytic.
  4. *Learned summaries defeat the discipline.* An embedding network trained end-to-end will use whatever features predict parameters under the simulator, including features the real data violate (tails, absences). That is tuning to a score by another name. Huang et al.'s penalty is a patch; declared summaries are the principled solution and are what the economics neural-estimator papers do.
  5. *Rank-level raw data are large.* One simulated panel is ~10^6 numbers; training on tens of thousands of panels with a set/sequence embedding is expensive, while the program's summaries are already ~10^2–10^3 numbers.
- **Where it can help without breaking the discipline:**
  - *Amortised posterior on declared summaries for a low-dimensional mechanism block* (e.g., 3–6 scalars of a returnable-jump or exit/return mechanism, band profiles frozen at MD values). The network is only a fast approximation to the map from pre-registered summaries to parameters (Wei–Jiang / Creel logic); it yields uncertainty quantification that SMM point estimates lack.
  - *Robust variants as misspecification localisers.* RNPE/RSNL/RBSL adjustment parameters on interpretable, declared rank-level summaries give a posterior statement of which summaries the simulator cannot reach — a formal counterpart of the fact sheet's residual list.
  - *Out-of-simulator check.* A Schmitt-style MMD (or the simpler density-of-observed-summaries check from the sbi guide) quantifies how far each platform's observed summary vector lies from the simulator's typical set — a single scalar "misspecification distance" per platform/metric that can be tracked as mechanisms are added.
  - *Amortisation makes SBC, coverage and model-recovery confusion matrices (§6) cheap*, because thousands of synthetic datasets can be inverted in one pass.
- **Preconditions before any neural-SBI result is reported:** prior predictive check that observed summaries are inside the simulated range; SBC with a data-dependent test quantity; TARP or expected coverage; L-C2ST at the observed summaries; posterior predictive check on untargeted statistics; and agreement with the MD/SMM point estimate on the same summaries. Disagreement is a finding about the approximation, not about the model.

- The ICLR 2026 and ICML 2026 items were seen only as conference listings; authors and results were not verified.
- No study was found that applies neural SBI to rank-dynamics or heavy-tailed panel variance-components models; nor a head-to-head of neural SBI vs moment matching on a deliberately misspecified structural economic model.

## 5. Synthetic likelihood and ABC for dynamic population models; ecology's state-space practice

### Takeaway
Synthetic likelihood on declared summaries is the method family closest in spirit to the program's discipline — it is "SMM with a Gaussian likelihood on pre-chosen statistics" — and its robust variant turns each declared summary into a misspecification test. Ecology's state-space practice independently endorses the program's two key design choices: fix observation variance from external replication, and verify estimability by simulate-and-refit.

### Cited Findings
- Wood's synthetic likelihood: for noisy nonlinear dynamics whose likelihood is intractable or pathological, reduce data to phase-insensitive summary statistics (autocovariances, regression coefficients describing dynamics, marginal distribution shape), assume the statistics are multivariate normal with mean and covariance estimated by simulation at each parameter value, and explore by MCMC; normality of the statistics should be checked. — [Wood 2010, Nature 466:1102–1104](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature09319) **[M]**
- Bayesian synthetic likelihood is less sensitive to tuning than ABC and scales better in the number of summaries; the posterior is fairly insensitive to the number of simulations per iteration. — [Price, Drovandi, Lee & Nott 2018, JCGS 27(1):1–11](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2017.1302882) **[M]**
- With well-behaved summaries the BSL posterior is asymptotically normal with correct coverage and BSL is computationally more efficient than ABC; adjustments exist for a misspecified working covariance (diagonal/factor), but they do not address non-Gaussian summaries or incompatibility. — [Frazier, Nott, Drovandi & Kohn 2023, JASA 118(544):2821–2832 (arXiv:1902.04827)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.04827) **[V, abstract]**
- Robust BSL: when no parameter value reproduces the observed summaries, standard BSL is unreliable; adding mean-adjustment parameters (RBSL-M, Laplace prior, scaled by the simulated SD of each summary) or variance-inflation parameters (RBSL-V) restores reliable inference and identifies which summaries are incompatible. Implemented in the R package `BSL`. — [Frazier & Drovandi 2021, JCGS 30(4):958–976 (arXiv:1904.04551)](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04551) **[V, abstract and package docs]**
- Under misspecification the BSL posterior can be multimodal and asymptotically non-Gaussian; likelihood tempering does not fix it, while the robust adjustments do. — [Frazier, Nott & Drovandi 2025, JASA 120(550):884–895 (arXiv:2104.03436)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.03436) **[V, abstract]**
- Semi-parametric BSL relaxes the Gaussian assumption on summary marginals. — [An, Nott & Drovandi 2020, Statistics and Computing 30:543–557](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-019-09904-x) **[M]**
- Ecology's state-space guide: structural non-identifiability cannot be cured by data; structural identifiability does not guarantee estimability with the data in hand; missing/sparse data can cause non-identifiability; the practical check is to simulate from known parameters, refit, and compare (non-identifiable models give biased, high-variance estimates); replicated observations help separate process variation from observation error, improve estimates, and improve selection of the observation distribution — with the caution that replicates are often not independent because shared conditions correlate them. — [Auger-Méthé et al. 2021, Ecological Monographs 91(4):e01470 (arXiv:2002.02001)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02001) **[V, preprint excerpts]**
- Replicated sampling improves information about observation variance. — [Dennis, Ponciano & Taper 2010, Ecology 91(2):610–620](https://doi.org/10.1890/08-1095.1) **[V, bibliographic record and secondary descriptions only]**
- Fixing observation variance at an externally estimated value, rather than estimating it jointly, reduced bias and standard errors in simulations; jointly estimating it produced implausibly small process variance in the motivating case. — [Polansky et al. 2021 (PMC7984174)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7984174) **[V, via search summary]**
- A 2025 preprint argues linear stochastic state-space models in ecology are generally structurally identifiable unless compartments are unobserved, so fitting problems are mostly practical identifiability. — [arXiv:2508.08714](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.08714) **[V, via search summary only]**
- Recommended sequencing from the Fasiolo et al. comparison: summary-based (synthetic-likelihood) methods for development and checking, state-space likelihood for final inference when the model is trusted. — [Fasiolo, Pya & Wood 2016](https://doi.org/10.1214/15-STS534) **[V]**

### Inferences
- **Spec-B is ecological best practice.** Pinning sigma_obs from within-week daily replication is the "replicated observations / externally estimated observation variance" remedy. The one caution the ecology guide adds applies directly: daily replicates within a week share conditions (news cycles, platform outages), so replicate errors are positively correlated and a within-week noise floor may under- or over-state weekly measurement noise. The fact sheet says Spec-B is "pinned/bounded"; treating it as a bound rather than a point is the conservative reading the literature supports.
- **Synthetic likelihood fits the program's summaries, with one exception.** Pooled rank-movement statistics are averages over many entity-weeks and should be near-Gaussian across seeds; head statistics (top-1 share, head collision counts) are driven by a few entities and a lognormal-times-t product tail and will not be. For those, use quantile or log transforms, a semi-parametric BSL, or keep them out of the synthetic likelihood.
- **RBSL as the formal residual list.** Run RBSL-M with the mechanism block free and band profiles frozen; summaries = the declared estimation moments for that block plus selected untargeted rank-level statistics entered with adjustment parameters. Adjustment posteriors that stay at their Laplace prior mark compatible summaries; those that move mark incompatibility, with a magnitude in simulated-SD units. Expect the established-departure rate and top-1 share to move; the interesting question is which others do.
- **Cost realism.** BSL needs n simulations per MCMC iteration (typically tens to hundreds) for thousands of iterations; a full-panel simulation must therefore take well under a second, which implies a vectorised/GPU simulator (§9) or an emulator.

### Gaps

## 6. Parameter-recovery and model-recovery studies as standard workflow

### Takeaway
Recovery studies are treated as mandatory in the computational-modelling and Bayesian-workflow literatures, and the missing piece in this program is model recovery of the whole pipeline: a confusion matrix showing how often the frozen estimation-plus-gate procedure attributes data generated under mechanism A to mechanism B, including under realistic misspecification.

### Cited Findings
- Wilson & Collins: parameter recovery (simulate with known parameters, refit, compare) before fitting real data; model recovery via a confusion matrix whose entries are P(model j fits best | data simulated from model i); recovery depends on the parameter ranges simulated — high noise makes models indistinguishable — so simulate in the empirically relevant range; an "inversion matrix" is offered for interpreting a fitted winner when the generating model is unknown; and always validate by simulating the fitted model and checking it reproduces the qualitative signatures. — [Wilson & Collins 2019, eLife 8:e49547](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.49547) **[V, abstract, figure captions, structure]**
- Relative fit is not enough: a model must be shown to generate the effect of interest (falsification by simulation). — [Palminteri, Wyart & Koechlin 2017, Trends in Cognitive Sciences 21(6):425–433](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tics.2017.03.011) **[M]**
- Parametric-bootstrap cross-fitting: simulate from each fitted model, fit both models to each simulated dataset, and use the two distributions of fit differences to calibrate the model-selection decision and quantify mimicry. — [Wagenmakers, Ratcliff, Gomez & Iverson 2004, J. Mathematical Psychology 48(1):28–50](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jmp.2003.11.004) **[M]**
- Bayesian workflow: fake-data simulation, prior predictive checks, computational validation (SBC), posterior predictive checks, and comparison across an expanding set of models are iterated, not done once. — [Gelman et al. 2020, arXiv:2011.01808](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01808) **[M]**
- Principled workflow steps: prior predictive checks, computational faithfulness (SBC), model sensitivity (can parameters be recovered; posterior z-score and contraction), posterior predictive checks. — [Schad, Betancourt & Vasishth 2021, Psychological Methods 26(1):103–126](https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000275) **[M]**
- Design and reporting of simulation studies: ADEMP (aims, data-generating mechanisms, estimands, methods, performance measures), with Monte Carlo standard errors for every performance measure; a review of 100 articles found simulation studies frequently poorly designed and reported. — [Morris, White & Crowther 2019, Statistics in Medicine 38(11):2074–2102](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086) **[V, abstract]**
- A preregistration template built on ADEMP, with formulae for Monte Carlo SEs and for the number of repetitions needed to hit a target Monte Carlo SE; and a 2026 preprint on when preregistering simulation studies helps. — [Siepe et al. 2024, Psychological Methods (DOI 10.1037/met0000695)](https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000695) **[V, abstract]**; [ADEMP-PreReg template](https://github.com/bsiepe/ADEMP-PreReg) **[V]**; [2026 preprint announcement](https://www.uni-marburg.de/en/fb04/team-heck/news/news/new-preprint-why-when-and-how-to-or-not-to-preregister-a-simulation-study) **[V, listing]**
- The ecology guide recommends simulate-and-refit as the practical estimability check for state-space models. — [Auger-Méthé et al. 2021](https://arxiv.org/abs/2002.02001) **[V]**

### Inferences
**A recovery design for this model (ADEMP-style, to be pre-registered).**
- *Aims.* (1) Does the MD pipeline recover each band profile and each scalar at the real N, T, banding, missingness and universe-selection rule? (2) What happens to the estimates and to the gate when the data-generating mechanism departs from the fitted model in each documented way? (3) Can the pipeline tell candidate mechanism A from B?
- *Data-generating mechanisms.* DGP-0: the fitted model at the registered parameters for each platform. DGP-1..k: one departure each, matching the fact sheet's residual list — returnable large displacements for established entities; temporary-absence (thinning) process with return; drifting amplitude spread s_t at the measured rate; entity-level kappa heterogeneity (log-SD 0.30); a heavier/lighter head product tail; correlated daily replicates (to stress Spec-B). Each DGP is passed through the SAME universe construction (top-K by absence-penalised permanent rank, buffer 4K) so that selection effects are inside the experiment.
- *Estimands.* Band profiles (reported as integrated squared error on log scale and as bias at head / middle / tail knots), scalars (s, phi, df, kappa level), and derived rank-level quantities (the gate statistics, established-departure rate, top-1 share).
- *Methods.* The frozen pipeline, plus any candidate upgrade (P-spline profiles, QML cross-check, alternative tail moment).
- *Performance measures.* Bias, empirical SE, RMSE, coverage of bootstrap intervals, each with Monte Carlo SE; for the gate, the pass rate; for mechanism discrimination, the confusion matrix.
- *Model-recovery confusion matrix for the full pipeline.* Rows: generating mechanism (DGP-0, DGP-1, ...). Columns: the pipeline's verdict under the pre-registered decision rule (e.g., "baseline passes gate and departure statistic", "baseline fails departure statistic, mechanism-1 extension passes", ...). Entry = fraction of replicates. Then compute the inversion matrix P(generating mechanism | verdict) under a declared prior over mechanisms. This directly answers whether a gate pass is evidence for the mechanism or merely compatible with several.
- *Two specific questions this would settle.* (i) Under DGP-0, how often does the established-departure statistic show a "deficit" of the observed size by chance and by universe-selection artefact alone? (ii) Under the returnable-jump DGP, does the baseline's MD estimator absorb the jumps into sigma_trans / t-df (pseudo-true shift), and does that shifted baseline then pass the gate — i.e., is the gate blind to that mechanism?
- *Sizing.* With pass/fail outcomes, 50 replicates per cell give a Monte Carlo SE of at most 0.07 on a proportion; 200 give 0.035. Head statistics need the program's existing >= 20 seeds per replicate on top of this, so GPU-batched simulation is the enabling investment.
- **SBC applies only if a Bayesian layer is added** (§4–5). For the MD estimator the frequentist analogue is coverage of the bootstrap intervals under DGP-0.

## 7. Regularising nonparametric rank profiles; testing a low-dimensional parametric profile; counting parameters

### Takeaway
The ~500 band values can be re-expressed as a small number of penalised smooth functions of log permanent rank with an honestly reported effective degrees of freedom, and a parametric law (e.g., a power law in rank) can be tested against the unrestricted bands with a classical minimum-distance statistic that the program already has every ingredient for. Because N is large, the test will tend to reject on trivial deviations, so the decision should rest on effect size in the held-out gate, with the chi-square statistic reported, not used as a gate.

### Cited Findings
- P-splines: a generous B-spline basis with a difference penalty on adjacent coefficients; the penalty order determines the limiting polynomial (a second-order penalty shrinks toward a straight line). — [Eilers & Marx 1996, Statistical Science 11(2):89–121](https://doi.org/10.1214/ss/1038425655) **[M]**
- Trend filtering (l1 penalty on discrete derivatives) yields piecewise-polynomial fits with adaptively chosen knots and is minimax-optimal for functions of bounded variation — preferable to l2 smoothing when a profile has kinks. — [Tibshirani 2014, Annals of Statistics 42(1):285–323](https://doi.org/10.1214/13-AOS1189) **[M]**; [Kim, Koh, Boyd & Gorinevsky 2009, SIAM Review 51(2):339–360](https://doi.org/10.1137/070690274) **[M]**
- Shape-constrained additive models impose monotonicity/convexity on P-spline smooths by reparameterisation; constrained regression splines give inference under shape restrictions. — [Pya & Wood 2015, Statistics and Computing 25:543–559](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11222-013-9448-7) **[M]**; [Meyer 2008, Annals of Applied Statistics 2(3):1013–1033](https://doi.org/10.1214/08-AOAS167) **[M]**
- Hierarchical GAMs: group-specific smooths are partially pooled toward a global smooth by penalising deviations; variants differ in whether groups share a smoothing parameter ("global smoother plus group-level smoothers with shared wiggliness" being the default recommendation); implemented in `mgcv` (`bs="fs"`, shared `id`). — [Pedersen, Miller, Simpson & Ross 2019, PeerJ 7:e6876](https://doi.org/10.7717/peerj.6876) **[V, abstract and secondary quotes]**
- Minimum-distance specification testing: with unrestricted reduced-form estimates pi-hat (here the band values) and a restriction pi = g(beta), n·min over beta of (pi-hat − g(beta))' V^(-1) (pi-hat − g(beta)) is asymptotically chi-square with dim(pi) − dim(beta) degrees of freedom; nested restrictions can be tested by differences. — [Chamberlain 1984, Handbook of Econometrics vol. 2 ch. 22](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1573-4412(84)02014-6) **[M]**; [Newey & McFadden 1994, Handbook of Econometrics vol. 4 ch. 36](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1573-4412(05)80005-4) **[M]**; over-identification test: [Hansen 1982, Econometrica 50(4):1029–1054](https://doi.org/10.2307/1912775) **[M]**
- Model and moment selection criteria for GMM (BIC/AIC/HQ analogues built from the J statistic minus a penalty in the number of over-identifying restrictions) consistently select the correct restrictions. — [Andrews & Lu 2001, J. Econometrics 101(1):123–164](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0304-4076(00)00077-4) **[M]**
- Over-identification and restriction tests remain available when cross-moment correlations are unknown. — [Cocci & Plagborg-Møller 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/restud/rdae099) **[V, abstract]**
- p-values for smooth terms (testing a smooth against zero or against a parametric null) in penalised regression. — [Wood 2013, Biometrika 100(1):221–228](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/ass048) **[M]**
- Effective degrees of freedom: for linear smoothers the trace of the hat matrix; generalised df = sum of sensitivities of fitted values to observations (covers adaptive/selected procedures); equivalently the covariance penalty sum cov(y-hat_i, y_i)/sigma^2. — [Ye 1998, JASA 93(441):120–131](https://doi.org/10.1080/01621459.1998.10474094) **[M]**; [Efron 2004, JASA 99(467):619–632](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214504000000692) **[M]**
- Caveat: effective df can behave non-monotonically in nominal model size and exceed the ambient dimension for adaptive procedures, so it is a descriptive summary rather than a parameter count. — [Janson, Fithian & Hastie 2015, Biometrika 102(2):479–485](https://doi.org/10.1093/biomet/asv019) **[M]**
- Many-moment bias and the case for regularised or equal weighting in MD apply equally when the "moments" are band values. — [Altonji & Segal 1996](https://www.nber.org/papers/t0156) **[V]**; [Cheng, Sánchez-Becerra & Shephard](https://economics.sas.upenn.edu/node/15540) **[V, listing]**

### Inferences
- **Two-stage ("smooth the MD output") is the discipline-preserving design.** Stage 1 is the current unrestricted per-band NNLS — unchanged, so defaults remain byte-identical. Stage 2 fits each profile f(log z) to the Stage-1 band values by penalised GLS using the bootstrap covariance V of the band estimates. The smoothing parameter is chosen by a declared, data-internal criterion (REML/GCV or entity-fold cross-validation of the MOMENTS, never the gate). Nothing is tuned to a score.
- **Parameter accounting to report** (three numbers per profile, per platform): nominal band count; effective df of the penalised fit (trace of the Stage-2 hat matrix); and the parametric dimension of the best non-rejected law. Communicate as "five latent components, each with a smooth rank profile of effective dimension ~d", with Janson et al.'s caveat acknowledged. If shape constraints (e.g., monotone sigma_trans in rank) are imposed, state them as assumptions and test them with the constrained-vs-unconstrained distance.
- **Test sequence for a parametric law** (e.g., log sigma_perm(z) = a + b·log z): (1) MD chi-square distance of band values from the law, df = bands − 2, with bootstrap V; (2) the same with diagonal V and Cocci–Plagborg-Møller worst-case critical values, since V's off-diagonals are noisy; (3) Andrews–Lu-type BIC across {power law, power law + head correction, low-edf spline, unrestricted}; (4) the decisive comparison — rolling-origin gate and proper scores with the restricted vs unrestricted profile. Pre-register that (4) adjudicates and (1)–(3) are reported.
- **Expect rejection at N ~ 10^4.** Report the maximum and RMS deviation of the law from the bands in units that matter (implied change in median |dRank|), not just the p-value.
- **Cross-platform pooling.** A hierarchical smooth — global profile in normalised log rank (z/K) plus platform-specific deviations sharing one wiggliness penalty — delivers a direct estimate of how much of each profile is common across Facebook, Reddit comments and Reddit submissions, and yields a pre-registered prior profile for Wikipedia before data contact. With only 3–4 platforms the between-platform variance is poorly estimated; present it as descriptive.
- **Where smoothing can hurt.** The frontier residuals live at the head (top-1 share) and at the K/2 boundary. A global second-order penalty will shrink the first few knots toward the trend. Use a rank-adaptive penalty or trend filtering, or leave the top few bands unpenalised, and check the head residual does not change sign merely because of smoothing. Finer rank bands were already tried and refuted as a fix; this proposal goes the other direction (fewer effective parameters) and is about parsimony, not fit.

### Gaps
- All smoothing references except Pedersen et al. are [M].
- I found no source on smoothing variance-component profiles estimated by NNLS with boundary (zero) solutions; penalised fits through boundary estimates need care (the Stage-1 sampling distribution is not normal there).
- No source addresses effective-df reporting for a simulator whose inputs are smooth profiles; the three-number convention above is a proposal.

## 8. Model comparison when the likelihood is unavailable or the model is misspecified

### Takeaway
The defensible comparison tools are out-of-sample predictive scoring with proper scoring rules and formal equal-predictive-ability tests, a Vuong-type minimum-distance test on untargeted moments, and stacking of predictive distributions; the tools to avoid are in-sample moment-count scorecards and simulation-based Bayes factors computed from summary statistics.

### Cited Findings
- Proper scoring rules (CRPS, energy score, log score) are the basis for comparing probabilistic forecasts. — [Gneiting & Raftery 2007, JASA 102(477):359–378](https://doi.org/10.1198/016214506000001437) **[M]**
- Tests of equal conditional predictive ability compare forecasting METHODS (model plus estimation window plus procedure) under rolling windows and are valid for misspecified and nested models. — [Giacomini & White 2006, Econometrica 74(6):1545–1578](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1468-0262.2006.00718.x) **[M]**
- Vuong-type comparison of misspecified calibrated models by minimum-distance fit, for nested and non-nested pairs, with averaged and sup-norm versions that reduce dependence on the weight matrix, and covering estimation on one moment set and evaluation on another. — [Hnatkovska, Marmer & Tang 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2012.01.007) **[V, abstract]**
- Stacking: in the M-open setting (no candidate is true) Bayesian model averaging is flawed; stacking chooses weights to maximise a proper score of the combined predictive distribution on held-out data; recommended over BMA and pseudo-BMA. — [Yao, Vehtari, Simpson & Gelman 2018, Bayesian Analysis 13(3):917–1007](https://doi.org/10.1214/17-BA1091) **[V, abstract]**
- Leave-future-out cross-validation (M-step-ahead, minimum history L) is the time-series-appropriate CV target. — [Bürkner, Gabry & Vehtari 2020, J. Stat. Comput. Simul. (DOI 10.1080/00949655.2020.1783262)](https://doi.org/10.1080/00949655.2020.1783262) **[V, abstract]**
- ABC/summary-based Bayes factors can be inconsistent because summaries sufficient for each model's parameters are not sufficient for model choice. — [Robert, Cornuet, Marin & Pillai 2011, PNAS 108(37):15112–15117](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1102900108) **[M]**
- Model recovery must precede any model comparison claim. — [Wilson & Collins 2019](https://doi.org/10.7554/eLife.49547) **[V]**
- Holdouts as protection against specification search. — [Schorfheide & Wolpin](https://www.nber.org/papers/w19565) **[V]**
- Calibration critique: fit assessed on selected moments without sampling uncertainty is not a test. — [Hansen & Heckman 1996](https://doi.org/10.1257/jep.10.1.87) **[M]**

### Inferences
- **The Tier-0 gate is the right kind of object** (rolling-origin, held-out, baseline-relative). Two upgrades the literature supports: (a) state the gate comparison as a Giacomini–White-style test on per-origin loss differentials between the model and the persistence baseline, so "at par" has a p-value and power statement; (b) add a Hnatkovska–Marmer–Tang statistic for mechanism A vs B on the untargeted rank-level moments, with the sup-norm version to avoid arguing about weights.
- **Proper scores for a simulator.** CRPS/energy scores are computable from simulation ensembles without a likelihood, per entity and horizon. The fact sheet's "CRPS skill ~ 0" should be decomposed by permanent-rank band and by horizon before concluding there is no edge; pooled scores are dominated by the mass of tail entities whose movement is nearly unforecastable.
- **Stacking is a constructive use of failed mechanisms.** With candidate mechanisms each producing predictive ensembles, stacking weights estimated on earlier origins and evaluated on later ones answer "does mechanism B add predictive information beyond A and persistence?" without declaring a winner. With >= 5 origins the weights will be noisy; report them with bootstrap bands.
- **What not to do, and why.** (1) "12–15 of 15" in-sample scorecards as comparison: thresholds are arbitrary, rows are correlated, there is no sampling distribution, and the same data estimated the parameters — keep the card for residual localisation only, as the program's hierarchy already states. (2) Posterior model probabilities from neural or ABC classifiers on summaries (Robert et al.). (3) Comparing mechanisms on statistics that either one targeted. (4) Selecting among many mechanism variants on the gate and then reporting the gate for the winner without a fresh holdout — the Wikipedia confirmation is the protection, so the number of variants tried against existing platforms should be logged as the "garden" size.

### Gaps
- Gneiting–Raftery, Giacomini–White and Robert et al. are [M].
- No source was found on stacking with as few as ~5 rolling origins of panel forecasts; small-sample behaviour of the weights is unverified.

## 9. Practicalities: differentiable simulators and soft ranks, GPU batching, emulators/history matching, reproducibility

### Takeaway
GPU-batched forward simulation is the enabling investment for everything above (recovery studies, RBSL, amortised checks) and carries no methodological risk; differentiable soft-rank calibration is technically available but introduces a temperature hyperparameter and biased gradients, so it should stay out of the estimation path; history matching is the emulator-based approach most compatible with the program's ethos because it rules parameter regions out under an explicit model-discrepancy allowance instead of tuning to a best fit.

### Cited Findings
- Differentiable sorting and ranking operators with O(n log n) time and O(n) memory, obtained as regularised projections onto the permutahedron (reduced to isotonic optimisation), exact gradients, converging to hard ranks as the regularisation goes to zero; demonstrated on a differentiable Spearman correlation. — [Blondel, Teboul, Berthet & Djolonga, ICML 2020 (PMLR 119:950–959)](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v119/blondel20a.html) **[V]**
- Earlier optimal-transport (Sinkhorn) soft ranks cost O(T·m·n). — [Cuturi, Teboul & Vert 2019, arXiv:1905.11885](https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.11885) **[M; complexity as reported in Blondel et al.]**
- Differentiable agent-based models: discrete randomness is usually relaxed with Gumbel-Softmax, which guarantees neither unbiased nor low-variance gradients, though it was adequate in the authors' experiments. — [Quera-Bofarull, Dyer, Calinescu & Wooldridge 2023, arXiv:2307.01085](https://arxiv.org/abs/2307.01085) **[V, abstract and section summary]**; a 2025 comparison finds straight-through estimators fail in strongly nonlinear regimes and the unbiased StochasticAD estimator is most robust but costly. — [arXiv:2509.03303](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.03303) **[V, via search summary]**; unbiased AD for discrete randomness: [Arya et al. 2022, arXiv:2210.08572](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.08572) **[M]**
- History matching: build fast emulators of simulator outputs, define an implausibility measure that includes observation error, emulator uncertainty and an explicit model-discrepancy variance, and discard implausible parameter regions in waves. — [Vernon, Goldstein & Bower 2010, Bayesian Analysis 5(4):619–669](https://doi.org/10.1214/10-BA524) **[M]**
- Tutorial and case study: a 22-input stochastic simulator matched to 18 outputs; nine waves shrank the non-implausible region by a factor ~10^11; open issues noted were simulator stochasticity and output correlation, later addressed by emulating the output variance. — [Andrianakis et al. 2015, PLoS Comput. Biol. 11(1):e1003968](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4288726) **[V]**; software: [`hmer` R package, J. Stat. Software 109(10), 2024](https://doi.org/10.18637/jss.v109.i10) **[V, CRAN record]**
- Bayesian calibration with an explicit model-discrepancy function. — [Kennedy & O'Hagan 2001, JRSS-B 63(3):425–464](https://doi.org/10.1111/1467-9868.00294) **[M]**
- Review of analysing stochastic simulators (replication, heteroskedastic emulation). — [Baker et al. 2022, Statistical Science 37(1):64–89](https://doi.org/10.1214/21-STS822) **[M]**
- Bayesian-optimisation calibration (BOLFI). — [Gutmann & Corander 2016](https://jmlr.org/papers/v17/15-017.html) **[M]**
- For economic agent-based models, surrogate and likelihood-approximation calibration methods have been compared head-to-head. — [Platt 2020, JEDC 113:103859](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jedc.2020.103859) **[M]**; [Dyer et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.00625) **[V]**
- Reporting standards for simulation studies. — [Morris, White & Crowther 2019](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086) **[V]**; [Siepe et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1037/met0000695) **[V]**
- The sbi guide notes simulations can be generated once offline and reused across methods, and that for ~100 or fewer parameter/data dimensions training takes minutes and may be as fast on CPU as GPU. — [Deistler et al. 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.12939) **[V]**

### Inferences
- **The simulator is embarrassingly vectorisable.** AR(1)/OU recursions over (seeds × entities) with a per-week sort are a `scan` plus batched `argsort` in JAX/PyTorch. Batching seeds on a GPU turns ">= 20 seeds" from a cost into a default and makes 10^3–10^4 simulations per experiment realistic. The research code path and defaults need not change: a batched simulator can be validated against the existing one by exact CRN replay on a small case before being used for any new study. (Packaging and frozen-spec rules in the repository govern where such code may live; this note makes no recommendation on that.)
- **Soft ranks: only as an optimiser aid, never as the model.** A temperature-smoothed rank changes the statistic being matched; the estimate then depends on the temperature schedule — a tuning parameter with no declared-moment justification. If SMM on a rank-level moment is ever adopted (§1), use soft ranks at most to obtain a search direction, then evaluate and report the objective with hard ranks under CRNs. Given §1's observation that the pooled objective is already nearly smooth, a Gaussian-process/Bayesian-optimisation search over a 2–5-dimensional block is simpler and has no bias.
- **History matching matches the program's epistemology.** Its output is a non-implausible SET given an explicit discrepancy tolerance — "which mechanism parameter regions are not ruled out by the declared statistics", not "the best-fitting value". For a new mechanism block this gives (i) a pre-registrable implausibility threshold, (ii) a direct statement when the non-implausible set is EMPTY (the mechanism cannot reach the departure rate and the head share simultaneously — a refutation with a number attached), and (iii) reuse of the same emulators for sensitivity analysis.
- **Reproducibility additions to existing practice** (CRNs, seeds, append-only log are already in place): record Monte Carlo SE next to every simulated statistic; state the repetition count's justification; archive simulation banks (parameters, seeds, summaries) so synthetic-likelihood, neural and emulator methods are compared on identical simulations.

## Implications for the rank-diffusion model

**Principles for adopting any of the above without giving up "declared moments, no tuning".**
1. Every estimator — MD, SMM, synthetic likelihood, neural — takes a pre-declared summary set as input. A network or a Gaussian synthetic likelihood may approximate the MAP from declared summaries to parameters; it may not choose the summaries.
2. Tier-0 gate statistics are never estimation targets. Any promoted rank-level statistic is declared before data contact, attached to one named parameter, estimated on training windows only, and reported with its sensitivity (Lambda) and informativeness (Delta).
3. Modularity is a feature: sigma_obs from the independent instrument, amplitude spread from cross-entity dispersion, and dynamics from autocovariances are "cut" modules in the sense of the SBI-misspecification literature; do not replace them with a joint fit that lets a misspecified block contaminate the others. Use joint/likelihood fits as cross-checks.
4. Smoothing and regularisation hyperparameters are set by criteria internal to the estimation moments (REML/GCV/moment cross-validation), never by the gate.
5. New inferential machinery is validated on simulated data from the fitted model (recovery, coverage, SBC where Bayesian) before it touches real data; the validation design is pre-registered in ADEMP form.
6. Changes to declared moments, frozen specs or thresholds remain owner-gated; items below that would require such a change are marked.

### Adopt now

**A1. Identification and sensitivity table (AGS Lambda, Honoré drop-group, profiles and robust sets for kappa and phi, Delta for validation statistics).**
- *First experiment:* on Facebook Era A and Reddit comments, compute the analytic Jacobian per band, a two-way block-bootstrap moment covariance, scaled Lambda grouped by moment family, Honoré E4 by family, the concentrated-objective profile and S-set for kappa per band and era, and Delta of each Tier-0/Tier-1 statistic for each scalar via a parametric bootstrap. Push the documented residuals through Lambda to get implied parameter biases.
- *Expected benefit:* identification clarity as a publishable table; a direct test of whether the 4–8x kappa era shifts exceed weak-identification uncertainty; documented near-independence (or not) of the gate from estimation moments; reviewer-proofing of "every parameter comes from a declared moment".
- *Failure mode to watch:* Lambda is a local, interior-solution object — bands with NNLS boundary solutions and heavy-tailed moment sampling distributions invalidate it; the bootstrap covariance may be unstable for kurtosis-type moments. Report boundary share and use subsampling as a check.

**A2. Sampling theory for the MD estimates (bootstrap covariance, two-step uncertainty from Spec-B, worst-case standard errors, descriptive over-identification statistics).**
- *First experiment:* entity-block × week-block bootstrap of the entire pipeline (universe selection included) for one platform; add Cocci–Plagborg-Møller worst-case SEs treating the daily-replication moment and weekly moments as having unknown correlation; report the MD distance statistic per band as a descriptive misfit map.
- *Expected benefit:* uncertainty quantification that currently exists only for scored statistics; intervals on band profiles needed by A4 and by any mechanism comparison.
- *Failure mode:* with N ~ 10^4 the over-identification statistic rejects everywhere; treat it as a map of where misfit concentrates (which bands, which lags), not as a verdict.

**A3. Pre-registered recovery suite, including model recovery of the full pipeline under realistic misspecification.**
- *First experiment:* DGP-0 (fitted model) and one misspecified DGP (returnable large displacements for established entities with temporary absence), 50 replicates each at real N and T, passed through the real universe construction; run the frozen pipeline; tabulate parameter bias/RMSE/coverage, gate pass rates, the established-departure statistic, and the 2×2 confusion matrix with Monte Carlo SEs.
- *Expected benefit:* the power and false-alarm rate of the gate and of the departure diagnostic; whether the baseline absorbs a jump mechanism into sigma_trans/df; a null distribution for the "2.3–2.7x deficit" under the model's own universe selection.
- *Failure mode:* recovery under the model's own DGP alone is uninformative and flattering — the misspecified arms are the point; compute cost if simulation is not batched; choosing misspecified DGPs after seeing which ones the pipeline handles (pre-register the list).

**A4. Smooth the band profiles in a second stage, report effective degrees of freedom, and test parametric laws against the unrestricted bands.**
- *First experiment:* P-spline (second-order difference penalty on log permanent rank) fitted to Stage-1 band values with bootstrap covariance for sigma_perm and sigma_trans on Reddit comments; report nominal count, effective df, MD chi-square for a power law (and power law plus head correction), and the gate with restricted vs unrestricted profiles. Then a hierarchical global-plus-platform smooth across the three metrics.
- *Expected benefit:* converts the main parsimony liability ("~500 band values") into a defensible statement with an effective dimension; a pre-registrable prior profile for Wikipedia; a quantified answer to "is it a power law in rank?".
- *Failure mode:* over-smoothing the head bands, where the open residuals live; reading large-N rejections as substantive; smoothing parameter chosen (even implicitly) by looking at the gate.

**A5. Levels-vs-differences and gap-edge diagnostics (from the earnings-dynamics literature).**
- *First experiment:* re-estimate the variance components from level autocovariances (within-entity demeaned) and compare with the difference-based estimates by band; separately, re-estimate after dropping the weeks immediately adjacent to any absence.
- *Expected benefit:* a cheap specification test targeted at exactly the documented estimand mismatch (temporary absence treated as death) and at the persistent-component residual at 13 weeks.
- *Failure mode:* level moments are sensitive to the home-level heterogeneity and to within-entity demeaning bias at short T; interpret disagreement as a diagnostic, not as a reason to switch estimators.

### Pilot

**P1. Latent-level panel quasi-likelihood as a cross-check, and a t-score-driven forecast filter.**
- *First experiment:* (a) on data simulated from the fitted model, verify that a Kalman QML with the amplitude integrated by Gauss–Hermite quadrature (parameters by permanent-rank band) recovers the MD estimates and compare standard errors; (b) on real data, compare QML vs MD by band and inspect how missing weeks change the answer; (c) replace the steady-state Kalman forecast filter with a Harvey–Luati t-score filter using the already-estimated df and variances, as a single-change experiment scored by CRPS by band and horizon.
- *Expected benefit:* efficiency benchmark; principled missing-week handling; a plausible route to the first proper-score edge over persistence via better filtered home states.
- *Failure mode:* Gaussian QML chases t-outliers and piles variances at zero; multimodal likelihood under misspecification (Fasiolo et al.); temptation to let the likelihood re-estimate sigma_obs jointly (breaks the instrument module).

**P2. Robust synthetic likelihood (RBSL) or RNPE on declared rank-level summaries as a misspecification localiser.**
- *First experiment:* freeze band profiles at MD values; free 3–5 global scalars; summaries = that block's declared moments plus untargeted rank-level statistics, each with a mean-adjustment parameter; run RBSL-M; report which adjustment posteriors leave their prior and by how many simulated SDs.
- *Expected benefit:* a posterior, per-statistic statement of what the simulator cannot reproduce; uncertainty quantification for the global scalars; a single misspecification-distance number per platform to track across model generations.
- *Failure mode:* non-Gaussian head statistics violate the synthetic-likelihood assumption (transform or exclude them); multimodality under incompatibility; simulation budget (needs the batched simulator).

**P3. SMM / quasi-Bayesian (Laplace-type) estimation for new-mechanism parameters only, on pre-declared tail moments.** *(Owner-gated: adds declared moments.)*
- *First experiment:* for a returnable-large-displacement mechanism, declare level-based quantile moments by permanent-rank band (exceedance rates and P10/P90 of h-week changes, reversal after large long differences), estimate the 2–4 new parameters by CRN-based SMM with a Gaussian-process search or a Chernozhukov–Hong quasi-posterior, and evaluate the untargeted rank-level departure/return rates and the gate.
- *Expected benefit:* addresses frontier item 1 with a mechanism whose parameters come from declared latent-level moments, leaving the rank-level deficit as a genuine out-of-sample test.
- *Failure mode:* compensating errors — the new block soaks up misfit that belongs to the exit machinery; quasi-posterior intervals invalid without the sandwich correction; drifting toward targeting the departure rate itself.

**P4. Replace the kurtosis-based df moment with a quantile-based or score-based tail statistic.** *(Owner-gated: changes a declared moment.)*
- *First experiment:* recovery study of df under DGP-0 comparing within-entity kurtosis, a Crow–Siddiqui-type quantile ratio, and the t-likelihood score, at df in {3, 4, 5, 8}.
- *Expected benefit:* a tail parameter whose estimator has a finite-variance sampling distribution; bears on the head-law residual (product tail of amplitude × t) and on the fatter-than-simulated standardised change tails.
- *Failure mode:* the program has already refuted rank-dependent df and "tail-shape surgery"; this is a change of estimator for the same parameter, and must be framed and pre-registered that way.

**P5. Amortised NPE on declared summaries with full validation, and an out-of-simulator distance.**
- *First experiment:* reuse the P2 simulation bank; train NPE from the declared summary vector to the global scalars; run SBC with a data-dependent test quantity, TARP, and L-C2ST at each platform's observed summaries; compute the density (or MMD) of observed summaries under the simulator.
- *Expected benefit:* near-free coverage diagnostics and fast inversion for the A3 confusion matrix; a quantitative "how far outside the simulator is each platform" metric.
- *Failure mode:* extrapolation when observed summaries are outside the training distribution — which the fact sheet's residuals suggest they are; treat any posterior obtained there as unreliable and report the distance instead.

**P6. History matching with an explicit discrepancy allowance for mechanism screening; predictive stacking across mechanisms.**
- *First experiment:* emulate 6–10 declared output statistics over a 3–5-dimensional mechanism block; run two or three waves with a pre-registered implausibility threshold and discrepancy variance; report whether the non-implausible set is empty. Separately, compute stacking weights for {persistence, baseline, candidate mechanism} on per-origin energy scores.
- *Expected benefit:* fast, principled refutation ("no parameter value reaches both the departure rate and the head share"); a non-binary measure of what each mechanism adds predictively.
- *Failure mode:* the discrepancy variance becomes a tuning knob — fix it in advance from seed and bootstrap variability; stacking weights are noisy with ~5 origins.

### Avoid

**X1. End-to-end neural SBI with learned summaries on raw panels for the full model.** ~500 parameters, known misspecification, closed-form moments available, and identification transparency lost. *Failure mode if attempted:* confident posteriors driven by features the real data violate, with no way to audit which.

**X2. Promoting Tier-0 gate statistics, or head shares, to estimation targets.** Destroys the only adjudicator and, for head shares, targets a statistic with a non-Gaussian, few-entity sampling distribution whose residual sign differs across platforms. *Failure mode:* in-sample fit improves while the variance components are distorted to compensate (visible in Lambda).

**X3. Optimal (inverse-covariance) weighting across ~10^3 moments, and moment-based kurtosis/fourth-moment targets under df near 4.** Altonji–Segal bias; non-existent or non-Gaussian moment sampling distributions.

**X4. In-sample scorecard counts or summary-based Bayes factors as model comparison.** No sampling distribution; summary insufficiency for model choice.

**X5. Gradient-based calibration through soft-rank relaxations as the estimator.** Introduces a temperature hyperparameter and biased gradients; unnecessary given low-dimensional blocks and near-smooth pooled objectives.

**X6. Full-Bayes MCMC, particle MCMC or panel iterated filtering over the joint latent-state model.** Wrong scale regime (10^4 units, conditionally linear); the quadrature-marginal Kalman likelihood gives the same cross-check at a tiny fraction of the cost, and the literature flags these methods as the more fragile option under misspecification.

**X7. Calibration-set SBI corrections (optimal-transport or flow-matching recalibration).** They require real observations with known ground-truth parameters, which do not exist here.

### Suggested order
A1 and A2 share one bootstrap and should be done together; A3 needs a batched simulator and gates P2, P5 and P6; A4 and A5 are independent of the rest; P1(c) (forecast filter) is the only item aimed directly at proper-score skill; P3 and P4 are the items that touch declared moments and need owner sign-off before any work.
