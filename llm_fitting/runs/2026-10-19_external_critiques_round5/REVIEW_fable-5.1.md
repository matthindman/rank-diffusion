## 1. Editor's decision now

**Send out, still without the adjective.** (i) v3 is a markedly more honest paper than v2: the null worlds have 20 seeds and bands, the calibrated worlds start from the data's ladder, three round-3 "discoveries" are withdrawn in print, the causal clause is gone, and every number I recomputed (R5, R6, R7, R8, the joint bootstrap, C(R), the persistence splits) reproduces from the saved outputs. (ii) The headline answer, however, rests on a gross accounting: "crossings return three quarters of what members lose" counts the universe's own crossing gain c_K on both sides; net of it, the crossing gradient returns a quarter of the members' relative decline pooled, and on the submissions head it is *negative in all five cells* (`law__reddit_submissions_long.json`, half-year: c_78 − c_K = −0.9 to −1.9 per 1,000 wk). The SI (l. 119) already states that volatility's net effect on the shape is c_k − c_K; the main text does not carry the consequence. (iii) The 30-year counterfactuals, which earn "holds up", use a drift by rank built from the two annual primary cells 2020→21 and 2021→22 only (`churn_counterfactual.py` l. 40–46; block 0 is the cohort block), the two years whose head decline the paper itself calls five times the noise level (l. 179); the head slide varies 1.5× (submissions) and 3.8× (comments) across the half-year cells, so the balance S* = σ²/(2s̄) is a two-year average extrapolated for three decades. (iv) The generative model is beaten on held-out movement by its own no-drift ablation on comments (0.100 vs 0.130) and by persistence on submissions (0.163 vs 0.183), so the ingredient that defines the balance makes forecasts worse. (v) Persistence on comments, the participation split and the time-scale framing are real and well done. **The single change that most raises the odds, with data on hand:** present the balance net of c_K and by period, and rerun `long30` with the drift profile from the earliest and latest cells; if collapse/steepening/convergence survive, the title is earned; if not, retitle to the question. ## 2. The title and the answer

The four pieces of evidence have different weights. **The time-scale argument** is sound and is the paper's best sentence: the shape moves by about 0.2–0.3 log units a year against a span of 4–5 while 15–33% of each set is replaced, a factor-of-ten separation (though at the submissions head the relaxation time of 2–6 years is barely longer than the membership half-life). **The flat start** shows the ingredients build *a* hierarchy, one 8–46% flatter than the observed at every cut-off (bands exclude the observation everywhere but the submissions head). **The measured balance** is a state description, as the paper now says, and its headline number depends on averaging: over the 60 half-year cells the ratio of pooled means is 0.724, the mean of per-cell c/s̄ is 1.04, the median 0.77; comments 0.85 (per-cell mean 1.39, range 0.39–3.99), submissions 0.64 (0.69, 0.26–1.27). "S*/S = 0.72 on average" (l. 147) is the ratio of means. **The 30-year counterfactuals** are arithmetic once the drift is fixed: a head drift gradient of ≈0.006/wk sustained for 30 years compresses 4.5 log units (collapse), a driftless walk spreads as √t (steepening without limit); what they add is that the two together converge within 5–16% of the flat start (verified: submissions 5–16%, comments 5–11%). They are evidence about the model, not about whether Reddit's 2020–22 drift is structural.

**Weakest word: "holds up."** In the data the submissions head's crossing gain (0.0027–0.0034/wk) is below the universe boundary's (0.0044–0.0047) in every cell, so turnover lowered the head relative to the universe over 2019–22; the hierarchy the ingredients hold up in the model is 35–46% flatter at the head than the one observed; and the drift that defines it comes from two post-surge years. "Durable" is second: the arithmetic share of the submissions head fell by a third in three years. "Constant" is third: the crossing gain differs by 40–70% between head and universe boundary with opposite signs on the two ladders.

Titles that keep the owner's question: (a) **"How turnover and decline set the hierarchy of online attention"** (mechanism account, both ingredients, no claim that the observed hierarchy is held); (b) "Why the hierarchy of online attention outlasts its members" (Opus R4; safest, rests on the time-scale argument alone); (c) "Where a durable hierarchy of online attention comes from when its members change so fast" (the question itself).

**(4) The model and the counterfactuals.** The equations describe the code, the gate story is told straight, and the flat-start/long30 numbers reproduce (CF-1 pass; CF-2 fails on comments, +28% with +0.52 in the last decade; CF-3 fails at K/2 on submissions, 15.7%). ## 4. What remains discovered

- **Persistence against every world:** robust on comments; on submissions it is a factor structure that an untested common-factor world would reproduce. Overstated by one world that was not run.
- **The measured balance and its time scales:** the time-scale statement is sound and undersold as a number (shape ≈2% of span a year; membership ≈20%); the balance as "three quarters" is overstated by the gross accounting and by the choice of average.
- **The participation split:** a genuine descriptive fact, undersold at the end of the last section.
- **Weaker-than-firm size dependence:** holds on submissions and Facebook, not on comments; no intervals; not like-for-like with firms.
- **Undersold elsewhere:** the boundary ratio excludes the volatility-sorting rival (1.38 in the protocol's power study), unmentioned; Facebook's C(1) = 0.52 against Reddit's 0.03–0.07 is a real platform difference left uninterpreted; the top ten's excess over every calibrated world is unnoticed.

Is it enough for PNAS? As a measurement of stochastic-portfolio-theory quantities on named attention units with a registration record, yes for sending out; the discovery content is persistence on comments plus the split, and the "answer" is an equilibrium account of a hierarchy flatter than the one observed.
