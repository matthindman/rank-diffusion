### Audit of the model

**1. Split-half volatility and empirical Bayes.** The estimator takes one-week squared changes in two separated halves, regresses each log mean square on \(1,z,z^2\), and sets
\[
\hat s=\sqrt{\max(0,\overline{r_{i1}r_{i2}})}.
\]
This addresses the old estimator’s tendency to mistake within-entity sampling noise for persistent heterogeneity. The claim of exact unbiasedness at zero is false. Even if the signed covariance is unbiased, the positive part and square root yield a positive expected estimate. In an in-memory check of the actual routine on **200 independent homogeneous Gaussian random walks**, \(T=86,N=1000,K=500\), fixed ranks, seeds 3000–3199, the mean estimate was **0.0195**, the median zero, the 95th percentile **0.0607**, and **46.5%** were positive. Persistent volatility regimes, selection into both halves, and residual rank-profile error can also make the signed covariance positive. With \(\gamma_L=0\), weekly variance has the form \(a_i+b_i v_i\). Its log dispersion is only a proxy for the dispersion of \(\log v_i\). [Split-half calculation](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:173) · [Single-seed null test](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/tests/test_common_rankdiff.py:52)

The EB point estimate correctly enforces the **sample arithmetic mean** of \(\hat v_i\) equal to one among training top-\(K\) entities. Its shrinkage uses \(w_i=s^2/(s^2+3/n_i)\), with a fixed heavy-tail allowance, clipping and a pooled centering correction. Serial dependence and kurtosis make \(3/n_i\) an approximation. The ratio divides by *total* weekly variance, including components which \(v_i\) does not multiply. In S3 the denominator also omits the entity’s squared surge scale. Finally the forecast uses clipped normal log-\(v\) draws and clips again after exponentiation in log coordinates. Subtracting half the Gaussian variance before those clips cannot preserve the conditional arithmetic mean exactly. The no-EB ablation redraws future \(v_i\) after an EB-conditioned filtering pass, so it isolates only part of the contribution. The saved Stage 1 move-size correlations of about **0.24–0.38** nevertheless justify retaining and testing an endpoint scale estimate. [EB fit](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:1141) · [Forecast draws](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:1405) · [Stage 1 record](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/MODEL_STATUS.md:14331)

**2. Structure functions, Spec-B and variance accounting.** At fixed band and independent stationary passing components, the working equation is sound:
\[
D_b(h)=2\sigma_{\epsilon,b}^2+2V_{\xi,b}(1-\phi^h)+h\sigma_{L,b}^2.
\]
Profiling a common \(\phi\) over 13 values and solving nonnegative least squares by band is interpretable. Equal lag weights and normalized band residuals prevent a large band from deciding everything, but overlapping lags have highly correlated errors. Nonnegative corner solutions are dropped from the later log-linear regressions, and the final profiles can differ from the NNLS fits. Drift by rank, changing band composition, informative observation, and moving latent ranks also enter the measured structure function. Profile curvature, corner frequency and the discrepancy after smoothing belong beside each parameter estimate. [Structure function](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:156) · [Profile objective](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:581) · [Final profile regressions](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:769)

Spec-B as a **lower bound** has a good scientific rationale. The recorded equality fit drove Facebook’s \(\phi\) to 0.45, while the inequality fit gave 0.84 and reduced the reported SSE from 0.015 to 0.002. There are two implementation qualifications. First, the NNLS target is \(\max(D_{\rm net}-2\sigma_{\rm floor}^2,0)\), so a lag already below the floor loses its negative residual in the optimization. Second, a subsequent unconstrained line fit to total band noise can cross the floor between or even at measured ranks. The final `sigma_obs_of` never reapplies the bound. “Spec-B lower bound” is verified at the intermediate coefficients only. The excess also combines measurement variation and genuine weekly bursts, which have different physical meanings. [Floor calculation](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:604) · [Final observation law](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:790) · [§2z-dx, failed equality fit](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/MODEL_STATUS.md:14354)

S3 subtracts the theoretical contribution
\[
D_{\rm sub,b}(h)=2c_b^2V_s(1-\rho_s^h)+
\sigma_d^2\!\left[h+2\sum_{j=1}^{h-1}(h-j)\rho_d^j\right].
\]
This is a substantial improvement over adding variance twice. **Exact conservation does not follow.** The code subtracts at most \(0.85D_b(h)\) while the simulator retains the entire fitted mechanism. There are two estimation passes, a subsequent smoothing step, capped jump draws, and time-varying \(c(r_{it})\). Even with an exogenous changing scale, the surge contribution conditional on two scales involves \(V_s(c_t^2+c_{t+h}^2-2c_tc_{t+h}\rho_s^h)\). A single band multiplier is an approximation. A reduction in \(\hat\sigma_{L0}\) is an expected consequence of subtraction. It does not verify the total simulated variance curve. [Subtraction guard](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:605) · [Two-pass estimator](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:738) · [Simulated observation](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:1058)

**3. Fading positive surges.** For a stationary linear AR(1) shot process with iid jump \(J=B E\), \(B\sim{\rm Bernoulli}(\lambda)\), \(E\sim{\rm Exp}(\mu)\) in mean-scale notation, the third-cumulant identity is correct:
\[
\kappa_3(\Delta_h s)=3r(1-r)\frac{\kappa_3(J)}{1-\rho_s^3},\qquad
r=\rho_s^h,\qquad \kappa_3(J)=\lambda(6-6\lambda+2\lambda^2)\mu^3.
\]
This supplies a shape and an amplitude. At rare arrival rates it identifies approximately \(\lambda\mu^3\), leaving considerable freedom in the separate rate and size. The observed changes are pooled, winsorized at six SDs and recentered. The empirical innovation pool can itself be skewed. Both facts weaken the attribution of their entire third cumulant to surges. [Marginal moment fit](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:434)

The odd fourth functional \(A(Z)=E[Z^3|Z|]-4E|Z|E[Z^3]\) vanishes for a centered symmetric distribution with finite moments. It is **not an additive cumulant**. For example, take centered \(Z=-1,2\) with probabilities \(2/3,1/3\). Adding an independent symmetric random sign leaves \(\kappa_3=2\) and changes \(A\) from \(-6\) to \(-7/3\). The code’s \(\hat\mu=\mathrm{clip}[(\sum A/\sum\kappa_3)/1.35,0.25,1.20]\) therefore needs a calibration or derivation for the actual nuisance process. The fitted \(\lambda\) is capped at 0.06 and can be reduced again by a 35% variance guard that explicitly sacrifices the third-moment fit. Both Reddit \(\mu\) estimates hit **1.20**. Instagram hits **both** \(\mu=0.25\) and \(\lambda=0.06\). Those are boundary estimates. [Scale, rate and variance guard](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/common_rankdiff.py:471) · [Fitted atlas](/Users/hindman/Documents/GitHub/rank-diffusion/llm_fitting/runs/2026-11-25_common_model/REPORT.md:49)

The cap matters for the moments being used. For \(E_5=\min({\rm Exp}(1),5)\), the first four raw moments are **0.99326, 1.91914, 5.25209, 17.63938**, versus **1, 2, 6, 24** without the cap. The third and fourth raw moments change by roughly **12.5%** and **26.5%**. The code still centers, filters and subtracts using the uncapped formulas. Its 64-week surge warm-up also leaves \(\rho_s^{64}=0.182\) of the initial mean discrepancy at a 26-week half-life. Reusing \(b_\xi\) gives a defensible one-restriction hypothesis and roughly unit top-\(K\) RMS. RMS normalization controls a second moment. The pooled third moment depends on \(E[c^3]\), and the empirical cohort is presence-selected. The effect of rank scaling should be tested in the estimating equations as well as in the simulator. **4. Persistent drift.** For known window weights \(a,b\), \(G_\rho=a^\top[\rho^{|j-k|}]b\) gives the stationary AR covariance. Suffix-summing the weights gives the corresponding \(G_d\) for the integrated drift. The code’s indexing and integrated variance formula are consistent with \(L_{t+1}=L_t+d_t+\cdots\). The inversion
\[
\hat\sigma_d^2=\left[\frac{\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(x,y)-\bar V_\xi G_\phi-V_sG_{\rho_s}}{G_d}\right]_+
\]
is a useful conditional moment estimate. Holding \(\rho_d=2^{-1/156}\) fixed makes one covariance sufficient to estimate an amplitude. It supplies no independent estimate of a three-year lifecycle. Shared trends, rank-specific slides, low-frequency supply changes and a misspecified passing process can produce the same covariance. **6. Filtering and forecasting.** The two-state covariance recursion is the standard one for \(H=(1,1)\). The four-state version correctly puts \(F_{L,d}=1\), has diagonal persistence \(1,\phi,\rho_d,\rho_s\), and observes \(L+\xi+s_{\rm obs}\). ### Evaluation defects that affect the scientific verdict

1. **Future-informed membership.** The cache builder selects and reranks the \(4K\) candidate universe using the entire panel. It also sorts columns by full-window permanent rank, which can break ties in later stable training sorts. The forecast runner recalculates top \(K\) inside those columns. An entity omitted using its future history cannot be recovered by a train-only sort. Facebook additionally uses a single full-era daily Spec-B curve for every origin. Instagram lacks the frozen exact per-origin membership path. The forecast dates are correct, but the input information set is not strictly historical. The magnitude and direction of the resulting score bias are unknown. 2. **The exploratory gate changes the statistic as well as the dates.** It fixes the last-training-week top 100, includes their later displacements whatever their starting rank, and computes pooled Spearman RACF. Frozen Gate v1 starts from the window’s top-200 cohort, restricts each displacement start to rank \(\le100\), and takes a median of within-entity Pearson autocorrelations. The exploratory baseline uses a recent test-length training segment. The frozen baseline uses the training window. The exploratory code averages per-member medians, whereas the frozen code pools displacements before taking the median. Test length, collision treatment of replacement identities, and bootstrap reporting differ too. The twelve rows were appropriately logged as exploratory. A common label does not make their errors comparable across harnesses. The benchmark counts `still_obs_below[1:] | ever_after[1:]`. An entity absent at \(t+1\) that later reappears *inside* \(K\) therefore counts.
