## 1. Editor's decision now

**Send out; not yet with enthusiasm.** (i) v5 is the first version whose frame is both correct and interesting: durability as what homogeneous random growth predicts, measured rank by rank, with the submissions head as the departure; the writing is now at exemplar density (median 21 words a sentence) and the registration record is better than anything a referee will have seen. (ii) The positive results are a measurement of standard theory, honestly labelled as such, so enthusiasm must come from the discovery section, and that section ends on three negatives (three forms fail) rather than a positive mechanism. (iii) The R12 comparison, which the abstract leans on ("at most three fifths"), has an undiagnosed problem: a planted annual spread of 0.5 should put a year-apart covariance of about 0.25 into the auxiliary statistic (the synthetic check recovers 0.097 of a planted 0.097), yet the calibrated world delivers 0.013/0.001/0.017 by stratum (`r12_grid__reddit_submissions_long__fixed.json`, `best.aux`), and in the comments body the sign is negative (−0.043); the worlds are therefore not planting what the reader is told they plant, and the "fail" is not yet secure. (iv) Several claims a referee can test from the SI alone are stated above their evidence (§7: "at most three fifths" against the 52-week comments match of +0.094 vs +0.095; "homogeneous growth misses top-ten turnover" against a matched-control band that contains the data). (v) The paper's genuinely new regularity, that decline persists and gains do not (+0.07 asymmetry against −0.04 to −0.10 in every world, including every heterogeneous one), is buried in the fifth subsection.

**Single change that most raises the odds:** before anything else, run the diagnostic the R12 design lacks, per world, the realized spread of mean growth among surviving communities and the fraction of (52σ_b)² that the auxiliary covariance recovers, with the drift re-solve and exits switched off in turn. Then restate R12 at the strength the diagnostic allows, and move the asymmetry to the abstract as the finding no symmetric form produces.

## 2. The frame and the title

**GLLM.** Earned as an order-of-magnitude statement and correctly scoped. The relaxation calculation (`gllm_relaxation_check.py`: 8S²/σ² = 13–34 years at K/64–K/4 against movement times of 2–4 years) is a calculation on measured parameters, not a test, and the SI says so. The paper's linearized τ_k = 2S*²ln(K/k)/σ² is ad hoc but reconciled with GLLM's λ at balance. One theorist's objection remains unaddressed in the main text: GLLM's λ is the spectral gap of a reflected Brownian motion; a rank-based system with drift by rank and re-selection has different convergence theory (Ichiba–Pal–Shkolnikov 2013; Baker & Smyth's no-spectral-gap remark, which the SI cites). Say in the main text that τ is "the reflecting-barrier rate evaluated at the measured parameters", not a property of this model. The transposition of GLLM's "fast transition" to a fall rather than a rise in concentration is apt, and the paper rightly reads the fall as a parameter shift (the slide quadrupled), not a transition dynamic.

**Stochastic portfolio theory.** Positioned correctly: S* = σ²/(2(s̄−e)) cited to Fernholz & Fernholz 2020 by equation; the paper claims measurement, not the identity; FIK 2013 is cited for name-specific drift. Fine.

**Gabaix.** Correct: barrier versus rank drift split at l. 95.

**Gomez.** Correct and used (decomposition, displacement term, selection baseline).

**Baker & Smyth (2026).** One clause (l. 135) maps their "turnover" to e_k. That is defensible per the round-6 check but they also predict the curve without fitting and test their time scales; this paper's prediction of the curve (flat start) comes out 37–41% too flat at the head. Say both in one sentence; a referee who knows the preprint will otherwise supply it.

**Platform research.** The uses/non-uses framing is the right bridge, and the Boik et al. sentence pre-empts "the coexistence is known".

**Durability as a contribution without novelty claims:** yes (l. 226: "a standard model that platform researchers can accept, and it is not widely used in their field"). **The title:** earned on comments, qualified by "When". The weakest word is **"attention"**: the paper insists three times that karma and interactions are engaged activity, not views, then puts attention in the title. Second weakest, "When": one platform, two ladders, and the condition (moderate decline, S*/S near 1) is inferred rather than tested across platforms. "Random growth and persistent decline in the hierarchy of online attention" (T3 Opus) names both findings and survives every referee I can imagine.

## 3. Each Results section

**R1, turnover while the shape changes slowly.** Sufficient; every number reproduces (`lockin_funnel__*.json`, `head_named__*`, `turnover_kinematics.json`). First attack: "27–33%" for the top ten is a range of three-year means (0.267, 0.333); by year it is 20–40%. Second: "at no cut-off and in no year did it move by more than a third of a log unit" is per year; cumulatively the submissions head shape fell by about half a unit, and the sentence should say "in any year".

**R2, random growth implies a hierarchy that outlasts its members.** Sufficient for comments, and the text says "on comments". First attack: Fig. 2A is titled "Shape outlasts members" while its own orange lines show the submissions relaxation time below the membership time; retitle the panel. Second: everything the balance says is an identity once c, s̄ and e are measured (the text concedes this), so the load-bearing content is the measurement design; a referee will ask for the per-cell distribution of S*/S, not only the pooled 0.64 (which I confirm: 60 half-year cells, c 0.0040, s̄ 0.0055, e −0.0007).

**R3, the model.** Sufficient and honest. First attack: the model's retention forecast beats carrying forward on submissions (0.028 vs 0.037) but not on comments (0.022 vs 0.013), and the fixed-home ablation beats it on submissions (0.024); the paragraph reports the first two and the SI the third. State in the main text that a simpler model does as well.

**R4, the head fell faster than the forecast.** The weakest heading. The homogeneous model's drift is the one "that holds the training ladder" (SI provenance table), so its 2022 forecast of the head share is near "no change" by construction; the paper's "The model misses one thing it should be able to see a year ahead" asserts a capability the hold closure does not have. The substantive evidence is the Gomez within term (−0.06 in 2019–20, the selection baseline; −0.24 and −0.26 after) and the rate law with the 2020–22 slide, which the second paragraph gives. Lead with that; present the forecast band as "a model with stable 2019–20 parameters".

**R5, persistence.** The strongest section and still undersold. First attack: l. 188 says removing each community's 2019 loading on the platform's total removes submissions persistence, and l. 191 says a factor world with 2019 loadings produces none. The SI reconciles them only implicitly: the world's loadings are empirical-Bayes-shrunk to four stratum means because the corrected loading variance is zero in three of four strata (A3). If the loadings are noise, the D7 removal is itself suspect (regressing out noise × a trending factor absorbs trend), and the SI already shows the cohort-mean version is membership-dependent. Say so in one sentence, or drop the D7 claim from the main text.

## 4. Heterogeneous growth (R12)

**Design.** The three forms, indirect inference on long differences (A2), the matched control (A4), the recovery runs and the pre-written scoring are the right standard pieces, and the amendments are disclosed with their timing (b113d0f, a042575, both before the outputs they govern; verified in `git log`). Three gaps:

1. **What is planted is not what is measured.** In the synthetic panel the gapped Pearson covariance recovers (52σ_b)² (planted 0.097, recovered 0.080–0.102). In the calibrated fixed world at s = 0.5 the same statistic gives +0.013, +0.001, +0.017 on submissions and +0.009, +0.022, −0.043 on comments, against a planted 0.25. The two-spacing lasting variance rises by 0.0018 (0.0064 → 0.0082), against the algebraic 0.0072 the SI itself quotes. Roughly 90% of the planted heterogeneity is therefore not reaching the statistics. Candidates in the code: `drift_hold_numeric` re-solved with the term present (8 passes × 52 weeks; it subtracts the order-statistic drift that heterogeneity creates, and with sorting the rank drift absorbs E[b | rank]); rank-dependent exit with rebirth and a fresh draw (`step_lasting`), which removes the negative tail and resets the bottom; and top-K selection. The negative covariance in the comments body points to rebirth. Until this is diagnosed, "calibrated to an annual spread of 0.5" describes a grid point, not a mechanism of that size, and the conclusion "standard forms fail even when calibrated" is not secure. This is also why the objective ran to the grid edge and the fit stayed at 6–11 SD per moment.

2. **Out-of-period forecasts for (a) and (b) are uninformative by construction.** Their drift is re-solved to hold the 2020 ladder, so they forecast no fall except through composition; only (c) carries 2021–22 information. R12-3 was rightly written for (c) only, but the main text says "none of the three forecasts the fall".

3. **Two registered recovery runs were not run and are not listed as not run:** the misspecification run (fit (a) to a (b) world) and the false-positive run (homogeneous world with a 26-week medium swing), A1 item 5. The second matters because A4 discloses that the calibration statistic "takes up medium-term variance". The SI registration list omits both.

**Conclusions and strength.**
- "At most three fifths": true at gaps of 13 and 26 weeks (0.25–0.60), false at 52 weeks on comments where the fixed form matches (+0.094 vs +0.095; the SI table shows it). Overstated as an unconditional bound.
- "What persists is decline, concentrated below the head": supported (asymmetry +0.07 vs −0.04 to −0.10 in every world; covariance by stratum −0.001/+0.024/+0.067). This is the paper's discovery and should be the headline.
- "The head's fall is a shift in its drift after 2020": supported by the Gomez terms and the rate law; but it is an accounting, not an identification, and the text says so.
- "Fixed differences reproduce the turnover of the submission top ten, which homogeneous growth misses": not supported against the matched control, 0.272 [0.133, 0.370], whose band contains the data's 0.333. The SI assessment table shows the closed-form control (0.23), not the matched one the scores use.
- R12-7 (symmetric forms give no asymmetry) is a valid model-versus-data discriminator but an expectation that could not fail; do not count it as a passed prediction.

**What an econometrician would still demand.** A J-statistic and an interval for the spread (with 9 moments and 2 parameters, J = 351 rejects; the confidence set is empty; say so). A rank-varying spread σ_b(k), since the diagnostic shows the mismatch is by rank and a single spread is the obvious straw man. The HIP/RIP false-positive run (Hryshko 2012's whole point). The planted-versus-recovered diagnostic above. Selection: the controls' negative persistence (−0.10) is regression toward the mean after cohort selection; the data's +0.17 is net of it, and the paper should state the implied gross effect.

**The proposed next test (type switching, mirrored).** It is the right mechanism for the asymmetry and for a lag profile that decays (submissions) or stays flat (comments), and it connects to the life-cycle literature. Two cautions: calibrated on 2019–20, its hazard will be too low to produce the 2020–22 fall, so register the out-of-period head share as expected to fail; and run the rank-stratified Gaussian spread first (one day, same harness), because a referee will ask why the symmetric family was not allowed to vary by rank before an asymmetric one was tried. Done together, with the planted-versus-recovered diagnostic, they would change a referee's view enough to be worth doing before submission.

## 5. What the paper should claim

Contributions, as I would state them: (1) the first rank-by-rank measurement of the random-growth balance on named attention units, with a permanent-rank design that avoids selection on noise and a crossing instrument validated in simulation (0.94; near-identity for diffusions; excludes jumps); (2) durability stated as time scales at measured parameters, holding on comments and not at the submissions head, with 5-year model-implied values; (3) geometry sets the turnover order: identical dynamics, data's ladder, +0.06 at the head and a 0.11 shift in the cultural-evolution profile exponent; (4) persistence of decline, not of gain, in participation, that no homogeneous world and no symmetric heterogeneous form reproduces; (5) the head's fall was in posting (22% → 9% of posts), five times the selection baseline; (6) four tools for platform research.

**Overstated:** "at most three fifths"; "homogeneous growth misses top-ten turnover"; "none of the three forecasts the fall"; "exposure to the platform's trend produces no persistence" (an underpowered construction); Fig. 2A's title; "they give no difference between risers and fallers" (they give −0.06, risers more persistent, as the control does).

**Undersold:** the asymmetry; the matched control's reproduction of two-year top-ten retention on comments (0.64 vs 0.65), which says lock-in at the very top is what random growth predicts; the estimator-free size result, which should be converted from rank to size units (p90 ratio 1.64 over 6.4 log units of size gives an elasticity of about 0.08 on comments, 0.04 on submissions, close to the structure-function values and far below firms' 0.15–0.2, and immune to the comments-estimator caution).

**Uses and non-uses:** convincing and useful; the SI tables are the best version of this I have seen in a draft. The main-text "rate" use ("a sixth to a third of the effect within five years") has no derivation in the tex comments or the record; give 1 − e^{−5/τ_k} from the SI table or delete it. **Horizons:** kept in the main text; Fig. 3B's 30-year axis is labelled model-implied; "two thirds within a decade" should carry the same label.

**Rewrites.**

*l. 141, split, first part:* "The slope that sets what newcomers return also sets how fast members leave. A member leaves its top set when its lasting move exceeds its distance to the boundary, and that distance scales with S_k (SI Appendix, Eq. S2; within 0.82–1.18 of observed in all 48 cells). The slope is flattest at the head, 0.7–1.0 against 1.2–2.3 lower down, so the top sets turn over fastest. This is geometry, not size. With identical dynamics and only the starting ladder changed, the data's ladder turns the top 78 over faster than the top 2,500 (0.21 against 0.14) and a ladder of constant slope does not (0.19 against 0.18); paired over runs the difference is 0.06 (0.03–0.09) (Fig. 2D)."

*Second part:* "Size itself gives little protection. Lasting volatility falls with size as size^−β with β = 0.05 on submissions and 0.06–0.13 on comments, below the 0.15–0.2 of firms. On simulated comment panels the estimator returns about half the planted exponent, so the comment values may be understated. A measure that needs no estimator agrees: from the top 1/64 of each universe to its lower half, the median and the 90th and 95th percentiles of the one-year change rise only 1.2- to 1.6-fold, an elasticity to size of about 0.04–0.08. The familiar funnel in a scatter of changes against rank is the extremes of strata thirty times larger."

*l. 175, opening of R4:* "A model with the parameters of 2019–20 forecasts no fall in concentration, because its drift holds the ladder it was fitted to. Run forward, it puts the 78 leading submission communities at 54% of their universe's activity in 2022 (5–95%: 48–72%); they held 36%. On comments the forecast is 60% (55–67%) against 50%. The question is therefore what changed after 2020."

*Abstract, last two sentences:* "Unlike homogeneous random growth, Reddit's changes persist across periods that share no weeks, and the persistence is asymmetric: declines continue, gains do not. Three standard forms of heterogeneous growth, two calibrated to that persistence, reproduce a quarter to three fifths of it at gaps of a quarter and half a year, none of the asymmetry, and, in the one form that carries the platform's 2021–22 path, none of the fall."
