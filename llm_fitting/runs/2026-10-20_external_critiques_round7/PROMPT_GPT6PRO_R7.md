## What the paper is

It studies why the hierarchy of online attention lasts while its members keep changing. The data are weekly activity of the leading Reddit communities (2019–2022, comment and submission ladders) and Facebook pages (2020–2022). It measures the ingredients of random growth rank by rank:
- lasting volatility;
- the relative decline ("slide") of each top set's members;
- the gain from risers crossing into the set;
- entry and exit.

From those it argues three things:
- **Durability is expected.** In homogeneous random growth with these ingredients, the shape of the hierarchy changes several times more slowly than its members turn over.
- **Turnover is geometry.** The top sets turn over fastest because the ladder is flattest at the head.
- **There are departures.** Concentration fell faster than calibrated homogeneous growth forecasts (most at the head of submissions after 2020), and Reddit's changes persist across periods that share no weeks.

## The constraints

These are the author's decisions; please respect them:
- **Frame.** Gabaix, Lasry, Lions and Moll's (2016) fast-transition problem. Durability, a hierarchy that outlasts its members, is presented as a property of homogeneous random growth. It is an important point for platform research, not claimed as new mathematics.
- **Horizons.** One to two years for individual communities and five years for the system. Longer durations appear only as model-implied rates.
- **Prior work.** The question was first posed in the author's 2018 book (*The Internet Trap*, ch. 5), which is cited.
- **Data status.** All analyses use development data. A 2023–24 extension is sealed for a registered test and has not been opened. Expected outcomes for each analysis were written down before it ran; failures are reported.
- **Heterogeneous growth.** Three standard forms of heterogeneous growth were calibrated and tested: fixed, autoregressive, and exposure to a platform trend. None reproduces most of the persistence, and none forecasts the fall. What persists is decline, below the head.

## History

Five earlier referee rounds by other models found and fixed real errors. The current draft answers them. Earlier criticisms included:
- that the answer is largely automatic in random-growth theory;
- that the title overclaimed;
- that persistence had been tested only against homogeneous worlds.
