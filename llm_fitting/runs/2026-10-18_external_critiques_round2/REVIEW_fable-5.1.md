## 1. Verdict now

Yes, this is closer to publishable than in round 1: the A–B contradiction is resolved by one dynamical picture (a wandering level held by drift by rank), the claim language was corrected where round 1 showed it wrong, Gomez and Batty are cited rather than rediscovered, the headline is falsifiable and legible to three readerships, and there is now one ahead-of-time test that passes (comments, 0.944). The single largest gap has moved: the generative model is validated at 1–13 weeks, where by construction it is model A, and it fails at 52 weeks, where every headline claim lives (retention too sticky at every scale, head index 0.52 against 0.36, kurtosis 3.6 against 29, momentum 0 against +0.37), so the paper still has no model of the annual-scale facts it leads with. Close behind: the headline exponent on submissions rests on one stratum (drop the top 78 in the feed-change year and β is 0.19, the firm value), and the submissions gate win is on a 30-week panel with 12–17-week train windows that the model's own recovery study says cannot identify its lasting parameters. A PNAS editor would now send this out; a referee who reads `gate_output.txt`, `structure_suite.py` and `size_variance_output.txt` would come back with the three points above. Fix those and the paper is hard to sink; leave them and the abstract's model sentence and exponent range are the first two things cut.

## 3. The model

**Design.**
- *Hold drift as a fixed point* (`rankdiff_rdm.py` `drift_hold_numeric`): a sound Atlas closure, honestly described as using only the ladder. But it imposes stationarity of the home ladder on ladders the paper says are not stationary (submissions' head 50% → 34%), which is why the hold variant gives a head index of 0.515 against the data's 0.359 while the move variant with measured slides gives 0.436. The one non-trivial check, hold drift −0.005 to −0.014 a week against measured slides 0.004–0.01, is the balance law again (s = σ²/2S), so it is not independent evidence for the model. The drift is an 80-point function solved to reproduce the ladder; a referee will call it a closure, not a mechanism, and should be told so.
- *Jumps rejected by the ladder's stability*: a good consistency argument against symmetric normal jumps carrying 70–98% of the lasting variance. It leaves the kurtosis of 29 (submissions) and 7.8 (comments) unexplained. If the excess kurtosis is collapses (bans, quarantines, going private; the 2020 ban wave), it is skewed, belongs in the exit hazard, and the symmetric-jump rejection is of a straw man. The skewness of 26-week changes and the share of the kurtosis carried by the largest 1% of |changes| are two numbers that would settle this; neither is in the record (§2z-dd §8 says within-entity re-identification was not done).
- *The moving home replacing the fixed home*: in the frozen form nothing is replaced. `step()`'s `movinghome` branch (lines 465–468) keeps A's κ(z) and σ_perm(z) knot profiles and reduces σ_perm² by σ_L²; σ_0, β and the drift function are added. §2z-dd §2 says this plainly; the dossier's §2 parameter count ("A's κ(z) and σ_perm(z) replaced by …"), frame c's Results 4 ("A's short-run block, plus a lasting level") and outline R4 imply a simpler model. The frozen model is strictly more complex than A, which bears on the paper's own simplicity criterion. Two further details: the "short-lag increments unchanged" claim is approximate (the home's increment enters L only through κ·Δhome, so the one-week variance falls by about (1−κ²)σ_L², and the 0.1·σ_perm² floor binds when σ_L² is large, as it is in the submissions gate fits); and κ_m and V_m are estimated and printed per split in `gate_output.txt` but never used by the frozen form, which a reader of that file cannot know.

**Evidence.**
- *Submissions gate.* The `reddit` panel has T = 30; `_gate_windows` gives test_len 13 and origins 12, 13, 14, 16, 17, so the five test windows overlap by 9–12 weeks and the train windows are 12–17 weeks (structure-function lags H = 6–8). The fitted σ_0 is 0.18–0.22 (σ_L² 0.03–0.05 a week) against 0.10 on the 212-week panel (s_band 0.0075–0.0136), β is 0.105–0.265 against the headline's 0.05–0.08, and the per-band s_band values run from 0 to 0.046 within one split (`gate__reddit.json`). The dossier's own recovery study (§2z-dd §6) says the lasting parameters are not recovered below 160 weeks. So 0.097 against 0.129 is a 13-week dispersion result ("less reversion than A over a quarter"), not evidence for a lasting random walk with size-law volatility. The scored medians are integers (dRank1 = 6, dRank4 = 9), so one unit is 11–17%, which is the size of the RDM–A gap. Paired by split (V3 log against `gate_output.txt`): RDM better on 5 of 5 splits on submissions, 2 of 5 on comments, 2 of 5 on Facebook.
- *Comments gate.* 0.152 against 0.137 is "non-inferior" by the +0.05 convention and worse on three of five splits; the error is concentrated in coll1 (0.82), as §2z-dd says. What §2z-dd does not say: the saved p90 of 13-week displacement is under-dispersed in every split (simulated 36–46 against observed 50–58, ratios 0.62–0.84), and on Facebook 148–202 against 140–638. The 90th percentile is saved but not scored; it contradicts the spirit of G2 ("the long-horizon spread grows as the data's"), which is listed as not adjudicated. The σ_obs scale calibration chose 0.0 and 0.1 on two comments splits, switching measurement noise off; it is the registered procedure, but a referee will ask what it compensates for.
- *Structure suite.* Scored honestly in §2z-dd §5: S1 fails on both Reddit ladders, S2 partial, S4 and S5 fail on Reddit, S7 fails on Reddit, S6 is non-discriminating (A gives 1.022). The RDM is further from the data than A on the head index on both Reddit ladders (0.515 against 0.497; 0.608 against 0.576) and on one-year retention on submissions. The paper's "turnover by scale" panel in `fig_rdm.png` C is therefore a failure panel, not a validation panel, and frame c lists it under validation.
- *Recovery.* Clean at 160 weeks (β error +0.006, σ_0 +7%), biased at 86 (+0.045 on β even at truth 0), six replications. `recovery.py` recovers with `variant="move"` and zero slides, so the hold closure is never put through recovery, and the truth is the model itself, so misspecification is untested.

**What they attack first:** the 30-week submissions gate as the headline validation. **Strongest fair criticism:** the model is validated at the horizon where it coincides with A and fails at the horizon where it differs from A; everything new in it (the wandering home, the size law, the hold drift) acts at years, and at years it is wrong in the same direction on every Reddit measure. The defensible presentation is "A with a wandering home: no loss at weeks, no return at a year on Facebook, and a measured list of what it misses on Reddit, led by momentum." That is a good Results section. "A generative model … validated on held-out movement" in the abstract is not.

## 4. Momentum

**Real as presented?** Largely yes. Spearman +0.373 and +0.320 for 2019→20→21, +0.449 and +0.431 for 2020→21→22 (`momentum_diagnostics.json` D2 "all"), half-year triples +0.11 to +0.34 (D1), Theil–Sen +0.365, survivorship-robust (D4), positive in every size stratum (D3). The shared middle block biases consecutive-change correlations negative when there is transitory noise, so the true momentum is larger, and the moving home's −0.089 on Facebook against the data's −0.076 is exactly what pure noise gives, which makes the Facebook contrast fair at 26-week blocks. Three qualifications. The Facebook null at the annual scale is one 26-week triple (three blocks in 86 weeks, Era A) plus four quarterly triples; the abstract's "Unlike Facebook" should say so. D2 does not test a lifecycle: "young" means first observed after week 26 of a panel that starts in 2019 (96 and 475 communities), so communities aged two to ten years are all "old"; §2z-de's "not a young-community lifecycle" should read "not a born-in-panel lifecycle". The abstract's "0.32–0.37" is one triple per ladder; the honest range is 0.32–0.45 at the annual scale.

**New?** I found no prior measurement of serial correlation of annual growth across Reddit communities (the lifecycle literature, Kairam et al. 2012 and the Panek group's aging study, does not test it). It has benchmarks that the paper should use: Coad (2007, *Rev. Ind. Org.* 31:69) finds positive serial growth correlation for large firms and negative for small ones, mixed overall, so "growth persists at every size with no size protection" is a sharper contrast with firms than β alone; and success-breeds-success at the item level (Salganik 2006; van de Rijt 2014) is the cumulative-advantage precedent that a community-level, whole-ladder, platform-contrasted momentum extends. I would call it new and not surprising, which is the right profile for a second result.

**Mechanism.** My ranking: (a) a stock-driven process: Reddit activity is produced by subscribers and habitual participants, a stock that integrates net joins, and integrated flows have smooth multi-year trends (momentum) without size protection; Facebook page interactions are a flow set by distribution, hence no momentum; (b) shared secular trends by topic (gaming, finance, politics) with heterogeneous loadings, which §2z-de names; (c) recommender amplification of risers (Home feed, r/popular), which predicts asymmetry: growth persists more than decline; (d) a residual capture artefact, made unlikely by D1.

**Tests with data on hand, in order of decisiveness.** (1) Split momentum exactly as `portfolio.py` splits variance: momentum of Δlog items against momentum of Δlog engagement per item. The stock mechanism predicts the items carry it; exposure mechanisms predict engagement per item carries it. One afternoon. (2) Factor residualization without names: extract a few principal components (or co-movement clusters) from the community × block matrix of changes and recompute momentum on the residuals; if it collapses, shared trends; if it survives, name-level trends. (3) Sign asymmetry: Spearman and slope separately for year-1 risers and fallers; (c) predicts asymmetry, (a) and (b) do not. (4) The structure function D(h) to 156 weeks on the long panels: momentum is superlinear growth of D(h), measured model-free, and it tells the RDM estimator what it is fitting beyond its 52-lag window and whether the velocity decays (AR) or not.

**Lead, report or wait?** Report, as Results 5, with the model's null for the differencing bias, the three diagnostics, and the two benchmarks above. Do not lead: the mechanism is open, the Facebook null is thin, and a lead result that no model reproduces invites "then what is the model for?". Do not wait: it is the most interesting new fact in the repository and the honest statement of the model's limit; tests (1)–(3) are cheap enough to run before submission.

## 5. The headline

"Size does not protect" is the right family: a measured contrast with a regularity three readerships know, which carries the entrenchment debate and leaves the balance as mechanism, so Gomez's precedence is acknowledged rather than contested. As stated it over-reads the numbers in three places.

- *Comments:* the sd of one-year change falls 1.58-fold over ×600 in size (0.670 → 0.424), against 3.6-fold at β = 0.2. "Barely shrinks" is doing the work; the honest sentence is "far less than for firms".
- *Submissions:* the year-1 β of 0.045 is produced by one stratum. The top 78 in 2020→21 (the feed-change year) have sd 0.785 against 0.304 for ranks 79–156; leaving that stratum out, the remaining six give β = 0.19 (recomputed from `size_variance_output.txt`), the firm value; year 2 gives 0.082 with an upper bound of 0.150. On comments the leave-one-stratum-out range is 0.067–0.081 and on Facebook 0.00–0.015, so the result is robust there. The record does not report this fragility and the abstract's "−0.01 to −0.08" hides it.
- *Facebook:* the sd is 0.84–1.01 at every size on a panel whose one-year change is dominated by transitory noise (Spec-B σ_obs; lasting β not computable at 86 weeks). A constant noise floor attenuates β toward zero; 0.008 is weak support until a noise-corrected version exists.

Two further risks. *Range and units* the memo already lists. *Precedence:* de Menezes and Barabási (2004, *PRL* 92:028701) found fluctuation scaling with exponent α ≈ 1 for daily visits to web pages over three orders of magnitude, their "externally driven" class, and Eisler, Bartos and Kertész (2008, *Adv. Phys.*) review it; that is the physics literature's version of β ≈ 0 for attention, published beside Stanley's firm result. The paper must cite it and differentiate: theirs is day-to-day fluctuation around a site's mean; this paper's is the lasting annual growth variance of named units, with turnover, displacement and momentum as consequences. Not citing it would be the Gomez episode again.

**A better lead.** Keep the contrast but state it as the measured fact and let the mechanism carry the novelty: "Leaders of online attention are nearly as volatile as newcomers" (title), with the abstract opening on the firm benchmark and closing on momentum. If the owner wants the mechanism in the title: "Attention moves as a unit: why size does not steady the leaders of online communities". Either is defensible once the submissions stratum and the fluctuation-scaling precedent are handled; "Size does not protect" survives only with "barely" quantified in the abstract.

## 7. Errors in the new material

1. `structure_suite.py` `metrics()` computes `share_K64_last` as the sum of expm1(block-mean log activity), the geometric-mean index of §2z-cw, and §2z-dd's tables (S4) and frame c ("the measured slides reproduce half of the fall", 0.436 against 0.359, set beside the arithmetic 50.2% → 34.0%) call it an "arithmetic share". The same mislabel round 1 caught, reintroduced.
2. `gate_output.txt` prints κ_m per split as a fitted parameter; the frozen form never uses κ_m or V_m (`step()` `movinghome` branch).
3. Dossier §2 "count of parameters" describes the pre-freeze variant; the frozen model retains A's κ(z) and σ_perm(z) and adds σ_0, β and the drift closure. Frame c and outline R4 imply replacement.
4. "σ_perm² reduced by σ_L² so that short-lag increments are unchanged" is approximate: the one-week variance of L falls by about (1−κ²)σ_L², and the 0.1·σ_perm² floor binds in the submissions gate fits.
5. Significance and abstract: "as well as or better than the previous model" — comments 0.152 against 0.137, worse on 3 of 5 splits; Facebook 0.123 against 0.103. Non-inferiority within +0.05 is not "as well".
6. Abstract "rank correlation 0.32–0.37": one triple per ladder; 0.43–0.45 for 2020→22; 0.11–0.34 at half-years.
7. §2z-de "not a young-community lifecycle" rests on 96 and 475 communities first seen after week 26 of 2019; it cannot speak to ages two to ten years.
8. Abstract "Unlike Facebook": the annual-scale Facebook null is one 26-week triple in Era A.
9. §2z-dd headline: momentum "also drives its too-sticky retention and too-concentrated head". The velocity experiment cut the retention error (0.056 → 0.021) and *raised* the head index (0.515 → 0.572); momentum does not explain the head. A hypothesis stated as a finding.
10. Frame c Results 4 lists "(C) turnover by scale" under validation; S2 is PARTIAL and S5 FAIL, and the RDM's retention exceeds the data's at every cut-off on both Reddit ladders.
11. §2z-dd §6: Facebook's bias-corrected β "about 0.05 … fits the near-zero raw β" — 0.05 against 0.002 does not fit, and the Facebook gate fits give β 0.08–0.41 across splits (`gate__facebook_a.json`).
12. The comments p90 of 13-week displacement is under-dispersed by 16–38% in all five splits (`gate__reddit_comments.json`); saved, unscored, unmentioned, and relevant to the unadjudicated G2.
13. `fig_rdm.png` C mixes 26-week (Facebook) and 52-week (Reddit) retention under "one block later" without saying so.
14. Outline abstract "16–21% of each Reddit top set is replaced each year" is comments only; submissions' one-year retention is 0.830–0.868 (13–17%).
15. Structure comparisons on comments used 10 seeds and the velocity grid 5, against the repository's own "at least 20 seeds" rule for head claims.
