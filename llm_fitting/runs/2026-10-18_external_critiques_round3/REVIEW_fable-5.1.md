## 1. Editor's decision now

**Send out, without the adjective yet.** The paper now has three measured facts that are new, legible to three readerships and honestly bounded (scale-free annual churn with the submissions instability shown; crossings equal to σ²/2S at thousands of ranks with a joint interval; persistent *decline* in participation that survives the averaging null), and its registration discipline is better than almost anything PNAS receives. It is held back by four things a referee will find in an afternoon: two of its headline "tests" (Eq. 1 and the flat-start slope) are near-identities presented as predictions; the model section's out-of-sample evidence is parity with a baseline that itself loses to persistence on submissions, obtained on the third attempt and quoted alongside a number from the abandoned version; the ranking-dynamics literature that already claims a universal pattern of rank stability (Iñiguez et al. 2022, Blumm et al. 2012, Batty 2006, all in `references.bib`, none cited) is absent, although the paper's "the top turns over fastest" contradicts it; and about a dozen numbers in the text disagree with the outputs behind them. None of these is fatal and all are fixable with data on hand. **The single change that most raises the odds:** write the introduction and Results 1 against Iñiguez et al. (2022) and Blumm et al. (2012), stating exactly which statistic differs (set replacement of the top k on an absence-penalised universe against rank flux at rank R) and why the top can be the least stable here; an editor triages on novelty against known work, and today the paper reads as unaware of the one literature whose referees it will draw.

## 2. The title and the central claim

"Continual turnover sustains a slowly changing hierarchy of online attention." The contradiction is stated literally and the genre fit is good. **The weakest word is "sustains."** What is measured is a balance: crossings return σ²/2S (0.94, 0.86–1.04) and on comments that gain equals the slide. "Sustains" asserts the counterfactual, and the paper's own counterfactual says that at the panel's horizon the hierarchy barely needs sustaining: with churn switched off, the comments head flattens by 0.038 log units a year against a span of about 3.9 (`out_churn_counterfactual_reddit_comments_ext.txt`), and the record's relaxation times are 5–22 years on comments (§2z-cp). Over a four-year panel the hierarchy is held up mostly by its own memory; churn and slide act at the margin, each about 0.003 a week. The causal reading of "sustains" therefore rests on the stationary theory and the 30-year simulation, not on the four-year measurement. The second-weakest phrase is "slowly changing": the submissions head lost a third of its share in three years. **This is also the short seller's case** against the load-bearing claim: "the title says turnover sustains the hierarchy; the paper's own Fig. 4C says that removing it changes the head by 1–2% a year; the paper's own §2z-cp says the ladder takes a decade or two to respond; so the data cannot distinguish 'sustained by churn' from 'has not had time to change', and the only thing that can is the model, whose steady state misses the observed slope by 20–50% at every rank (Fig. 4B, open against filled)." A title with the measured verb: **"Rapid turnover balances the slow hierarchy of online attention"** (9 words), or, keeping the owner's wording, "Constant turnover keeps the hierarchy of online attention in balance." "Balances" is earned at every rank where it holds and names the imbalance where it tips.

## 3. Each Results section

**(1) The puzzle.** Sufficient, with two corrections. "16–21% of each Reddit top set is replaced … from the top 195 to the top 12,500" (l. 65) is the mean over year pairs at three cut-offs (§2z-cu: retained 0.79–0.84); the per-year cells in `out_turnover_kinematics.txt` run 0.154–0.256, and submissions' head cells are 0.19–0.26. Say "about a fifth, 15–26% by year and cut-off." The Significance's "a fifth or more of every top set" (l. 37) is wrong at the universe boundary on comments (16%). First attack: "the top turns over fastest" rests on top-10 and top-25 sets over three year pairs (retention quantised at 0.1; comments 0.7, 0.8, 0.7), and on comments the top-10 and top-25 replacement rates are identical (0.267) against 0.21 at k = 195. The contrast is real on submissions and Facebook and modest on comments; and it contradicts Iñiguez et al. (2022), who report greater stability at the top across 30 systems with a different statistic. That paper must be met head on.

**(2) Scale-free churn and the turnover formula.** The exponent paragraph is now the strongest in the paper: fold change, leave-one-out, extension to 4K, lasting part, the submissions instability all stated, and `robustness_output.txt` matches every number. The fluctuation-scaling precedent is cited. First attack: **Eq. 1 is an identity posing as a test.** Given the observed boundary move Δθ_k, the observed changes of the names in (k/2, k] and the local slope, the count leaving the top k is the empirical version of the same integral; the formula's only content beyond bookkeeping is that Δ_i does not depend on height within the band and that the density is locally exponential, which is what S_k means. A 48/48 pass within ±18% is therefore unsurprising, and the registered linear version failed only because 1 − e^{−x} ≠ x at x ≈ 0.3 (ratio 1.16, which is the median over-prediction seen). The exact form was derived after the failure (`turnover_kinematics.py` docstring; commit b45a714 at 16:05, registered run at 16:00), which is disclosed, but the derivation is a Taylor correction, not a finding. Present Eq. 1 as a decomposition: turnover splits exactly into churn (E[D⁺], flat at 0.21–0.40 across scale) and slope (0.67–2.29, rising down the ladder). That is the scientific content and Fig. 2C carries it; "it reproduces the observed turnover in all 48 cells" (l. 82) should go. Also say in the main text that Facebook post counts do steady with size (item-count β 0.187, A7-4 FAIL); it is in the SI only.

**(3) The balance.** Sufficient and the paper's core. The joint bootstrap (A9) answers round 2; the per-ladder honesty is good (submissions 0.80, 0.63–1.01; the 300–600 shortfall; the out-of-time 0.62). First attack: V9 remains open. The RDM's own simulations give boundary ratios structured by cut-off (0.68–0.97 at k = 625–1,250; 1.16–1.30 at 2,500–5,000) with known lasting variance, so the by-rank test still lacks a model-derived null and the submissions shortfall may be the estimator meeting the size law; the SI says so, the main text does not. Second: the introduction's "They do not show, for a real hierarchy, that the measured churn is what holds it up, rank by rank" (l. 55) will be read against Gomez's Appendix F, which applies the decomposition at every income percentile (V11). Write the novelty as it is: a test of the *predicted* crossing term against *measured* crossings, with bands, where passing swings are first-order.

**(4) The generative model.** Not sufficient for its lead sentence as written. Three problems. (a) The held-out-movement evidence is parity with A at 1–13 weeks, where the paper itself says the lasting process is invisible, and on submissions neither model beats persistence (0.163). (b) The sentence mixes versions: "0.183 against 0.188 … 0.130 against 0.123" is the corrected `movinghome_vp`; "better in all five splits (0.097 against 0.128)" (l. 125) is the **frozen** form on the 30-week `reddit` panel (GATE_LEDGER row at 96be22f), the form the next sentence says failed. The corrected variant was never run on `reddit`, `reddit_comments` or `facebook_a`. (c) The out-of-period structure test (A6) was run with the frozen form (`structure_oop.py:117`, `lasting in ("home", "movinghome")`), so the one structural, out-of-period number in the paper belongs to the abandoned version. Further, the "corrected" arm's scores are the third try at this gate (frozen → vp without drift by a bug → vp), its own A5b-2 expectation failed (13-week median within ±1 in 2 of 5 splits; it now overshoots by 0–3, `gate_movinghome_vp__reddit_submissions_long.json`), and the arms are separated by less than one quantum of the integer medians they are scored on (dRank1 = 3 or 4 on submissions, so one unit is 25–33%). First attack: "a model whose two new ingredients each hurt on one panel (wander+drift 0.130 against wander 0.100 on comments; 0.183 against fixed+drift 0.171 on submissions), that no horizon can distinguish from the fixed-home model, and whose out-of-sample structural forecast is matched by carrying last year's retention forward." The defensible main-text claim is structural and counterfactual, as RESULTS A5c says; the gate belongs in one sentence and the SI.

**(5) Persistence and the falling head.** Sufficient and the best new result since round 2. The gapped statistic, the simulated null, the participation split, the sign asymmetry and the aggregate-loading result are all reproduced in `out_momentum_decomposition_*.txt`. First attack, from the econometrician: the sign asymmetry is a within-subgroup Spearman after splitting on the sign of change 1, which is sensitive to the left tail (collapsing communities keep collapsing); the quintile means in the same output show risers do continue, mildly (mean rank of change 2 0.547 for the top change-1 quintile against 0.399 for the bottom), so "among those that rose it is zero" (l. 136) is true of the within-group correlation and not of the conditional mean. Report both. From the inequality economist: in `gomez_decomposition.py:56` the between term is (W_b − Σ_{P_a} t_b)/W_a with W_b the maximal top-k sum at b, so it is **non-negative by construction**; "displacement added a steady +0.05 to +0.08" is a reordering gain whose positivity is not a finding and whose steadiness is partly the stationarity of passing noise. The within term for a top set selected on the current year's total includes regression to the mean: 2019→20 gives −0.061 within against +0.052 between with the share unchanged, which is the pure-noise signature. The 2020–22 within terms (−0.24, −0.26) are far beyond that baseline, so the substance stands, but the paragraph should state the baseline and the decomposition should be rerun on permanent-rank cohorts, as round 2 asked.

## 4. The model in the main text

The core is stated nearly correctly and nearly minimally; three mismatches with `rankdiff_rdm.py`. (i) The text's L (Eq. 5) is the code's home H; the code's L is an OU deviation toward H with model A's κ(z) and σ_perm(z) (`_State.step`, ll. 458–480). The SI says this; the main text's "the passing parts — a swing that decays within weeks and observation noise" (l. 119) hides a second, slower passing component with A's half-lives, and the model therefore keeps every parameter of A and adds σ₀, β and the drift closure (round-2 V8). One sentence in the main text should say so; "minimal" it is not. (ii) "it subtracted the wandering level's whole variance from the fast swings" (l. 125): it was subtracted from the reverting component's innovation σ_perm² (l. 479), not from the fast swing ξ. (iii) The drift by rank in the simulations is the rank of the home among homes (`hrank`), while exits use the rank of L; fine, but "drift that depends only on rank" should say which. The algebra is right: I verified Var ΔL = (2q + κσ_L²)/(2 − κ), the frozen form equal to A's minus exactly σ_L², and q = σ_p² − κσ_L²/2 as the preserving choice; `test_variance_preserving_home_keeps_model_A_one_step_variance` and `test_corrected_moving_home_applies_drift_by_rank` exist and check the right things. Not contradicted by the gates, but not supported by them either: the gates say the four arms are indistinguishable (0.017 and 0.030 spread). What the main text omits that the gates show: the 13-week 90th percentile is still wrong (vp comments T0 = 53: 74 against 53; submissions T0 = 53: 46 against 34), and the submissions kurtosis is 22 against 5 (A6). "What it lacks is persistence" should read "persistence and tails."
