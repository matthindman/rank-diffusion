# Referee report, round 8: manuscript v7 (`paper/pnas_draft.tex`, `paper/pnas_si.tex` at the §2z-dq commit)

Everything below was checked against the tex sources, the compiled `pnas_draft.pdf` (8 pages), `make_v5_figures.py`, `r16_tier3.py`, `r16_capture.py`, `rankdiff_rdm.py`, `tests/test_rankdiff_rdm.py`, and the saved `r16_*.json`, `r15_*.json`, `r12_grid__*`, `r12_moments__*` outputs. ## 1. Editor's decision now

Send out, and closer to enthusiasm than v5 was, but not yet with it. The paper now has a positive core that is honest (the shape moves with the leaders' decline; the crossing gain is steady), a model-based time-scale result that replaces a wrong approximation, a capture audit, and a discovery (declines persist, gains persist less) that no symmetric world reproduces. What holds enthusiasm back is that the two model results added in v7 are stated above their edges: the joint fit sits at a corner of its grid on submissions and fails on comments, and the decline state gets its sign at a point rejected twenty-five times over, with a lasting variance 1.4 to 2.4 times the data's. The asymmetry, which is the paper's best claim, is still reported without a band and with two statistics that give different levels for the risers. The single change that would most raise the odds: make the asymmetry a banded, within-stratum, one-statistic result (bootstrap over communities and origins; demeaned and raw splits; by stratum; before July 2021; with and without collapses) and move it into the first half of the abstract, while cutting the main text from eight pages to PNAS's six.

## 2. The paper after R16

Stronger as a PNAS paper, for three reasons. The durability claim is now a model statement (the calibrated model absorbs a step in decline in 3 to 4 years, 0.7 to 1.0 times the membership time) rather than a formula the model turns out not to obey. R12's "at most three fifths" is withdrawn on evidence rather than argued around. And the asymmetry has been tested against the natural rival (type switching) and survives as the one thing no calibrated form produces.

Weaker in one respect: the paper has traded a clean negative ("standard heterogeneity cannot reproduce the persistence") for a hedged positive ("jointly estimated fixed differences reproduce most of it, at a grid corner, with a passing swing whose half-life is now 2.7 years, at the cost of turnover and the boundary ratio"). The abstract's final two sentences carry that trade and read as a model-fitting footnote.

The main contribution is clear in the Discussion's first paragraph (l.235) and not yet in the title or the abstract's opening. The title should keep the question ("outlasts its members") because that is what the balance explains; the abstract should lead with the two-flow account in two sentences, then state the asymmetry as the discovery in the middle, not the end, and drop the last sentence about mean-growth differences into the Results. A title that names both findings would be "Random growth and persistent decline in the hierarchy of online attention" (an owner item).

## 3. R16's designs and readings

**R16-A (decline state).** Design: a two-state Markov drift, centred, calibrated on R12(b)'s 15 moments at 3 seeds a point, is a fair mirror of GLLM's type switching. Reading: "gets the sign, not the size" is right, but three facts should accompany it. (i) The optimum (π 0.25, s 0.7, half-life 208 weeks) is at the grid's largest spread and longest half-life on both ladders and the objective is still falling (`r16_decline_grid__*.json`: at π 0.25, hl 208, objective 1025 → 598 → 382 across s 0.3/0.5/0.7 on submissions; 2964 → 1647 → 1106 on comments). A half-life equal to the panel's length makes the "reversible" state a fixed skewed type in practice; the result is better described as "a skewed two-point distribution of growth types gives the sign". (ii) At that optimum the lasting-variance moments are badly missed: world sf [0.0072, 0.0094, 0.0154] against target [0.0050, 0.0059, 0.0100] on submissions (z² 21, 91, 141 of the total 382); [0.0084, 0.0138, 0.0190] against [0.0039, 0.0057, 0.0138] on comments (z² 32, 350, 292 of 1106). The sign is obtained in a world with far more lasting movement than Reddit has. (iii) On comments the decline world's within-stratum covariances at gap 0 are negative in every stratum (−0.071, −0.024, −0.010 against the data's +0.011, +0.030, +0.066) while its pooled Spearman is positive (+0.049): its "persistence" is a between-stratum composition effect, not persistence within strata. A spread of 0.7 means a quarter of communities drift 1.2 log units a year below the rest; the paper's own quantile table (SI l.81–84, p90 of |Δ| 0.67–0.73 at the head) suggests the decline world's spread of annual changes is far outside the data's, and this was not checked. Edges: the registered expectations named π 0.1 and four of six failed; π 0.25 is reported as a profile, which is correct practice, but the main text (l.205) presents the π 0.25 result without saying the registered value failed.

**R16-B (joint re-estimation).** Design is standard for income processes and the 24-moment vector is sensible. The reading overstates. On submissions all four parameters are at grid edges, not two: s 0.8 (maximum; profile 188 → 47 → 39), head 0.25 (minimum), km 0.5 (minimum; profile 39 → 338 → 1752), m 0.8 (minimum; `r16_joint_grid__reddit_submissions_long.json`). SI l.391 names only km and head; RESULTS adds s; nobody mentions m. The argmin is a bound, not an estimate, and the objective is still falling steeply in km, i.e. toward a medium swing slower than 2.7 years, at which point a "passing" swing and slowly decaying heterogeneity in level are not distinguishable. "Fixed differences in growth fit on submissions" (l.199) rests on the 9-moment objective (8.0, which I verified from the grid's aux vector against `r12_grid__reddit_submissions_long__fixed.json`'s target and sd) scored against a correct-specification distribution computed for a different world; the 24-moment objective it was actually estimated on (38.8) has no such calibration. The grid omitted s = 0 at km 0.5, so "a slower swing alone" was never tested (l.199 says a faster one produces none, which is true and not the relevant case). On comments the fit is rejected (487; sf z² 248) and its stratum pattern is wrong: −0.00, +0.14, +0.06 against the data's +0.11, +0.14, +0.15, so the main text's "rising down the ladder as the data's does" is false on comments (the SI's own table l.399 shows it). The costs the paper lists (top-ten turnover 0.18 against 0.33; boundary ratio 1.22 against 0.80) are large and should be in the main text sentence, not three sentences later.

**R16-C.** Standard (a parametric bootstrap of the objective under the null) and sufficient for its purpose. Two small caveats: the correct-specification world sits at the registered grid's edge (s 0.5 is the maximum), so "chosen s 0.5 in 20 of 20" says nothing about bias; and the weights are the data's bootstrap sd, so the distribution is of the data's objective, which is what is needed. The deviation from A1 (grid not re-simulated per replicate) is disclosed and minor.

**R16-D.** Standard and sufficient (10 panels per truth; verified means 0.108, −0.012, 0.063, 0.061 from `r16_beta__*.json`). One point for the text: on submissions the estimator returns 0.06 when the truth is 0, so the data's 0.045 is below the estimator's null, which the paper reads correctly as "none that can be detected".

**R16-E (capture).** The boundary-against-placebo design is the right one for what the daily panels can show, and the within-file sawtooth (median mid-month step −0.024 against +0.000 at boundaries, verified) is a genuine power demonstration. What it cannot exclude: a relative capture rate that drifts smoothly; removal rates in the large communities rising over time (moderator removals happen within hours, so a three-week delay already misses them and the July 2021 change would not show, which makes the null at that boundary the expected null under either reading); and changes in score updating, to which karma per item is sensitive (the 27 April 2020 pocket, −0.17 in the head's karma share, shows that). The main-text reading ("not the cause in any simple way", l.189) is at the right strength. SI l.23's "within-year change of −0.93" is the sum of four within-block changes (−0.028, −0.315, −0.377, −0.206), not a within-year change.

**R16-F (durability in the model).** The post hoc lag-aware fit is legitimate: the registered form ignored the model's own observation lag through the passing swing, a specification error, not tuning, and the fitted τ is not a parameter chosen against a score. I reproduced both fits from the saved block responses (registered τ 1.47/2.17; lag-aware 3.72/3.43 at m 1, SSE 0.0002/0.0003). But: (i) no script in the repository produces `r16_durability_scores.json` (grep finds the file only in `make_v5_figures.py`); deposit the fitting code. (ii) τ is weakly identified from five nearly linear points. For submissions at m 0.7 the increments are −0.09, −0.12, −0.11 and the SSE profile is flat above τ ≈ 4 (0.0004 at 4.5, 0.002 at 6, 0.006 at 8); by year 5 the shape has reached only 44 to 65 per cent of the fitted asymptote. Extend the run to 520 weeks or report τ with an interval. (iii) The comparison standard shares the model's scaling: both τ and the membership time scale as S²/σ², so a ratio that "hardly depends on m" (R16-F3) is expected; the content is the prefactor 0.8 against the linearized 2 ln(K/k)(S*/S)². In the model itself the hold drift makes S* = S, so the linearized factor is 2 ln 64 ≈ 8.3 and the linearized τ about 19 years on comments against the model's 3.4: a five- to six-fold overstatement, not the 2.6 reported for R16-F2 (which used the data's S*). The solid "shape relaxes (linearized)" line in Fig. 2A is therefore not a prediction of the model whose stars sit beneath it. (iv) The shock is local to the top K/64 while GLLM's slow transition concerns a parameter change at every rank; one more run with a uniform step in the slide, reporting K/16 as well, would settle whether "no separation" holds in GLLM's own sense.

**R16-G.** Holding the joint optimum fixed and adding a decline state adds growth variance to moments that were already fitted, so G1's failure is close to mechanical and the design cannot answer whether skewed and symmetric heterogeneity can combine. The record says so (RESULTS l.1638); the main text's "added to the jointly estimated differences it leaves their symmetry in place" (l.205) should say "at the joint optimum, with its spread held fixed". A joint calibration with both spreads free, scored on untargeted asymmetry statistics, is the test that remains, and it is not expensive by R16's standards.

## 4. Each Results section

**R1 (turn over while the shape changes far less).** Sufficient for the heading. First attack: "far less" compares a mean-log span (−11 to −13 per cent) with a membership share (a third of the head in two years), and the arithmetic share fell by 16 points at the head of submissions; a referee will ask for the comparison in commensurable units (the model's own turnover-to-shape ratio). Second: "A constant chance of leaving would give about 1.8" (l.107) is right for independent yearly replacement, but pure diffusion gives about 1.4, and the observed 1.3 to 1.7 lies between the two, so the inference "concentrated near the boundary, some of which return" is weaker than stated.

**R2 (random growth shows when the hierarchy outlasts its members).** The accounting is sufficient and the paper says it is near an identity. First attack: the "when" is read from five half-year cells at one cut-off on comments (SI l.176). Second: the time-scale paragraph (l.155) still carries the linearized factor "1 to 4" beside the model's 0.7 to 1.0, and a referee will ask which the paper believes; the model's number should stand alone, with the linearized formula demoted to the SI as an approximation the model does not obey.

**R3 (a model forms a hierarchy, but flatter at the head).** First attack: forming a hierarchy from equality is generic to any diffusion with a rank-dependent drift measured from the data's own slides, so the positive part is thin, while the 37 to 41 per cent shortfall at the head is a failure at the one place the paper cares about. The section is honest about both; the heading could carry the failure more plainly.

**R4 (with stable parameters, random growth forecasts neither head's fall).** A hold-drift model cannot forecast a fall by construction; G31's wording is fixed, but the heading still presents a non-forecast as a finding. First attack: the fall is in posting and coincides with the surge, the ban wave and a platform-wide decline of submissions, none identified; the capture audit excludes steps, not drifts.

**R5 (declines persist and gains do not).** First attack: under the statistic compared with the worlds (`r12_hetgrowth.asymmetry`, a raw-sign split), the data's risers persist at +0.069 (submissions) and +0.042 (comments) against fallers' +0.141 and +0.113 (`r16_moments__*.json`): gains persist about half as much, not "not at all". The "0.00 to +0.03" in l.202 comes from the true-sign split that pools origins with one cohort, which is not the construction used anywhere else in the paper. Second: no band on the +0.07. Third: within strata, persistence at the head of submissions is zero (−0.003 in the top K/16; SI l.399), so the persistent decliners cannot be the submissions head's fall; the last sentence of l.205 should say this rather than "we have not shown".

## 5. The asymmetry as the discovery

*Statistic.* Two constructions, two riser levels (above). Pick the world-comparable one everywhere, report both splits in the SI, and add the demeaned `tail_followup` numbers (−0.27/+0.03; −0.22/+0.05) as the headline, since they are least exposed to selection. *Selection.* The raw-sign split makes the minority-sign group a noise-selected one under a platform trend; the strongest answer is already in the data and unstated: the two ladders had opposite platform trends after 2019 (`r15_d3_loading_placebo.json`: −0.24 on submissions, +0.45 on comments) and the same +0.07. Say it. *Mean reversion.* Handled by the gapped design; the symmetric worlds give −0.04 to −0.10, which is the right baseline. *Capture.* Persistence survives ending before July 2021 (R4b), but the asymmetry itself was not computed on that subsample. *Survivorship.* `wmean` requires 90 per cent presence in both windows, so communities that collapse to absence in the follow-up window are dropped; this biases against the asymmetry and should be stated as such. *Bands.* None are given for +0.07 or for the tail follow-ups; with six origins and thousands of communities the bootstrap is cheap.

*Novelty.* Against Guvenen et al. (2021): they find sign- and position-dependent persistence of earnings changes, with negative changes more persistent at the top; the paper's finding is the attention analogue and is cited as such (l.205), so the claim should be "the pattern known from earnings at the top of the distribution appears in community attention, where no theory predicted it", not that the asymmetry is new in kind. Against the life-cycle literature (Halfaker 2013; TeBlunthuis 2018; Cunha 2019): decline as a phase is documented; what is new is the measurement of the asymmetry as a statistic against a calibrated random-growth null, on named communities, with the symmetric and type-switching forms both failing. That is a real contribution if banded.

*What would make it convincing.* Bands; one statistic; by stratum; the opposite-trend argument; the demeaned transition statistic as the lead; a world with a platform-wide negative drift (one run) to show the split does not manufacture the sign; and the registered test on the sealed 2023–24 data with the sign and size written down first.

*Density.* l.155 and l.199–205 carry eight numbers a sentence. *Concrete rewrites.*

l.155, replace from "Linearized, the shape relaxes ..." to the end of the paragraph with: "In the calibrated model itself, a lasting rise of 0.2 a year in the head's decline reaches the observed shape after about a year, through the passing swing, and is then absorbed with a time constant of 3 to 4 years at the measured variance (2.8 to 4.5 across variance multipliers 0.7 to 1.4), 0.7 to 1.0 times the e-folding time of the head's membership on both ladders. A reflecting-barrier approximation to the same model overstates that time several-fold (SI Appendix). The hierarchy of comments held in 2020–22 because the decline sat near the crossings it draws, not because the shape responds slowly."

l.199, last four sentences: "Estimated jointly with the passing swing, and with a smaller spread at the head, fixed differences in growth reproduce most of the persistence (+0.15 against +0.17 on submissions, +0.11 against +0.15 on comments at a 13-week gap), at the cost of a passing swing that reverts half as fast, a top ten that turns over half as often as the data's, and a crossing ratio of 1.22 against 0.80. The estimate sits at the edge of its grid in every parameter on submissions and is rejected on comments, where it gives the head no persistence. Persistence as such is therefore consistent with communities that differ in their mean growth, within what these moments can tell from differences that decay over two years."

l.205, second to fourth sentences: "A skewed decline state, which a quarter of communities occupy with a half-life as long as the panel, is the only form that gives the asymmetry the data's sign on both ladders and the data's ordering after large falls and rises; it does so at a lasting variance 1.4 to 2.4 times the data's, with a third to two fifths of the persistence, and it is rejected. Added to the jointly estimated differences with their spread held fixed, it does not move the asymmetry."

Abstract, last two sentences: "Unlike homogeneous random growth, Reddit's changes persist across periods that share no weeks, and what persists is mostly decline: a fall in the bottom tenth is followed by a further fall of a quarter of a log unit, a rise in the top tenth by almost nothing. Communities that differ in their mean growth reproduce the persistence; no symmetric form reproduces the asymmetry."
