## What the paper is

It studies why the hierarchy of online attention lasts while its members keep changing. The data are weekly activity of the leading Reddit communities (2019–2022, comment and submission ladders) and Facebook pages (2020–2022). It measures the ingredients of random growth rank by rank:
- lasting volatility;
- the relative decline ("slide") of each top set's members;
- the gain from risers crossing into the set;
- entry and exit.

It now argues:
- **Durability is the balance.** The crossing gain is steady, set by volatility and the ladder's slope. The hierarchy holds while the leaders' decline stays near it and moves within a year or two when the decline jumps. In the calibrated model itself, the shape responds to a change in decline on the time scale on which members turn over. The paper no longer claims that the shape changes "several times more slowly" than members turn over.
- **Turnover is geometry.** The top sets turn over fastest because the ladder is flattest at the head.
- **The departures.**
  - Concentration fell faster than calibrated growth forecasts, and the fall is not an artefact of the archive's capture changes.
  - Reddit's changes persist across periods that share no weeks. Fixed differences in growth, estimated jointly with the passing swings, reproduce that persistence. What no symmetric model produces is its asymmetry: declines persist and gains do not.
  - A skewed "decline state" model, a mirror of Gabaix, Lasry, Lions and Moll's type switching, gets the asymmetry's sign but not the persistence. The two together do not combine.
