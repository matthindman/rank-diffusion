## 1. Editor's decision now

**Send out, still without the adjective.** Five reasons. (i) The puzzle, the data (three ladders, four years, named units, thousands of ranks) and the registration record are what PNAS wants, and v2 fixed most of round 3: null worlds, the literature, Eq. 1 as a decomposition, one model, the persistence repairs, nearly all of the W19 numbers. (ii) The automatic/discovered split, which is now the paper's organising device, contains an error: "a balance in which crossings fall short of the slide" is listed as a discovery (SI l. 205; RESULTS R3-A), yet three of the four null worlds have c < s̄ (`null_world__atlas.json` 0.0027 < 0.0048; `__neutral` 0.0044 < 0.0048; `__rdm` 0.0030 < 0.0037); only the driftless Gibrat world, which has no stationary state and is spreading from its Zipf start, has c > s̄. Fig. 3B shows this: three of the four crosses sit below the diagonal. (iii) The null worlds are one seed each (`null_worlds.py` l. 47–115, 228–230), so the top-10 turnover that carries "size-blindness exposes the top" is 10/30, 9/30, 5/30 and 1/30 name-years, reported to two decimals with no band, against the repository's own rule that head claims need bands and 20 seeds. (iv) The size-blindness headline compares an attenuated estimator (SI l. 205: planted 0.2 returns 0.11) with the firm literature's 0.15–0.2, while the paper's own structure-function fit of the lasting β on comments is 0.126 on the whole panel (`out_r3c_churn_counterfactual_reddit_comments_ext_flat20.txt`) and 0.056–0.092 on the gate windows (`gates_reddit_comments_ext.txt` l. 6–10); none of these appears in the β section. (v) Persistence is the one discovery that survives every check I could run, and it is well done. **The single change that most raises the odds:** make the null-world comparison statistically real: 20 seeds with bands for every world, a planted-β ladder (0, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2) through the paper's own estimator with the data's passing parts, and a size-blind world started from the data's ladder shape rather than Zipf S = 1. Then restate the discovered list with what survives (size-blindness as calibrated, persistence), and drop the flattening balance from it.

## 2. The title and the question

The question is answered in the stationary sense and demonstrated in the model: the hierarchy's steepness is churn over slide (S* = σ²/2s̄), a flat start builds a ladder with that steepness (Fig. 4A–B, 20 seeds now), and measured crossings return about three quarters of the slide (0.0040 against 0.0055 a week over the 60 half-year cells, which I recompute as 0.724). It is not answered in the data at the panel's horizon: with churn removed the comments head moves 0.04 log units a year against a span of 3.9 (SI table l. 102–104), entry and exit stay active in those worlds, and the record's relaxation times are 5–22 years. So the four-year data cannot distinguish "held up by turnover" from "has not had time to change"; the title's verb rests on the model and on Fernholz's identity.

**The weakest word is "holds up"**, with "durable" close behind and "the" third. "Durable": the submissions head lost a third of its share in three years, the arithmetic share fell in all 18 cells, and the comments mean-log shape does not even move in one direction (section 6, item 2). "The": three ladders, one platform family. Titles that keep the owner's question and are earned by what is shown:
- "Constant turnover sets the steepness of a durable hierarchy of online attention" (declarative; the stationary identity plus Fig. 3A and 4B; makes no counterfactual claim about collapse);
- "How constant turnover holds up a durable hierarchy of online attention" (already an ALT in the source; "how" marks it as a mechanism account rather than an established counterfactual).

**The short seller's case**, built from the repository: "Of the three discoveries, one (the flattening balance) appears in three of the paper's own four null worlds; one (the exposed top) is 10 against 9 against 5 name-years at a single seed, in a Zipf-start world that is flat from k = 78 to K/2 (0.23–0.25) while the data fall 0.21 → 0.16–0.18, so the data's gradient beyond the top 25 is the ladder's curvature, not size-blindness; and one (β ≈ 0) uses an estimator the SI itself says turns 0.2 into 0.11, in a paper whose own model fits β = 0.126 on comments. The title is about those three. Persistence, the discovery that stands, is the one the title does not mention."

## 3. Each Results section

**(1) The puzzle.** The lead holds for the arithmetic shares (all 18 Gomez cells negative) and the turnover numbers reproduce (`out_turnover_kinematics.txt`: cells 0.154–0.256; top ten 0.27–0.33). First attack: "The hierarchy moves … in one direction: the mean-log shape … flattened by 0.03 (comments)" (l. 98). That number is the mean over the 30 half-year-start cells (`balance_law_halfyear`, −0.029/yr; head −0.070/yr). Over the 12 annual primary cells (`balance_law/cells/law__reddit_comments_ext.json`) the comments shape steepened, +0.023/yr, with c > s̄ in 9 of 12 cells and every cut-off positive in the second pair. Comments' head flattens and its body steepens; "one direction" is true only of the arithmetic shares. Also the head share is 34.0% here and 0.356 in Results 4 and the SI (two universes, `mts_step1.matrices` against `restrict_universe`; gpt's round-3 point, still unreconciled), and the top-10 points in Fig. 1C/2B still have no bands.

**(2) Size gives little protection.** First attack: the estimator's calibration. The SI's own sentence (l. 205) concedes that a planted β = 0.2 is measured as 0.11; the model world shows the bias is world-dependent, since its planted 0.045 (`out_r3c_…submissions_long_flat20.txt`) is measured as 0.070 (`null_world__rdm.json`). On this footing the data's comments 0.075 (0.061–0.093) sit at two thirds of the firm-like-world-as-measured, not at a third of 0.2, and submissions' 0.045 (−0.013–0.143) does not exclude it. The paper has three lasting-β estimators that disagree on comments: 0.047 (SI l. 39, from §2z-dc's two-step difference), 0.126 (the model's structure function on the whole panel, which Fig. 4A–B is run on) and 0.056–0.092 (the gate windows). Facebook is the clean case (0.008; lasting 0.019; fold change 1.03) and should carry the headline; Reddit's claim should be stated as "0.05–0.13 against 0.15–0.2". The rest of the section is right: Eq. 1 as decomposition is now honest (48/48 cells reproduce), the "600-fold" range checks (e^(12.95−6.54) = 608), the C(R) reconciliation with Iñiguez is correct and convincing (`rank_change_data.json`), and Fig. 2C is the best new panel. Two things to say: the Gibrat world is flat beyond k = 25 (above), so "the data side with size-blind growth" is a top-25 statement; and the model world under-predicts the body's turnover (0.14 at K/2 against the data's 0.18, visible as the grey line in Fig. 2B), so "reproduces the data's turnover profile" (l. 154) is loose.

**(3) The balance and the null worlds.** The lead sentence is earned as accounting, and the disclosures are now right (composition term 25–35%; Fig. 3C's correlation carried by the slide; the near-identity stated; neutral copying 0.58 and jumps 1.56 excluded). First attack: "The informative quantity is therefore the level of the balance" (l. 144) followed by the SI's listing of c < s̄ as non-automatic. As above, three null worlds flatten too; the sign of c − s̄ says whether a ladder is above or below its own stationary steepness, which any world started off its steady state shows. What is informative is the size of the data's slide against its definitional part (0.0055 against the driftless world's 0.0044 at σ = 0.10) and the head of submissions (slide 1.6–3.5 × crossings, which reproduces from the cells). Second attack: V9 is still not in the main text. The model world's own by-rank ratios with known truth run 0.88–1.27 (`null_world__rdm.json` boundary_by_k: 1.27, 0.94, 0.88, 1.08, 1.09, 1.20, 1.18), so the by-rank test carries ±20% of estimator noise; submissions' 0.61–0.72 at 300–600 is outside that, but the comparison belongs beside it.

**(4) The generative model.** Much better: the equations describe the code, the gates' cohort of 200 and the noise-scale recalibration are in Methods, the frozen 0.097 is in the SI with its label, A6 is rerun with `movinghome_vp`, the flat start has 20 seeds and bands. First attack: world (iv) uses the hold closure (`null_worlds.py` l. 112, `variant="hold"`), so its ladder, hence S_k and through Eq. 1 its turnover profile and C(R), is an input; the main text says the closure "makes the ladder an input" but not that the model world uses it. Second: "errors within 0.03 of each other" (l. 157) against the SI's "fails narrowly on comments" (0.1305 − 0.0999 = 0.0305, l. 94). Third: the flat-start conclusion "flatter than the observed ladder at every rank … consistent with its flattening" cannot hold for the comments body, where the measured balance steepens (c > s̄ at k ≥ 781 in both annual pairs); the model's occupant slide and the data's cohort slide disagree there, and the paper should say which it believes. Fourth: A6's resolving power is small (retention 0.79–0.88 across cut-offs; a hindsight constant of 0.84 scores about 0.02), so "forecasts one-year retention … 0.028" is not a strong claim. The honest main-text sentence remains the structural one.

**(5) Persistence and the falling head.** The strongest section and the one discovery that survives all four worlds (−0.03 to −0.11 against +0.14 to +0.20). The repairs are real: prefix membership (D-1, ≤ 0.008), true-sign split with quintile means, platform-total factor on matched origins (`persistence_r3.py`). The post-2020 and pre-2021 checks are correctly labelled post hoc, their expectations were written first, and they pass; the SI is right to say 2–4 and 1–2 origins, but should add that those origins are 4-week shifts of one another, so each set is effectively one comparison. First attack: the abstract's last clause, "persistence, which cut the share of Reddit submissions' head from 50% to 34%". No analysis links the communities whose declines persist to the head's fall; Gomez's within term is decline, not shown to be persistent decline. Second: "the size expected from selecting the year's leaders on noise" (l. 177) has no computation behind it. PLAN R3-E promised a regression-to-the-mean baseline from a share-stable year; `gomez_r3.py` has none, and RESULTS R3-E reports none. It can be computed from the model world. Third: the permanent-rank cohorts' between term (0.086–0.133 at the submissions head, `out_gomez_r3.txt`) is larger than the current-year one and than the implied 0.071–0.089; the text reports only that the within term is unchanged.

## 4. Automatic against discovered

- **Wrong:** "crossings fall short of the slide" as a discovery (SI l. 205; Results intro by implication). Three of four worlds share it. The Discussion (l. 181) says "Two are not [automatic]", the introduction says "Three findings follow", and the SI lists three non-automatic items; the Discussion's count is the right one.
- **Fragile:** "size-blindness makes the top set turn over fastest". One seed per world; top-10 quantised at n/30; and the Zipf-start world cannot produce the data's decline from k = 78 to K/2. A size-blind world with the data's passing parts and the data's ladder shape would, I expect, reproduce the whole data profile, which would make this finding Eq. 1 plus β ≈ 0, that is, a corollary rather than a separate discovery. Run it.
- **Discovered only after calibration:** β. The planted-β ladder is the missing step; the data's "0.01–0.08" and the firms' "0.15–0.2" are not on one footing until it is done.
- **Discovered, robust:** persistence. The model world's −0.10 (A's reverting deviations behave as a medium-memory swing) widens the null band, and the data sit 0.17–0.25 above it in every configuration, including 4-week windows.
- **Called automatic but informative:** the boundary ratio near 1. It excludes copying (0.58), rank jumps (1.56) and the volatility-sorting rival (1.38 in the protocol's power study), which is more than "near-identity" suggests; the paper undersells it. Also Facebook's C(1) = 0.52 against Reddit's 0.03–0.07 is a real difference between platforms that the text passes over.

Adequate in structure and more careful than most CrowdTangle papers. Three problems. (a) "will be made available to editors and reviewers on request, for verification only": `DATA_AVAILABILITY_NOTES.md` l. 49–52 says this permission was not found on CrowdTangle's own pages and the FAQ forbids sharing exports outside the research team; the repository's documented terms do not support the sentence. PNAS accepts restricted data when the restriction is stated; say that the exports cannot be shared under the terms, and offer verification by re-running the deposited code on the authors' machine or through Meta Content Library. Naming subreddits is within the owner's decision and, as the notes argue, within Reddit's terms; publishing Page IDs is within the FAQ as documented.
