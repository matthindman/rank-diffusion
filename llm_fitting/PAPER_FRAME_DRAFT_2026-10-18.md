## The paper in one paragraph

On every platform we measure, the names at the top of attention change
quickly and the ladder they stand on changes slowly. About a fifth of every
top set is replaced each year, at almost the same rate for the top 195 as for
the top 12,500 (§2z-cu). Meanwhile the head's share of activity drifts by a
few points a year. One measured balance links the two speeds. A top set's
share changes by its members' drift against the field plus a gain from
crossings at its boundary. Wherever lasting movement can be measured, the
crossing gain equals the lasting variance of names at that rank over twice
the ladder's local slope:
- across ranks on four ladders, for three platforms;
- not for models in which names trade ranks.

The shape of attention inequality is therefore set by how much names move in
level space, which is nearly independent of size, and by how drift varies
with rank. Drift by rank is what moved when Reddit changed its feed, and
concentration has been falling since toward the much lower level that
current movement sustains. That relaxation takes years.

## Significance (draft; PNAS limit 120 words; 112 words)

Online attention is concentrated in a few communities, yet those communities
change places constantly. We measure how the two fit together on Reddit,
Facebook and Instagram. At every rank, communities crossing into a top set
return what its members lose. The size of that return follows from how much
communities at that rank move and how crowded the ranks are. This identifies
movement as growth in levels, not exchange of ranks. Concentration therefore
changes only through drift that differs by rank. On Reddit that drift changed
around a dated feed change. The leading communities' share of activity has
since fallen by a third, toward a far lower level that current movement
sustains, over years.

## Abstract (draft; PNAS limit 250 words)

Online attention is highly concentrated, yet the communities holding it
change places constantly. On Reddit, 2019–2022, a fifth of every top set is
replaced each year, at nearly the same rate from the top 195 to the top
12,500, while the head's share of activity changes by a few points a year.
We show how mobility and structure fit together.

For any top set, the change in its share is exactly its members' movement
against the field plus a gain from crossings at its boundary. Rank-based
diffusion theory predicts that gain from the lasting volatility of
communities at that rank and the local slope of the rank-size curve.
Measured on cohorts fixed in advance, crossings match the prediction:
- across ranks on Reddit comments;
- below the very top on Facebook pages and censoring-corrected Instagram;
- in the body of Reddit submissions.

The pooled ratio is 0.96 (0.89–1.05). Rank-exchange models fail the test, so
movement is growth in levels, and its lasting volatility barely depends on
size.

Shape therefore changes only through drift that varies by rank. Around
Reddit's 2021 feed change, unlike in placebo years, attention moved from the
leading submissions communities to the next tier and to their comments. The
leaders' share of activity fell from 50% to 34% in three years, toward a far
lower level that current movement sustains, with a time constant of years.

The balance explains structure but forecasts neither the curve better than
its trend nor retention better than rank.

## The argument, section by section, and what carries each claim

1. **Fast names, slow ladders.**
   - Turnover nearly invariant to scale (§2z-cu).
   - Arithmetic concentration falling (§2z-da).
   - *Figure 1:* `fig_puzzle.png` with arithmetic shares.
   - Position against Batty (2006) and Duranton (2007). The fact itself is
     documented; what is new is measuring both speeds on the same names and
     linking them.
2. **The exact balance.** For a top set, shape change = members' drift +
   reordering + entry.
   - The precedent is Gomez (2023) for top wealth shares. Cite it as the
     closest analogue.
   - *Figure 2:* the decomposition, now on comments and submissions; Facebook
     and Instagram are still to do.
3. **What the crossings say about movement.**
   - The boundary formula, stated with its cumulant expansion and its four
     exit routes (theory note §8).
   - On the data:
     - the ratio by rank;
     - lasting variance by rank from the variance route, the variogram and the
       crossings (`fig_variance_by_rank.png`, §2z-da);
     - the estimand's composition term, stated.
   - What it excludes: rank exchange. What it does not: the level models.
   - Lasting volatility nearly independent of size: β ≈ 0–0.09, against
     about 0.2 for firms.
4. **Drift by rank sets the shape.**
   - The same-year rate law goes to the SI (it is close to accounting,
     §2z-cg).
   - The main text has the sustained ladder and the relaxation (§2z-cp),
     framed as the Gabaix–Lasry–Lions–Moll question of transition speed.
5. **A dated change.**
   - June 2021: the daily onset (§2z-ca).
   - The event study against placebo years and the comments ladder (§2z-da).
     The distinctive signature is a shift of attention one tier down and from
     submissions to comments in the same communities. The head's level step
     does not stand out from its continuing slide.
   - What the capture regime of 1 July 2021 could and could not do. The
     comments head rose, which argues against a common deletion artefact.
   - *Figure:* event-study Δ by year and tier, two ladders.
6. **Who stays.**
   - Retention by rank. Movement adds little to rank in forecasting it.
   - The under-forecast under Gaussian movement is the finding: rare large
     jumps carry most displacement, which is two-speed movement (Guvenen et
     al. 2021 is the precedent in earnings).
   - Supplementary, not a headline.
7. **Limits.**
   - in sample;
   - no forecast advantage;
   - the submissions head unmeasurable, and the 300–600 shortfall
     unexplained (drift by rank does not explain it, §2z-da);
   - capture-delay deletion not audited;
   - Instagram scope: a-captioning accounts, mostly English and Romance
     languages.
