# Prompt 3 — finite-sampling bias in rank crossings and relative volatility

Study a precise statistical bridge between named trajectories, reordered observations, and collision local time in a rank-based diffusion. The goal is to determine which continuous-time balance quantities can actually be recovered from discrete noisy observations. Do not assume a weekly rank crossing count equals collision local time or instantaneous variance.

Begin with two named continuous diffusions X_1,X_2 and their gap Z=X_1-X_2. If their martingale covariance matrix is c, the gap variance rate is a_rel=c_11+c_22-2c_12. Define explicitly the normalization of semimartingale local time L_t^0(Z) used in Tanaka's formula and the occupation-density formula. Rank-ordering applies max/min to these trajectories; distinguish increments of a fixed name, increments of an ordered position, and changes in the ordering permutation.

Observations occur at n Delta: Y_i,n=X_i(n Delta)+e_i,n. Start with a fully observed pair, constant local coefficients, and independent known Gaussian observation noise. First set the noise to zero to check the discrete Tanaka residual and any leading bias term; then derive what changes with noise. State whether your target is expected local time over an interval, a long-run collision rate, or instantaneous relative variance. These are different estimands.

Choose and define one concrete estimator from the discrete observations. Derive its finite-Delta expectation or leading bias with a justified remainder, or prove why the chosen information cannot identify the target. Include drift, starting-gap dependence, and the possibility of multiple unseen crossings. A useful theorem should say when a claimed correction has a known sign, magnitude, or rate and when it does not. If studying reordered-increment volatility bias, define the denominator and name-matching rule before deriving the correction.

Distinguish fixed Delta with a growing span or many pairs from Delta→0, and distinguish fixed positive measurement noise from noise that shrinks with Delta. Do not import noiseless high-frequency consistency into a weekly noisy panel. If a standard microstructure-noise correction already solves the restricted case, state that clearly and focus any extension on the genuine missing assumption.

Only after a proved pairwise result, determine what can be extended to adjacent ranks in an N-particle system. Selection of the adjacent pair, changing identities, dependence, triple collisions, and local-time normalization must be addressed.
