## Where you are right and I was not

- **"The data contradicts a home" is too broad.** What is challenged is
  the fitted reversion strength and form at the measured horizons. Weak
  reversion with a relaxation time beyond the panel, and identity
  attributes that change, remain open. I accept your wording.
- **"A candidate has to keep a pull on the ladder" overreached.** A8 is an
  intervention in one fitted simulator, with the lasting volatility left
  where a model with reversion put it and the existing rebirth rule kept.
  It shows that this model without its home does not hold its ladder. It
  does not show that every model without a home over-concentrates, and it
  does not test independently estimated turnover as the stabiliser. I
  withdraw the sentence in the decision document.
- **The closure ratio is not yet a detector of anything.** The formula is
  for a constant-coefficient stable version; weekly crossings are not
  local time; noise, jumps and correlated shocks can break it. I demote it
  from a measurement to a candidate diagnostic whose power has to be shown
  on planted data first.
- **Size and rank cannot be told apart in one cross-section.** My "growth
  law by size" should not have been written as if it could choose between
  them.
- **The complexity count.** Removing about 55 reversion values does not
  make the model simple while the ladder, the other profiles, the
  amplitudes and the boundary rules stay. I counted only what moved.
- **Two corrections to the evidence sheet.** Entry is partly measured
  (§2b); "nothing beyond 78 weeks" applies to the variance comparison, not
  to the direction statistics. Accepted.
- **Three levels of claim** (conditional dynamics; distributional
  explanation; social mechanism or causal effect) is a better frame than my
  "assumed, derived, tested". I adopt it.

## What V2 adds, which neither first position had

- Whole-year slopes separate reversion to a home, and slow memory, from a
  random walk and from current-rank attraction, and are unmoved by a
  planted annual cycle. The data sit on the random-walk side. So the
  challenge to the fitted home is stronger than when you wrote, within the
  horizons and the frozen nuisance parameters V2 covers.
- **No endpoint-level statistic in the battery separates a random walk
  from current-rank attraction.** This bears on your mechanical concern
  (long residence near rank targets): at pulls of 0.02 and 0.10 per week
  on the 212-week profile it did not show; slopes were +0.19 and −0.01,
  like a walk. It may still show at the head, where gaps are large. It
  also means your fourth row (does a deviation from a train-only target
  predict a restoring response?) is the only proposed test that could
  detect candidate 2 as a conditional model, and that test has no planted
  check yet.
- My reading that the fitted reversion "is the cycle" was wrong: planted
  cycles lower the fitted reversion rate. Your caution about the cycle
  subtraction was justified. I have corrected the record (§2z-bd).
- The estimator's own reversion rates are not recovered (median relative
  error 0.9–1.7). Nothing in this debate should rest on a fitted κ.

## Where I still differ, or want it sharper

1. **The owner needs an order, not six open rows.** Your table is right in
   content. I propose it be run as a first batch of three, cheap, on
   existing development data, each statistic validated on planted data
   before it is read on real data:
   - *Restoring response, three ways on one footing:* does the lasting
     level move back toward (a) the endpoint's own level in a disjoint
     earlier window, (b) the train-only ladder at its current latent rank,
     or (c) neither. This is your rows one and four merged. It decides
     which conditional description is licensed, and it is the direct test
     candidate 2 never had.
   - *Turnover, at nested cutoffs:* your row three.
   - *State-dependent growth:* your row two, with size and rank treated as
     one family until exogenous variation can separate them.
   Then, only if those give stable primitives, the held-out ladder
   prediction (your row five). The social-process designs (your row six)
   are named as the route to the third level and not scheduled.
2. **The convergence experiment is worth keeping for a model whose target
   is not supplied.** You are right that for candidate 2 it would show
   attraction to a supplied target and nothing else. For a model with
   frozen, independently estimated primitives it is a real test, provided
   the convergence time is reported against the length of the panel, as
   you say.
3. **What to tell the owner about the paper.** I agree no one can promise
   a journal. I would still say plainly: with the data in hand the
   reachable claims are the first level now and the second level if the
   first batch and the held-out prediction work; the third level needs
   designs the panels may or may not contain. Candidate 2 would have
   stayed at the first level while looking like the second, which is the
   worst of the three places to be.

## Proposed consensus text

> 1. Under candidate 2 as written the rank-size structure is supplied, not
>    derived. The owner's reading is correct for that proposal. It is not
>    true of rank-dependent models in general: a law in which growth
>    depends on state, with no ladder in it, can generate one.
> 2. The model as it stands also takes the ladder as given. Neither it nor
>    candidate 2 explains why the hierarchy has its shape.
> 3. Candidate 2 is withdrawn as a replacement for the lasting layer. No
>    gate evaluation is spent on it. The present model stays as the
>    conditional benchmark, with its fitted reversion marked as challenged
>    at horizons up to about a year and a half and its fitted reversion
>    rates marked as unrecovered.
> 4. Three levels of claim are kept apart: conditional dynamics;
>    distributional explanation (primitives estimated without the ladder,
>    the ladder predicted and held out); social mechanism (a design that
>    separates explanations). The paper's present language is at the first
>    level and stays there until a second-level result exists.
> 5. Next, on development data only and with each new statistic validated
>    on planted data first: the restoring-response comparison, turnover at
>    nested cutoffs, and state-dependent growth; then, if the primitives
>    are stable, a held-out ladder prediction. A design and predictions are
>    written and reviewed before any of it runs. None of it is a candidate
>    and none spends a gate evaluation.
> 6. What would reverse this: a restoring response toward an earlier
>    identity anchor that survives noise and seasonal controls (the home
>    stays, weaker); a stable restoring response toward the train-only
>    ladder with temporal predictions that beat the alternatives
>    (candidate 2 returns as a conditional model, honestly counted);
>    primitives that predict the held-out ladder and mobility together (a
>    second-level model).
> 7. Flags for the owner, changing nothing: the outline's sentence that

>    dynamics); "durable concentration without durable incumbents" has to
