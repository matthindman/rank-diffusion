## 1. Does candidate 2 generate the ladder?

The owner's reading is correct **for the particular proposal**, not for current-rank models generally. Let L(r) be the externally supplied, measured log-size ladder. A natural interpretation of candidate 2 is

    dh_i = λ [L(r_i(t)) − h_i(t)] dt + σ_i dW_i.

This is a schematic interpretation of the prose, not an implemented or adopted specification. The model must still say whether r is the rank of latent h or noisy observed X, whether L is centered, and which coordinate is pulled. Those choices have different implications.

L(r) supplies a desired cross-sectional shape. Swapping the identities occupying its rungs does not explain that shape. Even if a simulation converges from a flat initial condition, that would show attraction toward the supplied target, not independent explanation of the target. Conversely, because diffusion, crossings, boundaries and observation effects matter, the stationary observed ladder need not equal L exactly: this is not a literal tautology or a guarantee of passing the card. Untargeted movement and finite-horizon maintenance predictions can still fail and be scientifically useful.

There is an additional mechanical concern. Within a region with no changes of rank, each h_i follows an OU pull toward a fixed L(r_i). An endpoint switches targets only when ranks cross. Thus removing a personal home does not imply random-walk-like increments over the observed horizons; it can replace identity pinning with long residence near rank targets. Crossing times and gap-dependent residence are predictions needing tests, not consequences of the phrase 'endpoints can change places for good.' If rank is observed X, transient/noise shocks can switch the target of the lasting process; the proposal needs a substantive reason for that feedback. Statistical observation error cannot automatically become a force acting on the entity.

**Rank dependence itself does not prevent emergence.** A different law, dh_i = g(r_i)dt + σdW_i, need not contain L. For a finite, equal-volatility Brownian system, stationary gaps can follow from the drift vector under explicit stability conditions [L1, read in this session]. That is a mathematical existence example, not evidence for attention dynamics. Estimating g by inverting the empirical gap curve would put the ladder back in through calibration. Estimating movement primitives independently and predicting an unused ladder is the informative direction.

## 2. Does the existing model do something more causal?

Not concerning ladder genesis. MODEL_STATUS §1 explicitly conditions on the measured cross-section of homes. A personal-home process, dh_i = κ_i(μ_i − h_i)dt + σ_i dW_i, assigns each identity an anchor μ_i from that cross-section. It adds identity persistence to the conditioning assumption; it does not derive the anchor distribution from audience behavior. Its existing, appropriately limited claim is conditional reproduction and maintenance. That remains a legitimate research object, but cannot establish why concentration arose.

The evidence does not warrant defending the current fitted lasting layer unchanged. §2z-at reports poor reproducibility of κ profiles and substantial grid-boundary estimates. §2z-aw reports annual-block direction statistics and season-adjusted variance growth inconsistent with the fitted process, plus insufficient relocation. However, 'the data contradicts a home' is too broad: these observations challenge the fitted reversion strength and specification on the measured horizons. Weak reversion with a relaxation time exceeding observation, changing identity attributes, seasonality, correlated shocks and changing volatility remain alternatives. The cycle subtraction is post hoc, assumes a sinusoid, and was not a symmetric refit (§2z-aw addendum). Recovery of distinguishing statistics remains outstanding. Simulation-seed SD is not uncertainty about the empirical population or identification.

§2z-ay is a useful **intervention in a fitted simulator**, not an intervention on a platform or a refitted comparison. Keeping lasting volatility estimated jointly with reversion while zeroing reversion changes their balance. It cannot show that all non-home models overconcentrate, or that the real system needs a pull toward its empirical ladder. It retains the existing bottom rebirth machinery, so it also does not refute independently estimated entry/exit as a stabilizer. Its failures matter as much as its successes: repeat exits/excursions and the excess of sudden lasting exits on comments do not improve. A lasting-layer replacement alone is not a complete departure explanation (§2z-au, §2z-ay).

I therefore dispute the decision document's inference that a candidate **has to** preserve a pull on the ladder while eliminating the endpoint pull. The defensible inference is narrower: the current combination of restoring dynamics, innovations and boundaries misses important temporal behavior; replacing that combination requires both mobility and structural checks. Stationarity may arise from state-dependent growth, competition, entry/exit or weak identity forces. A stable-looking finite panel is not proof of exact stationarity.

## 3. What should the lasting layer be for the owner's scientific objective?

No unique layer is identified yet. I would seek a model whose independently measured growth, interaction and turnover rules jointly constrain the distribution and movement of attention. I would allow slow identity heterogeneity if supported; 'durable concentration without durable incumbents' must be an empirical conclusion, not an exchangeability assumption. Retaining permanent-rank-dependent volatility and persistent amplitudes also means candidate 2 is not automatically an exchangeable model, even after deleting personal-home drift.

Three levels of claim must stay distinct:

1. **Conditional dynamics:** given a ladder and initial states, describe mobility. Both home and ladder-target models can address this honestly.
2. **Distributional explanation:** estimate a restricted dynamic law without fitting it to the ladder being evaluated; derive and test its implied gaps, concentration and adjustment paths. This is stronger, but remains dependent on assumptions.
3. **Social mechanism or causal effect:** show which audience, supply or platform process changes growth and concentration, with a design that distinguishes competing explanations. A generative fit or successful planted-model recovery is necessary validation of an inference procedure, not proof that the real mechanism has been causally identified.

This hierarchy is my methodological judgment, not a claim about a formal PNAS acceptance rule. A strong paper can make a limited contribution without solving every origin question. For the owner's stated ambition, a mechanistic result should earn its strength through restrictions and independent evidence, not through the journal's name. There is no basis here to promise publication.

The literature offers useful alternatives and cautions. Gabaix, Lasry, Lions and Moll explicitly use random growth plus stabilizing turnover/boundaries and distinguish stationary distributions from transition dynamics; they acknowledge multiple microfoundations for the same reduced form [L3, read in this session]. This makes entry/exit a serious competitor and makes 'random growth is silent on occupants' too sweeping. It does not establish the appropriate law for pages or subreddits.

The strongest social-science precedents intervene on an observable process. Salganik, Dodds and Watts randomize the availability of popularity information and replicate independent cultural markets; this identifies an influence contribution to inequality and unpredictability in that setting [L4, read in this session]. Muchnik, Aral and Taylor randomize initial comment votes and observe later responses [L5, read in this session]. These are examples of designs, not transportable effect estimates or proofs about subreddit-level lasting activity. They motivate asking whether measured rank is actually displayed to users, affects exposure, or is merely an analyst's ordering.
