## Point-by-point response to Claude's first position

1. **Supplied versus emergent ladder: agree with the answer, disagree with 'explains nothing.'** A target ladder supplies the shape but still imposes specific dynamic restrictions. The restoring law λ[L(r)−h] is not a consequence of stationarity and is not true of every stationary system. Stationarity balance identities and structural conditional drift functions are different objects. Candidate 2 could explain or predict aspects of conditional adjustment if independently supported; its failure to explain genesis is sufficient to reject the proposed causal framing. This distinction is worth preserving after the withdrawal.

2. **Personal home: agree that it supplies the ladder.** Your second-round narrowing of the empirical claim is essential. Poor recovery of κ and the observed temporal discrepancies challenge this fitted process; they cannot establish absence of all lasting identity heterogeneity or of weak long-run reversion. 'Fitness' is not synonymous with an OU personal home: durable attributes can affect growth, exposure or innovation without inducing return to a fixed level. The three mechanism classes are therefore useful working contrasts, not mutually exclusive or exhaustive causal categories.

3. **Movement primitives predicting structure: agree, with an independence condition.** The ladder cannot select the drift profile, smoothing, cutoff, boundary rule or preferred mechanism and then serve as independent validation. Permanent-rank labels and the universe carry structural information too. A dynamic model may legitimately condition on them, but the claimed holdout must say what information was allowed. A held-out *later* ladder establishes temporal transport conditional on that information; it is not by itself proof of genesis from an uninformative starting population.

4. **M1: worthwhile, not yet a decision rule.** Nonoverlapping blocks do not establish independent errors: the passing component is serially correlated, an estimated permanent level is noisy, and persistent state can move between blocks. Restricting to the top half reduces exposure to the boundary but conditions away the region needed to identify entry/exit and may miss effects localized there. Use permanent-rank cohorts, actual state uncertainty, known observation dynamics and declared censoring assumptions. Ask whether each statistic can distinguish specified rivals with nuisance uncertainty, not whether it supports 'growth law' in general.

5. **M2: agree.** A recorded-list entrant is not necessarily a new endpoint, and losing rank does not mean losing absolute activity. A source/sink argument requires relative growth and mass-flow accounting with consistent denominators. Nested cutoff sensitivity is useful; whole-platform claims remain unavailable where the instrument sees only a recorded list.

6. **M3: agree with your round-2 demotion.** Fernholz's stable-version gap approximation and collision local time are not an identified mechanism classifier. The equal-volatility finite system of Pal–Pitman does have a stationary product law, but arbitrary diffusion profiles, name effects and noisy weekly sampling do not inherit that theorem. Near-one closure would be a diagnostic outcome to explain, not a certificate of exchangeability. Sources [L1–L2, read in this session] in CODEX_1.

7. **Wrong-initial-ladder demonstration: retain only with a sharper control.** Keep parameters and all empirical targets fixed while changing initial states. A home model *can* return from perturbed initial h to its fixed supplied homes. If one instead resets homes to the perturbed ladder, one has changed a parameter/input, not just initial conditions. Thus convergence from a wrong initial state does not by itself distinguish supplied-home from derived-ladder models. For the latter it is useful only in conjunction with the explicit absence of a supplied target and independently measured primitives. Report transient predictions and convergence time, not only the limiting curve.

8. **Paper language: agree with the three claim levels, not a promise of identification.** No law is selected by the PNAS aspiration. The existing binding conditional claim can remain useful. Distributional explanation needs unused restrictions; named social causes require defensible identifying variation. Neither a platform era change nor multiple panels automatically supplies it: changes in measurement, audience composition, supply and exposure may coincide. There may be no adequate causal design in these panels. 'Durable concentration without durable incumbents' must be tested and qualified, including nonexchangeable identity attributes, not inherited from a modeling choice.

9. **What changes our minds: agree subject to scope.** Evidence for a home need not mean adopting a weaker home automatically; compare its transport and movement predictions. Likewise a target-restoring response supports a conditional model only after alternatives and observation-driven artifacts are addressed. Failure of one derived-ladder model does not show that these data can support none. Failure to discriminate is an acceptable endpoint for the proposed diagnostic batch.

## What the new evidence changes

The integrated V2 study provides information unavailable to either first position. It strengthens the case for confronting the fitted home with temporal statistics and for distrusting fitted κ as a mechanism label. It also supports your withdrawal of the claimed sign of the annual cycle's effect on fitted κ. I update accordingly; my first-position recommendation is unchanged.

My concern about residence near rank targets was a possibility, not a claim that it must appear in the whole-year slopes. EVIDENCE_2 and §2z-bd report walk-like slopes for the two tested attraction strengths in the middle group; that is evidence against using those slopes to establish such pinning in those cases. It leaves head residence, target-gap dependence and other strengths/horizons open. I do not insist that a conjectured effect exists just because I suggested it.

However, I cannot endorse the universal wording 'no endpoint-level statistic in the battery separates' or 'whatever distinguishes them is in the cross-section, not in how endpoints move' solely from the evidence cited. The primary integrated V2 entry §2z-bc establishes failure of the **declared 26-week middle-dispersion contrast** and failure of fitted middle κ to separate the strong alternatives on the tested profiles. The second sheet reports additional mean slopes but not a complete pairwise uncertainty audit. That does not prove equality of all temporal distributions, let alone a theorem about all endpoint statistics. Nor is my target-response row the 'only' conceivable distinguishing test: joint conditional increments, occupancy/residence and gap-conditioned crossings are other possibilities whose usefulness remains unmeasured.

The canonical result explicitly calls its separation labels descriptive, not formal tests of unique mechanism identification; nuisance parameters were fixed, native length and profile confounded, and the battery has no family-wide error guarantee (§2z-bc). The daily noise instrument and movement stacks were not recovered. We should say **'the tested contrasts do not identify random walk versus current-rank attraction'**, not identify a whole class of real-world processes by elimination. The empirical whole-year slope being beyond the tested random-walk values is another reason not to declare a quantitative match from its sign. No fresh numerical inference is possible here without its uncertainty.
