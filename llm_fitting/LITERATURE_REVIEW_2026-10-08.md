# Literature review for the rank-diffusion research agenda

2026-10-08 · Companion to [the project assessment and agenda](RESEARCH_AGENDA_2026-10-08.md).

## Scope, search, and evidentiary limits

This is a targeted methodological review of 31 primary research and methods papers. It combines the project's established inspirations with adjacent literatures that bear directly on its current residuals. It is not a systematic review, a claim that every paper was read cover to cover, or evidence that any proposed method will improve this model.

Searches covered eight clusters: (1) stationary size laws and rank interactions; (2) permanent–transitory panel identification and heterogeneous shocks; (3) state-space identifiability and recovery; (4) temporary absence, detection, and survival; (5) simulated estimation and misspecification; (6) probabilistic forecasting and temporal validation; (7) smoothing and model comparison; and (8) simulation-study design and reporting. Query examples included “Atlas models rank stationary distribution,” “earnings permanent transitory heterogeneity measurement error,” “state space models process observation noise identifiability,” “capture recapture temporary emigration,” “synthetic likelihood misspecified models,” and “proper scoring rules finite ensemble leave future out.” A separate search checked recent simulation-based inference work, including 2025 methods.

Sources were sought through author manuscripts, original journals, arXiv, institutional research records, and proceedings. Full texts or relevant full-text sections were inspected where accessible. Entries marked **abstract/record** were used only for claims supported there; some publisher pages failed to open even though their indexed abstracts or author versions were accessible. No secondary blog or generated summary is treated as technical authority.

The distinction in each annotation is between what the paper establishes and this review's proposed application. References already familiar to the program are retained as comparators, not presented as newly discovered insights.

## A. Structural rank dynamics and the stationary-law problem

**1. Gabaix (1999), “Zipf's Law for Cities: An Explanation,” Quarterly Journal of Economics.** [Author record](https://xgabaix.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/zipfs-law-cities-explanation), [paper](https://xgabaix.scholars.harvard.edu/sites/g/files/omnuum7761/files/xgabaix/files/zipfs_law.pdf). **Full text.** Proportional growth and a lower-bound mechanism can produce Zipf behavior under stated assumptions. Here it motivates a tractable structural comparator and explicit boundary accounting. It does not establish that the empirical homes or platform observation process satisfy those assumptions.

**2. Banner, Fernholz, and Karatzas (2005), “Atlas models of equity markets,” Annals of Applied Probability.** [Author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0602521). **Abstract/record.** Rank-dependent diffusions connect capitalization dynamics with stable ranked structures. Their relevance is the joint treatment of movement and size ordering. Equity-capitalization conservation, interaction rules, and observability should not be silently transferred to platform attention.

**3. Ichiba, Papathanakos, Banner, Karatzas, and Fernholz (2011), “Hybrid Atlas models,” Annals of Applied Probability.** [Paper and full text](https://arxiv.org/abs/0909.0065). **Full-text sections.** Combines rank and name dependence and studies invariant behavior under conditions. Particularly relevant to this model's identity homes plus rank-varying coefficients. Use its mathematical structure to formulate the appropriate generator and conditions; a permanent-rank parameter estimate is not automatically a current-rank diffusion coefficient.

**4. Blumm et al. (2012), “Dynamics of Ranking Processes in Complex Systems,” Physical Review Letters 109, 128701.** [Paper](https://physics.aps.org/featured-article-pdf/10.1103/PhysRevLett.109.128701). **Full text.** Studies ranking dynamics through score evolution and heterogeneous noise. Useful for distinguishing score stability from rank stability and for comparing mechanisms across systems. Its empirical examples do not validate this project's latent decomposition or censored samples.

**5. Iñiguez, Pineda, Gershenson, and Barabási (2022), “Dynamics of ranking,” Nature Communications 13, 1646.** [Published article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41467-022-29256-x), [manuscript and supplementary material](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.13439). **Indexed main-article material and supplementary full text.** A compact displacement/replacement model explains multiple ranking observables across many systems. It deserves an explicit low-complexity benchmark. It generates rank behavior rather than the full activity ladder; universe quantities learned from an entire empirical record must be reconstructed using training information for forecasting.

**6. Gabaix, Lasry, Lions, and Moll (2016), “The Dynamics of Inequality,” Econometrica.** [Published-work record](https://xgabaix.scholars.harvard.edu/publications/dynamics-inequality), [working paper](https://www.nber.org/papers/w21363). **Abstract/record and paper search excerpts.** Shows why reproducing a stationary distribution need not reproduce the speed of distributional change. The direct lesson is to constrain stationary shape and dynamic timescales jointly. Wealth-specific mechanisms are hypotheses, not ready-made attention mechanisms.

**7. Clauset, Shalizi, and Newman (2009), “Power-Law Distributions in Empirical Data,” SIAM Review.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/0706.1062). **Abstract/record.** Establishes statistical methods for assessing power-law claims and alternatives. Relevant to a structural theory branch that might otherwise assume a Zipf target from a log-log plot. The project's temporal and cross-entity dependencies need additional treatment; an independent-sample recipe is not sufficient by itself.

## B. Panel identification, heterogeneous amplitudes, and nonlinear persistence

**8. Abowd and Card (1989), “On the Covariance Structure of Earnings and Hours Changes,” Econometrica.** [Author paper](https://davidcard.berkeley.edu/papers/covar-earn-hour.pdf), [1986 working-paper record](https://www.nber.org/papers/w1832). **Abstract/record and paper excerpts.** Uses longitudinal covariance structure to learn about earnings and hours dynamics. This is a close methodological ancestor for separating persistent and transitory variation through panel moments. It supports examining exactly which combinations the available horizons identify, not importing an earnings specification unchanged.

**9. Meghir and Pistaferri (2004), “Income Variance Dynamics and Heterogeneity,” Econometrica 72, 1–32.** [Author manuscript](https://web.stanford.edu/~pista/meghir.pdf). **Full-text sections.** Treats permanent/transitory income variation, heterogeneous variances, and measurement error. Especially relevant to persistent entity amplitudes and the distinction between heterogeneity in innovation variance and heterogeneity in persistence. Its identifying assumptions must be restated for aggregated platform activity.

**10. Arellano, Blundell, and Bonhomme (2017), “Earnings and Consumption Dynamics: A Nonlinear Panel Data Framework,” Econometrica.** [Article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.3982/ECTA13795), [author manuscript](https://www.homepages.ucl.ac.uk/~uctp39a/Arellano_Blundell_Bonhomme_January_2017.pdf). **Abstract and manuscript excerpts.** Motivates measuring nonlinear persistence and dependence on shock size or sign. Here the application is a selected-event diagnostic processed identically in simulations and data. The paper does not prove that a nonlinear attention model is needed, nor that short panels identify its flexibility.

**11. Guvenen, Karahan, Ozkan, and Song (2021), “What Do Data on Millions of U.S. Workers Reveal About Lifecycle Earnings Dynamics?” Econometrica.** [Article](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.3982/ECTA14603). **Article abstract and sections accessible through the publisher page.** Documents rich non-Gaussian and heterogeneous earnings dynamics. Useful inspiration for examining tails, asymmetry, and persistence together rather than using one kurtosis estimate as the entire shock description. Large administrative earnings data offer identification power that these shorter platform panels may not share.

**12. Sinatra et al. (2016), “Quantifying the evolution of individual scientific impact,” Science.** [Article](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaf5239). **Structured abstract.** The persistent Q-factor construction offers an analogy for stable individual heterogeneity. In this project, temperament controls variance; it is not automatically ability, quality, causal fitness, or the same object as Q. Its value is a possible factorization that reduces the number of separate heterogeneous components.

## C. Inverse problems, observation processes, and simulator-based estimation

**13. Auger-Méthé et al. (2016), “State-space models’ dirty little secrets: even simple linear Gaussian models can have estimation problems,” Scientific Reports 6, 26677.** [Published article](https://www.nature.com/articles/srep26677), [manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1508.04325). **Full-text sections.** Demonstrates parameter/state estimation failures even in simple state-space models, especially when measurement variation dominates. Directly supports synthetic recovery and profile diagnostics before interpreting permanent/transitory components. Fixing one variance externally can help without resolving every ambiguity.

**14. Raue et al. (2009), “Structural and practical identifiability analysis of partially observed dynamical models by exploiting the profile likelihood,” Bioinformatics.** [Article](https://doi.org/10.1093/bioinformatics/btp358). **Full-text sections.** Separates structural and practical identification using profiles. For rank-diffusion, profile the actual estimation criterion and forecast outputs to locate ridges. A minimum-distance profile is not a likelihood profile, and likelihood confidence thresholds cannot be copied without justification or calibration.

**15. Kendall, Nichols, and Hines (1997), “Estimating Temporary Emigration Using Capture–Recapture Data with Pollock’s Robust Design,” Ecology 78, 563–578.** [Paper](https://webhome.auburn.edu/~grandjb/wildpop/readings/Robust%20Design/Kendall_et_al_1997.pdf). **Full text.** Distinguishes detection, temporary absence, and survival using structured repeated observation. This informs the exit/return taxonomy. The required sampling structure is not guaranteed by daily platform records, so permanent disappearance may remain only partially identified.

**16. Bretó, He, Ionides, and King (2009), “Time series analysis via mechanistic models,” Annals of Applied Statistics 3, 319–348.** [Author paper](https://ionides.github.io/pubs/breto09.pdf). **Paper excerpts.** Develops simulation-based mechanistic time-series inference with distinct process and observation components. The useful transfer is an explicit observation operator and independently checkable process simulation. It does not make full-population particle inference computationally easy for coupled ranks.

**17. Wood (2010), “Statistical inference for noisy nonlinear ecological dynamic systems,” Nature.** [Author-indexed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20703226/). **Abstract only.** Synthetic likelihood uses simulated distributions of summary statistics for difficult stochastic models. It is a possible route if selection-dependent summaries lack tractable formulas. Summary sufficiency, covariance estimation, approximation accuracy, and misspecification all need study; the existing omnibus distance is not thereby a likelihood.

**18. Frazier and Drovandi (2021), “Robust Approximate Bayesian Inference With Synthetic Likelihood,” Journal of Computational and Graphical Statistics 30, 958–976.** [Article](https://doi.org/10.1080/10618600.2021.1875839), [author manuscript](https://arxiv.org/abs/1904.04551). **Abstract/record and article excerpts.** Shows unreliable inference under incompatible simulated and observed summaries and proposes robust extensions. This is a reason to test simulator adequacy before upgrading inference machinery, and to expose which moments cannot be jointly matched rather than burying them in an aggregate objective.

**19. Wehenkel et al. (2025), “Addressing Misspecification in Simulation-based Inference through Data-driven Calibration,” ICML, PMLR 267.** [Proceedings paper](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/wehenkel25a.html). **Proceedings abstract.** RoPE uses a real-world calibration set with ground-truth parameter measurements to address simulator misspecification. That requirement limits immediate applicability here: latent homes, temperament, and reversion are not directly labeled. This paper is a current-method check, not a recommendation to replace transparent moment estimation with a neural posterior estimator.

**20. Gouriéroux, Monfort, and Renault (1993), “Indirect Inference,” Journal of Applied Econometrics 8, S85–S118.** [Article](https://doi.org/10.1002/jae.3950080507), [paper](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~cshalizi/462/readings/Indirect%20Inference%20%28Gourieroux%2C%20Monfort%20and%20Renault%29.pdf). **Abstract and opening paper sections.** Matches auxiliary estimates obtained from data and simulations when direct inference is difficult. This is a principled candidate for event-selected return summaries if the auxiliary procedure and observation selection are reproduced exactly. Identification, simulation error, and model adequacy remain separate requirements.

## D. Forecast evaluation and protection against repeated selection

**21. Gneiting and Raftery (2007), “Strictly Proper Scoring Rules, Prediction, and Estimation,” Journal of the American Statistical Association.** [Paper](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/people/raftery/Research/PDF/Gneiting2007jasa.pdf). **Full-text sections.** Supplies the basis for evaluating probabilistic forecasts with scores that reward honest predictive distributions. Here CRPS/ranked probability scores and event scores should be attached to origin-defined endpoints and outcomes. A proper score applied to a pooled marginal forecast does not establish conditional individual forecasting skill.

**22. Ferro (2014), “Fair scores for ensemble forecasts,” Quarterly Journal of the Royal Meteorological Society.** [Author manuscript](https://empslocal.ex.ac.uk/people/staff/ferro/Publications/ferro2013.pdf). **Full text.** Addresses biases introduced when finite ensembles represent predictive distributions. Relevant when comparing simulators with different ensemble sizes. Corrections require the corresponding sampling assumptions; dependent pooled trajectories or empirical observations are not automatically exchangeable independent forecast draws.

**23. Bürkner, Gabry, and Vehtari (2020), “Approximate leave-future-out cross-validation for Bayesian time series models,” Journal of Statistical Computation and Simulation.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1902.06281). **Full-text sections.** Formalizes predictive evaluation using only information preceding the target. The applicable principle is past-only fitting and tuning at every origin, including universe construction and state estimates. Its particular Bayesian approximation is optional; ordinary leave-one-out is not an interchangeable substitute for this forecasting task.

**24. Scheuerer and Hamill (2015), “Variogram-Based Proper Scoring Rules for Probabilistic Forecasts of Multivariate Quantities,” Monthly Weather Review.** [Article](https://journals.ametsoc.org/abstract/journals/mwre/143/4/mwr-d-14-00269.1.xml). **Indexed abstract.** Provides dependence-sensitive multivariate forecast scores. Useful as a supplementary check that individually plausible endpoint forecasts produce sensible joint rank competition. Properness alone does not mean a particular score identifies every aspect of the joint distribution.

**25. Cawley and Talbot (2010), “On Over-fitting in Model Selection and Subsequent Selection Bias in Performance Evaluation,” Journal of Machine Learning Research 11, 2079–2107.** [Paper](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/volume11/cawley10a/cawley10a.pdf). **Full-text sections.** Explains how model-selection noise can contaminate reported generalization. Here repeated architecture selection on the same rolling origins spends validation information even if each fit uses train-only data. Keep inner selection within development history and reserve new evidence for a final selected candidate.

## E. Recovery design, computational checking, and measured simplicity

**26. Morris, White, and Crowther (2019), “Using simulation studies to evaluate statistical methods,” Statistics in Medicine.** [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/1712.03198), [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1002/sim.8086). **Full-text sections.** ADEMP organizes a simulation study around its actual inferential question and requires attention to Monte Carlo uncertainty. Apply it to recovery of parameters, states, and forecasts over a designed set of relevant difficulties. A convenient number of repetitions is not a substitute for precision calculations.

**27. Gelman et al. (2020), “Bayesian Workflow.”** [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.01808), [author PDF](https://sites.stat.columbia.edu/gelman/research/unpublished/Bayesian_Workflow_article.pdf). **Full-text sections; methodological synthesis/preprint.** Integrates computational checks, predictive checks, and iterative model criticism. These principles can inform the present moment-based workflow without adopting Bayesian inference wholesale. Iteration still needs a protected final evaluation; posterior predictive fit is not an out-of-sample acceptance criterion.

**28. Talts, Betancourt, Simpson, Vehtari, and Gelman (2018), “Validating Bayesian Inference Algorithms with Simulation-Based Calibration.”** [Preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/1804.06788). **Abstract.** SBC checks inference algorithms against repeated draws from an assumed generative model. Appropriate if a posterior-based estimator is introduced. Current minimum-distance recovery experiments should be described as recovery/coverage studies, not relabeled SBC; neither procedure proves the simulator describes real platforms.

**29. Eilers and Marx (1996), “Flexible Smoothing with B-splines and Penalties,” Statistical Science.** [Paper](https://sites.stat.washington.edu/courses/stat527/s13/readings/EilersMarx_StatSci_1996.pdf). **Full-text sections.** Provides a practical way to regularize flexible curves. This motivates a compact rank-profile compression candidate with effective complexity reported. Penalty selection must stay within training data, and smoothing must not overwrite the frozen current profiles before evaluation.

**30. Self and Liang (1987), “Asymptotic Properties of Maximum Likelihood Estimators and Likelihood Ratio Tests Under Nonstandard Conditions,” Journal of the American Statistical Association.** [Paper](https://www.stat.cmu.edu/~brian/763-2015/week06/papers/self-liang-1987.pdf). **Full-text sections.** Boundary parameters create nonstandard inference. This matters for zero-variance and no-heterogeneity ablations. It neither supplies an automatic reference distribution for the project's minimum-distance score nor justifies using an uncalibrated score difference as a likelihood-ratio test.

**31. Williams et al. (2024), “Transparent reporting items for simulation studies evaluating statistical methods: Foundations for reproducibility and reliability,” Methods in Ecology and Evolution.** [Article](https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/2041-210X.14415). **Abstract/record.** Recent reporting guidance complements ADEMP. Use it to document generating mechanisms, estimands, failures, computation, and uncertainty in the recovery study. Better reporting improves auditability; it does not compensate for a poorly identified empirical model.

## Conclusions for this project, rather than generic prescriptions

The largest opportunity is to combine the project's existing moment-based discipline with explicit recovery of its latent decomposition and genuinely conditional forecasts. The adjacent earnings literature is especially useful because it addresses persistent versus temporary variation, measurement error, and heterogeneous shocks in longitudinal panels. Capture–recapture clarifies what absence cannot establish. Rank-based diffusion theory clarifies what a stationary ladder can and cannot identify.

The literature does not support immediately adding a Hawkes process, a large regime mixture, neural inference, or unrestricted hierarchical heterogeneity. It supports a discriminating sequence: verify inference on known truth; identify the missing transition with the correct observation model; compare refitted simpler models; and then test one residual-driven mechanism against genuinely reserved evidence.
