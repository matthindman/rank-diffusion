\significancestatement{Large firms are steadier than small ones, and that steadiness makes market leaders hard to dislodge. The leaders of online attention have almost no such protection. On Reddit and Facebook, the year-to-year fluctuations of a community's activity barely shrink with its size, so about a fifth of every Reddit top set, at every scale, is replaced each year. A stochastic model in which each community's typical level wanders, and the ranking is held in shape by drift that depends on rank rather than by communities returning to fixed levels, forecasts held-out movement as well as or better than the previous model. What it cannot yet explain is Reddit's momentum: growth that persists from one year to the next.}
\keywords{attention inequality $|$ rank dynamics $|$ displacement $|$ mobility $|$ platform governance}

\begin{abstract}
Online attention is concentrated, yet its leaders change. We ask whether size protects them, as it protects large firms, whose growth fluctuates far less than small firms'. In weekly records of Reddit communities (2019–2022) and Facebook pages, the spread of a community's one-year change in activity scales as size to the power $-0.01$ to $-0.08$, against $-0.15$ to $-0.2$ for firms. On Reddit neither the volume of posts nor the engagement each earns steadies with size. Leaders are therefore displaced at similar rates at every scale: 16–21\% of each Reddit top set is replaced each year. Displacement at each rank follows a balance known from top wealth shares: communities crossing a rank's boundary return lasting variance over twice the local slope of the rank-size curve (pooled ratio 0.96; 0.89–1.05, four ladders). Models of jumps to random ranks fail this test. A generative model in which each community's lasting level random-walks, and the ranking is held by drift that depends on rank rather than by reversion to a fixed level, forecasts held-out weekly movement better than the previous model on Reddit submissions and as well on comments, and reproduces Facebook's absence of reversion. Unlike Facebook, Reddit shows momentum: a community's growth in one year predicts its growth in the next (rank correlation 0.32–0.37), which no model we tested reproduces. The 78 leading submissions communities' share fell from 50\% to 34\% in three years.
\end{abstract}

\PLAN{Intro par.~2, the theory that links them, and whose it is.}
{From the fact to the accounting: a top set's share changes by its members' drift (within) plus a displacement gain from crossings at its boundary, plus entry. Gomez (2023) derived and measured this for top wealth shares; stochastic portfolio theory gives the rank-based mathematics; Gabaix and GLLM give the stationary form and the speed question.}
{Gomez: displacement $\tfrac12(\zeta-1)\nu^2$ for arithmetic shares, predicted 2.0\% against measured 2.3\% a year on the Forbes 400 (theory note §9). Our shape index is the mean-log version: crossing gain $\sigma^2/(2S)$, $S = 1/\zeta$.}
{State plainly that the law, its cumulant extension and the stationary balance are Gomez's and SPT's (theory note §8--§9).}

\PLAN{Intro par.~3, what is not known.}
{From the precedent to the gap. The precedent tests one top set, in wealth, where growth has almost no transitory part (AR(1) about 0.01) and where conditioning on current members costs little. Attention has large passing swings, so selection on current rank is the main bias, and the ranking-dynamics literature models movement as rank exchange. Open: does displacement theory hold rank by rank across a whole ladder when most movement is passing; can rank exchange be excluded; and what moves the shape when a platform changes its design?}
{Iñiguez et al.\ 2022 (rank-exchange models); Blumm et al.\ 2012; Wu \& Huberman 2007.}
