\WT{500}
\PLAN{Intro par.~1, the fact. Attention is concentrated in a few communities, and the communities change places constantly.}
{From the reader's experience of platforms to the two speeds: names churn in years while the ladder's shape changes slowly. The fact is NOT new (rank clocks for cities and firms); what is open is how the speeds connect.}
{A fifth of each top set replaced a year, at about the same rate from the top 195 to the top 12,500 (§2z-cu); median annual change of log rank 0.19--0.24 (§2z-cu); arithmetic share of the top 78 submissions communities 50.2\% $\to$ 34.0\%, 2019--2022 (§2z-da).}
{Cite Batty 2006 and Duranton 2007 as having documented stable rank-size over churning membership; never claim the joint regularity is undocumented (§2z-da). Never say "stable form" or "stationary" (owner, 2026-10-18).}

\PLAN{Intro par.~2, the theory that links them, and whose it is.}
{From the fact to the accounting: a top set's share changes by its members' drift (within) plus a displacement gain from crossings at its boundary, plus entry. Gomez (2023) derived and measured this for top wealth shares; stochastic portfolio theory gives the rank-based mathematics; Gabaix and GLLM give the stationary form and the speed question.}
{Gomez: displacement $\tfrac12(\zeta-1)\nu^2$ for arithmetic shares, predicted 2.0\% against measured 2.3\% a year on the Forbes 400 (theory note §9). Our shape index is the mean-log version: crossing gain $\sigma^2/(2S)$, $S = 1/\zeta$.}
{State plainly that the law, its cumulant extension and the stationary balance are Gomez's and SPT's (theory note §8--§9).}

\PLAN{Intro par.~3, what is not known.}
{From the precedent to the gap. The precedent tests one top set, in wealth, where growth has almost no transitory part (AR(1) about 0.01) and where conditioning on current members costs little. Attention has large passing swings, so selection on current rank is the main bias, and the ranking-dynamics literature models movement as rank exchange. Open: does displacement theory hold rank by rank across a whole ladder when most movement is passing; can rank exchange be excluded; and what moves the shape when a platform changes its design?}
{Iñiguez et al.\ 2022 (rank-exchange models); Blumm et al.\ 2012; Wu \& Huberman 2007.}
{}

\PLAN{Intro par.~4, findings preview (numbered).}
{One sentence each, in the order of Results.}
{(1) Exact balance at every rank (§2z-cr). (2) With permanent-rank cohorts and spacing differences, crossings match lasting variance over twice the slope across ranks on four ladders, pooled 0.96 (0.89--1.05) (§2z-cm, §2z-ct, §2z-cx, §2z-da). (3) Rank exchange fails; movement is level growth with lasting volatility nearly independent of size, $\beta$ 0--0.09 (§2z-cv, §2z-da). (4) Shape changes through drift by rank; concentration relaxes toward what movement sustains over years (§2z-cp, §2z-cw). (5) Around Reddit's 2021 feed change attention moved one tier down and toward comments (§2z-da).}
{In sample, development data; no forecasting claim.}

\subsection*{Measuring lasting movement}
\WT{450}
\PLAN{R3. The method contribution: why attention needs it and what it does.}
{Passing swings dominate weekly movement; ranking by current position selects on them. Cohorts fixed by permanent rank before the window; the difference of two spacings removes one-time constants; the estimand includes a composition term.}
{Current-rank and cohort slides agree once one-time constants are removed, 7 of 7 cut-offs on three ladders, 6 of 7 on Instagram (§2z-cm, §2z-ct, §2z-cx); $R_{ac}-R_{ab} = R_{bc} + C$ exact, $C$ +25--35\% on Reddit, $-30$\% on Facebook (§2z-da).
