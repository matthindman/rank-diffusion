\section{Data}
\paragraph{Ladders.} Three weekly ladders of named endpoints: Reddit comment karma and submission karma by community (December 2018--December 2022, 212--213 weeks; daily negative karma set to zero before weekly sums) from the Pushshift archive, and Facebook page interactions from CrowdTangle (November 2020--June 2022, 86 weeks, the stable instrument era; days flagged by the day guard excluded). Universes: top $K$ = 12,500 (comments), 5,000 (submissions), 3,500 (pages), with membership penalised for absence; endpoints ever ranked inside $4K$ in a week are tracked so that every weekly top-$k$ set with $k \le K$ is complete. Sets ranked by block or annual totals are guaranteed complete only for endpoints that entered a weekly top $4K$: a missed endpoint would need a total inside the top $k \le K$ while never reaching weekly rank $4K$, whose level is about 7--16 times lower than that of rank $K$ at the slopes measured near $K$. This is not checked. Log activity $x=\log(1+a)$.
\paragraph{Terms of use.} Reddit data come from the Pushshift archive; the archive and the community-level panels built from it are not redistributed, while analyses may name communities. CrowdTangle's terms for researchers allowed insights from its data to be published but not the raw data, and allowed the IDs of the Pages studied to be posted so that others could rebuild the list; we follow that line, as careful CrowdTangle studies did (no per-Page series are released). Details and sources are in the repository (\url{llm_fitting/runs/2026-10-18_pnas_push/DATA_AVAILABILITY_NOTES.md}).
\paragraph{The archive's capture.} The archive's monthly files were collected with delays that differ by file, from days to a year; the delay rose from about 20 to about 360 days at the file boundary of 1 July 2021 (MODEL\_STATUS \S2z-bz). A capture artefact would therefore show as a step at the first of a month. From the daily panels (each block's head, the top $K/64$, against its top $K$), the step in the mean log share over the 14 days after a date against the 14 days before is no larger at the 44 first-of-month boundaries than at the 43 mid-month placebo dates, for the head's share of items, its share of karma and its karma per item on both ladders (mean absolute steps 0.011--0.031 against 0.012--0.041). The 1 July 2021 step is ordinary ($-0.034$ in the submissions head's item share, at the 20th percentile of boundary steps), and the boundary steps sum to $+0.09$ against a within-year change of $-0.93$: none of the head's decline sits at file boundaries. The largest steps are dated events (mid-February and mid-May 2021, February 2022). The audit can detect capture: the head's item share falls by about 1\% within each file and recovers at the next (median step $-0.024$ at mid-month against $0.000$ at boundaries on submissions; $-0.010$ against $+0.004$ on comments; post hoc), as removals that accumulate with the time to collection would make it. Excluding communities whose activity ends in a collapse (152 on submissions, none in its 2019 head; 2,259 on comments, including r/Cringetopia and r/MGTOW) changes the submissions head's fall in share from 2019 to 2022 by less than 0.001 and persistence by less than 0.001. A removal that grows smoothly with calendar time across files is not tested; that needs the raw files' collection times. One short-delay week (27 April 2020) lowers the submissions head's karma share by 0.17 in log for that week. (RESULTS R16-E; expectations R16-E1--E5 met.)

\section{Blocks, cohorts and permanent rank}
Blocks are non-overlapping and aligned to each panel's end (52 weeks; 26 for the boundary test; 13 or 26 on Facebook). A block level requires presence in 90\% of the block's weeks. Permanent rank is a community's mean weekly rank over a fixed block, absent weeks counted at that week's floor. Cohorts are fixed on a block disjoint from the blocks they are measured on. The reason is selection: a community observed high this week is high partly by passing chance, and conditioning on current rank selects on that noise. With weekly passing swings of the size measured here this bias is first-order.

\section{Turnover from churn and slope}\label{sec:turnover}
\subsection{Derivation}
Let the ladder be locally linear in log rank at rank $k$, $x(r) = x_k - S_k \log(r/k)$, so the number of names above level $x_k+u$ is $k e^{-u/S_k}$ and their density at height $u$ above the boundary is $(k/S_k)e^{-u/S_k}$. Over an interval the boundary moves by $\Delta\theta_k$ and a name at height $u$ by $\Delta_i$; it leaves the top $k$ when $\Delta_i < \Delta\theta_k - u$. If $\Delta_i$ does not depend on $u$ within the reach of a year's moves,
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathbb{E}[\#\text{leaving}]}{k} = \frac{1}{S_k}\int_0^\infty e^{-u/S_k}\,\Pr(\Delta < \Delta\theta_k - u)\,du = \mathbb{E}\!\left[1-e^{-D^+/S_k}\right],\quad D = \Delta\theta_k - \Delta .
\end{equation}
Names of the top $k$ that vanish from the universe (share $v_k$) leave with certainty, giving
\begin{equation}
f_k \;=\; v_k + (1-v_k)\,\mathbb{E}\!\left[1-e^{-D^+/S_k}\right].
\label{eq:S_turnover}
\end{equation}
For small moves $f_k \approx \mathbb{E}[D^+]/S_k$. The typical downward move at the boundary is nearly constant across scale (0.21--0.40 log units), while the slope rises from 0.7--1.0 at the head to 1.2--2.3 lower down, so the top sets turn over fastest.
\subsection{Registered and post hoc versions}
The registered expectation used the small-move form with constant density, $v_k + (1-v_k)\mathbb{E}[D^+]/S_k$; it over-predicted on every ladder (median ratio 1.30, 1.27, 1.45) and failed its expectation (within $[0.8,1.25]$ in 11\%, 39\%, 25\% of cells). The exact form above was derived after that failure. It holds in all 48 cells (ratios 0.82--1.18, median 0.98 on each ladder; rmse 0.018, 0.009, 0.024). Inputs: $S_k$ from a regression of level on log rank over ranks $k/\sqrt2$--$k\sqrt2$ at the start block; $\Delta_i$ for names ranked $k/2$--$k$ present in both blocks.

\subsection{Turnover profiles and geometry}
Studies of cultural evolution summarize how the number of new entries $z$ into a top-$y$ list grows with $y$ as $z = a y^b$, and read departures of $b$ from its neutral value as evidence of biased copying \cite{bentley2007,acerbi2014,evans2011}. With $z = f_y\, y$, the data give $b$ = 0.95 (submissions, $y$ = 78--2,496) and 0.92 (comments, 195--6,240). Under identical calibrated dynamics, the data's ladder gives $b$ = 0.88 (size-blind) and 0.91 (firm-like), and a ladder of constant slope 0.99 and 1.04 (20 runs each, $y$ = 78--2,500). The ladder alone shifts $b$ by 0.11--0.13, so an inference about copying from a turnover profile needs a control for the ladder's slope (post hoc, PLAN.md addendum R13; R13-3 passed).

\section{The balance at every rank}\label{sec:boundary}
\subsection{The rate law}
Names move by Itô processes with local drift $\mu$ and variance $\sigma^2$; the density of levels obeys the forward equation, and integrating over the top $k$ gives $dU_k/dt = \overline{\mu}_{\text{top }k} + \sigma^2(x_k)/(2S_k)$. For the shape $U_k-U_K$ this gives Eq.~2 of the main text. The stationary form is $S_k = \sigma_k^2/(2(\bar s_k - e_k))$, Fernholz's gap identity in continuum notation and, with one drift, the Atlas slope $\sigma^2/(2g)$ \cite{fernholz2002,fernholz2017,fernholzfernholz2020}; the arithmetic-share analogue is Gomez's displacement term, $\tfrac12\nu^2(a_k/\bar a_{\rm top})/S_k$ in general and $\tfrac12(\zeta-1)\nu^2$ on a Pareto tail with index $\zeta = 1/S > 1$ \cite{gomez2023}. Because $c_k = \sigma_k^2/(2S_k)$, the ratio of crossing gain to net decline is $c_k/(\bar s_k - e_k) = S^\ast_k/S_k$: crossings fall short of the net decline exactly when the ladder is steeper than its own stationary steepness.
\subsection{Estimators}
\emph{Lasting variance}: for a cohort and three blocks $a<b<c$, $[\mathrm{var}(x_c-x_a) - \tfrac12(\mathrm{var}(x_b-x_a)+\mathrm{var}(x_c-x_b))]/(b-a)$, which cancels passing swings and the block-averaging offset; a variogram estimator agrees within about 10\%. \emph{Crossings}: the reordering gain of the top $k$ (mean of the top $k$ at the end minus the end-mean of the initial members), differenced between spacings of two and four half-years; the difference removes passing swings and equals the lasting crossing gain plus a composition term $C$, which is $+25$ to $+35$\% of the statistic on Reddit and $-30$\% on Facebook. In simulated homogeneous growth $C$ is negative, and with fixed differences in growth it has Reddit's size (Section~\ref{sec:hetero}). \emph{Slide}: the mean change of a cohort's members above $k$ against the mean of the universe.

\subsection{Time scales}\label{sec:timescales}
Three time scales follow from the measured lasting variance $\sigma_k^2$, slope $S_k$ and balanced slope $S^\ast_k$. (i) Lasting movement crosses one e-fold of rank in $S_k^2/\sigma_k^2$; a member drawn uniformly from the ranks of a top set lies on average one e-fold above its boundary ($\int_0^1 -\ln u\,du = 1$), so this is a characteristic diffusion time for a member's lasting movement to reach the boundary (not an expected hitting time). (ii) For a ladder of uniform slope between $k$ and $K$, linearized about $S^\ast$, the shape relaxes toward its balance with time constant $\tau_k = 2S^{\ast2}_k\ln(K/k)/\sigma_k^2$. (iii) In the reflecting-barrier model of random growth, the cross-section converges at rate $\lambda = \sigma^2/(8S^2)$ \cite{gllm2016}; at balance, with $S = S^\ast$ and $\ln(K/k)\approx 4$, (ii) and (iii) nearly coincide, and the ratio of the shape's time scale to the movement time is about $8(S^\ast/S)^2$.

These formulas apply to a single slope and a homogeneous drift; the ladders are curved, the drift is by rank, and exits are omitted. Exits shorten the convergence time of (iii), little at the head, where they are rare. In rank-based models without entry and exit the mean-field equation need not have a spectral gap at all \cite{bakersmyth2026}. The numbers are therefore orders of magnitude, and model-implied: they describe how the measured ingredients act if held fixed, not forecasts.

\section{The generative model}
\subsection{Specification}
Observed log activity $x_i = L_i + m_i + \varepsilon_i$ with $\varepsilon$ observation noise and $m$ an AR(1) passing swing with $t$-tails. In the implementation (\texttt{rankdiff\_rdm.py}, \texttt{lasting="movinghome"}) the slow component follows the earlier permanent--transitory model's mean reversion toward a home $H_i$ that itself random-walks: $L_{t+1} = L_t - \kappa(L_t-H_t) + \epsilon_t$, $H_{t+1} = H_t + g(r(H_t)) + \eta_t$, $\mathrm{Var}\,\eta = \sigma_L^2 = \sigma_0^2 e^{-2\beta(H-L_{\rm ref})}$. The main text's lasting level is $H$; the reverting part of $L$ is the medium swing. Drift by rank $g$ is either the measured slide profile or the fixed point that holds the end-of-training ladder.
\subsection{The variance error and its correction}
With $\mathrm{Var}\,\epsilon = q$, the one-step variance of $L$ is $[2q+\kappa\sigma_L^2]/(2-\kappa)$. The frozen form set $q = \sigma_p^2-\sigma_L^2$, which is the earlier model's one-step variance minus $\sigma_L^2$; preserving it requires $q=\sigma_p^2-\kappa\sigma_L^2/2$ (\texttt{lasting="movinghome\_vp"}). A known-answer test confirms both statements.

\section{Persistence}
\subsection{The averaging null}
For a random walk with increment variance $v$ averaged over blocks of $B$ steps, consecutive block-mean changes have variance $v(2B^2+1)/(3B)$ and covariance $v(B^2-1)/(6B)$, so correlation $(B^2-1)/(2(2B^2+1)) \to 1/4$ ($0.2499$ at $B=52$; Spearman $0.239$) \cite{working1960}.
\subsection{The gapped statistic}
Changes between windows of $w$ weeks $h$ weeks apart, the second change starting $g$ weeks after the first ends; any independent-increment process plus white noise gives zero; a short-memory swing gives a small negative value. Synthetic check (5 seeds): random walk + noise + OU, $-0.032$, $-0.009$, $-0.005$ at gaps 0, 13, 26; with drift by community (sd 0.4 a year), $+0.17$ to $+0.19$.

\section{Model atlas: every simulated model and how each fails}\label{sec:null}
\paragraph{Selection rule.} The models represent the standard explanations of a stationary hierarchy with churning members: random growth with a size-blind and with a firm-like volatility law (Gibrat; the firm-growth literature), neutral copying (cultural evolution), and the generative model built here, plus, from round 6, three standard forms of heterogeneous growth (Section~\ref{sec:hetero}). Each growth law is run from a ladder of constant slope and from the data's 2019 ladder, so that geometry and dynamics can be separated. Two sets of worlds start from a constant slope: stylized worlds started from a Zipf ladder (``Zipf ladder'' below), and the calibrated dynamics with the data's top 5,000 reset to a constant slope (R9; Table~1 of the main text uses these). Every model is reported in full on every statistic, including those it fails; none is dropped after scoring. Models that were fitted and replaced during development (earlier stacks, an ablation ladder, drift by name, life-cycle drift, jumps to random ranks) are listed in the repository's gate ledger and record; jumps to random ranks enter the boundary comparison (1.56).

\paragraph{What is automatic.} Eq.~1, a positive slide, a boundary ratio near one for any diffusion of levels, and crossings short of the net decline in any world that starts steeper than its own balance (since $c_k/(\bar s_k - e_k) = S^\ast_k/S_k$). The boundary ratio by cut-off is structured even with known truth (Zipf-ladder size-blind 1.02--1.26, firm-like 0.95--1.37), and neutral copying's pooled 0.69 comes from the edge of the universe (0.22 at $K$; 0.64--1.24 between $K/64$ and $K/2$), so the ratio robustly excludes only jumps to random ranks (1.56). Which top sets turn over fastest is set by the ladder: from the data's ladder, size-blind and firm-like volatility give the same profile within noise; from a Zipf ladder, size-blind growth turns every set over at one rate and firm-like growth protects the top.

\paragraph{What is not.} Persistence (every world $-0.02$ to $-0.10$, the data $+0.14$ to $+0.20$). The weak size-dependence of lasting volatility is measured, not automatic, but the raw exponent cannot show it: passing swings that differ by rank give a planted 0 a raw 0.06 and a planted 0.2 a raw 0.09. The model's structure-function estimator recovers a planted exponent: on panels of 160 weeks calibrated to Facebook, a planted 0.113 is recovered with median error $+0.006$ (sd 0.029) and a planted 0 with $+0.015$ (0.029); at 86 weeks the null is biased by $+0.045$ (0.041) (run record \url{runs/2026-10-18_rdm/recovery_output.txt}).

\section{Heterogeneous growth}\label{sec:hetero}
\paragraph{Specification.} Three standard forms add a community-specific term $a_{it}$ to the lasting level of the calibrated model, $\Delta H_{it} = g(r_{it}) + a_{it} + \sigma_0 e^{-\beta(H_{it}-H_0)}\eta_{it}$: (a) a fixed Gaussian effect $b_i \sim N(0,\sigma_b^2)$, drawn once per community, as in heterogeneous income profiles and second-order rank models \cite{guvenen2009,guvenen2021,fik2013}; (b) an autoregressive growth rate, $u_{i,t+1} = \rho u_{it} + \sigma_u\sqrt{1-\rho^2}\,\xi_{it}$, as with Ornstein--Uhlenbeck fitness in ecology and autoregressive growth components of firms \cite{mallmin2026,sterk2021,gllm2016}; and (c) exposure to the platform's weekly total, $(\lambda_i - \bar\lambda_K) f_t$, with $f_t$ the observed weekly change of mean log activity over the tracked universe, leaving out the community itself, and $\lambda_i$ each community's loading on it, estimated on the training weeks, with the spread of loadings corrected for estimation noise, as with factor loadings in finance \cite{carhart1997,ichiba2011}. A world driven by the observed $f_t$ is a conditional historical world, not a forecast of platform activity. The drift by rank is re-solved with the heterogeneity present, so that it does not count the composition twice.
\paragraph{Calibration.} Under fixed heterogeneity, weekly growth autocovariances beyond the passing memory equal $\sigma_b^2$, about $4\times10^{-5}$ a week at an annual spread of 0.3, against a sampling error of order $10^{-4}$ in these panels: the low-power problem of the income literature \cite{guvenen2009,hryshko2012}. Long differences carry the same component as $(52\sigma_b)^2$ against a much smaller passing variance, so forms (a) and (b) are calibrated by indirect inference \cite{gourieroux1993,guvenen2014} on two auxiliary statistics computed by the same code on data and simulated panels: the covariance, within three strata of permanent rank, of changes between 13-week window means a year apart that share no weeks (at gaps of 0 and 13 weeks for (a); 0, 13, 26 and 39 for (b)); and the two-spacing lasting variance $[V(52)-V(26)]/26$ by stratum (a simpler statistic than the model's structure-function estimator, which it does not reproduce exactly; PLAN.md amendment A4), with a multiplier on $\sigma_0$ so that the world's measured lasting variance matches the data's under heterogeneity. Each statistic is scaled by its bootstrap standard deviation (communities resampled within strata, 40 draws); grids of annual spread $\{0, 0.1, \ldots, 0.5\}$ by multiplier $\{0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 1.0\}$ for (a), and half-life $\{26, 52, 104\}$ weeks by spread $\{0.2, \ldots, 0.5\}$ for (b), 3 runs a point. Fixed effects are clipped at $\pm 2.5\sigma_b$ and run no longer than the panel, since fixed Gaussian drift can violate the stability condition of rank-based models \cite{fik2013}. In (a) and (b) the growth types are drawn independently of size and with one spread at every rank. Form (c) has no free parameter: its loadings come from 2019 co-movement, shrunk by empirical Bayes to their stratum means, and its drift is solved with the factor's weekly values shuffled, so that the drift does not absorb the observed path's trend. For (a) and (b) the gapped covariances at the calibration gaps are fitted, not tested. Their tests are the lag profile beyond those gaps, the asymmetry between risers and fallers (which, after rank drift, exits and selection, every simulated form gives reversed), the prior-decliner contrast, the out-of-period head share, turnover, retention, the boundary ratio and recovery. For (c) every statistic is untargeted. Expectations and the amendments that set this design were written before any run (PLAN.md addendum R12, amendments A1--A4).
\paragraph{Recovery.} Under heterogeneous growth the variance of $h$-week changes grows as $qh + Bh^2$, so the two-spacing estimator of lasting variance reads $q + 3Bh$ and the size exponent is attenuated \cite{guvenen2009,hryshko2012}. The bias can be large. In a synthetic panel with lasting variance 0.004 a week, passing parts like the data's and a fixed annual spread of growth of 0.3, the two-spacing estimate is 0.0071, not 0.0040; without the spread it is 0.0042, and the covariance of year-apart window changes recovers the planted $(52\sigma_b)^2$ at gaps of 13--52 weeks (\url{runs/2026-10-18_pnas_push/r12_synthetic_check.py}). How much of the measured lasting variance, and so of the crossing gain and the time scales, is heterogeneity therefore depends on the spread the data support. The estimators are rerun on panels simulated from the fixed form at planted spreads of 0, 0.15, 0.30 and 0.45, with known truth (20 panels each); the decaying form and the factor form were not recovered.
\paragraph{Calibration results.} On both ladders the objective falls steadily as the spread grows, for both forms, and the longest half-life is preferred. The optima are at the edge of the registered grids: an annual spread of 0.5 for fixed differences (multiplier 1.0 on submissions, 0.7 on comments) and a spread of 0.5 with a half-life of two years for decaying ones. A post hoc extension of the fixed grid to spreads of 0.6--1.0 (multipliers 0.8--1.1) gives 362 at 0.6 against 351 at 0.5 on submissions, a difference within the noise of three runs a point, and 1,437 against 1,013 on comments. The extension omitted the comments optimum's multiplier, 0.7, so it does not establish a minimum there. The fit is poor: 6 standard deviations per moment on submissions and 11 on comments. With diagonal weights the objective is not $\chi^2$-distributed, so its size was calibrated by simulation (RESULTS R16-C; expectations committed before the run). Twenty pseudo-data panels from the fixed world at its own optimum, each calibrated by the same grid, give a median minimum objective of 6 (95th percentile 15) on submissions and 13 (29) on comments; the data's 351 and 1,013 are 12 to 35 times those percentiles, so the fixed form is rejected decisively on both ladders. The same exercise answers the two registered runs of amendment A1. A homogeneous world whose passing swing has a half-life of 26 weeks is fitted with a spread of 0 (or 0.1 in 4 of 20 on comments), so a medium-term swing does not masquerade as heterogeneity. But a world with decaying differences (spread 0.5, half-life two years) is fitted as fixed differences of 0.4--0.5 with an objective near the correct-specification range (medians 14 and 19): the calibration cannot tell the two forms apart, and only the untargeted tests can. The two kinds of moment pull apart. The covariances alone choose the largest spread, while the lasting variance alone chooses none. The data's persistence grows down the ladder (covariance at gap 0: $-0.001$, $+0.024$ and $+0.067$ in the three strata of submissions). A spread that is the same at every rank gives the head too much persistence and the body too little ($+0.013$, $+0.001$, $+0.017$ at the optimum). The algebra says a spread of 0.5 adds about $3(0.5/52)^2\cdot 26 \approx 0.007$ a week to the two-spacing lasting variance. In the calibrated worlds it adds 0.0014--0.0031, because the statistics see only part of the planted spread (below).

\paragraph{Recovery results.} The model's estimators were rerun on 20 panels simulated from the calibrated model with fixed differences planted at annual spreads of 0, 0.15, 0.30 and 0.45. Each planted world is compared with the one with nothing planted, on the same seeds. On submissions, at 0.45, the lasting variance at the head is overstated by 21\% and $\sigma_0$ by 26\%, and the size exponent is raised by 0.023. The inflation is larger lower down the ladder, so heterogeneity raises the estimated exponent rather than attenuating it. With nothing planted, this recovery's estimator (the moving-home variant with oracle passing parts and no slides) returns 0.063 for a true 0.045 on submissions and 0.069 for a true 0.126 on comments. The estimator applied to the data, run in full with each panel's own passing parts, is nearly unbiased on comments (Section~\ref{sec:sizeprotect}; RESULTS R16-D), so the earlier caution that the comment exponents are understated is withdrawn. \paragraph{Applications for platform research.} Five rest on results in hand and are in the main text: a benchmark for claims of entrenchment, a selection baseline for studies of top lists (the members of a set chosen on one year's activity lose about 5\% of their share the next year from selection alone, R7), turnover read against the ladder's slope (Eq.~\ref{eq:S_turnover}; R9), an accounting of a change in concentration, and the model's conditional response of concentration to a change in the leaders' decline, at the measured parameters. Four more are here. \emph{Lock-in a year or two ahead.} Forecasts of whether each member of a top set is still in it, from measured movement alone, discriminate who stays on four ladders (Brier score 13--21\% below constant turnover) but under-forecast staying by 2--5 points at the first horizon and 5--17 points later, and a logistic on log rank and horizon fitted to past outcomes scores better on every ladder, by 0.0006--0.0048 (MODEL\_STATUS \S\S2z-cy, 2z-cz, 2z-da). Rank alone therefore predicts who stays as well as movement does; the forecasts' strata are by current rank, a declared departure from the permanent-rank rule. \emph{What data a study needs.} Crossings require levels below the cut-off (here tracked to $4K$); in a small recovery study calibrated to Facebook (six replications) the size exponent's estimator showed little bias at 160 weeks and $+0.045$ at 86 in a small recovery calibrated to Facebook, is nearly unbiased on comments, and cannot separate 0 from 0.045 on submissions, so its accuracy depends on the panel; persistence needs annual horizons. \emph{Passing against lasting position} and \emph{detecting platform interventions} are possible with the model's filter and with placebo-year event designs; in these data the June 2021 fall of the submissions head is not distinctive against placebo years (MODEL\_STATUS \S2z-da). Comparing platforms in dimensionless coordinates, measuring from top-$K$ lists alone, and registered projections of the ladder's shape are future work; the homogeneous model's out-of-period projection of the head's share failed (main text, Fig.~4\textit{A}).

\section{Relation to the earlier analysis of web traffic}
This paper continues chapter 5 of ref.~\cite{hindman2018}, written with B. Rogers. That chapter posed the question asked here: why web traffic has a stable concentrated structure while individual sites are highly unstable. It answered that churn itself produces and maintains the power law. It modelled daily traffic of the top 300 sites of 2005--2008 with stochastic models from finance \cite{fernholz2002}, and simulated them from several starting conditions, including a flat one \todo{page references pp.~89--99 and n.~15 to be checked against the edition cited}.

This paper keeps the question and the class of model and changes what is measured:
\begin{itemize}
\item the balance between crossings and decline is measured rank by rank, with a tested identity for crossings (Eq.~1);
\item lasting movement is separated from passing movement, by cohorts fixed on permanent rank;
\item entry and exit enter the accounting;
\item every statistic is computed with the same code in the data and in simulated worlds, including a flat start (Results 3);
\item persistence of change, which homogeneous models cannot produce, is measured;
\item the communities are named.
\end{itemize}

Two findings of the chapter do not carry over to Reddit. On the web the top ten rarely changed; on Reddit a quarter to a third of the top ten is replaced each year, because the ladder is flattest at its head (Results 2). The chapter also read a steep fall of volatility with size from a widening scatter of changes against rank, which it called the funnel of churn. Much of that widening is a matter of sample size. Lower strata hold many more sites, so their extremes are larger even when the typical change is not. On Reddit the median, 90th and 95th percentiles of the one-year change rise only 1.2- to 1.6-fold across a 64-fold range of rank, while the maxima triple (Section~\ref{sec:sizeprotect}). \todo{Owner: the same quantile plot for the 2005--2008 web data, as described in the owner's note of 2026-10-20.} On Reddit, what looks like protection at the top is the geometry of the ladder together with a weak dependence of volatility on size. Whether the same holds for the web data of 2005--2008 is not tested here.

\section{Rank change and set replacement}
Iñiguez et al.'s rank change $C(R)$ is the probability that the element at rank $R$ differs between consecutive observations. Weekly $C(R)$ on the data: Reddit submissions 0.07, 0.36, 0.79, 0.99 at $R$ = 1, 3, 10, 100; comments 0.03, 0.46, 0.87, 0.99; Facebook 0.52, 0.74, 0.94, 1.00. The top is stable in rank order on both Reddit ladders, not on Facebook, while the top set's membership turns over fastest; both follow from a ladder whose neighbour gaps are about $S/R$ and whose slope $S$ is smallest at the head.
